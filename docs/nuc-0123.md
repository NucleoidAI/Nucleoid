## Abstract

- Lists and class instance lists gain two read-only queries, `variance`
  and `stdev`. `RECEIVER.variance()` returns the population variance of the
  receiver's items, the mean of their squared deviations from their mean,
  and `RECEIVER.variance(selector)` that of `selector(item)` for every item.
  `RECEIVER.stdev()` and `RECEIVER.stdev(selector)` return its square root,
  the population standard deviation.
- Both divide by the number of values, as NumPy's `var` and `std` and
  Python's `statistics.pvariance` and `statistics.pstdev` do, and as a case
  of [NUC 80](nuc-0080.md) does when it writes the variance out from two
  averages. A list or a class population holds every value it describes,
  so it is the whole population, and a single value has variance `0`.
- The values are read in two passes, in 64-bit floating point. The mean is
  computed as `average` computes it, and the variance is the mean of the
  squared deviations `(value - mean) * (value - mean)`; the standard
  deviation is the correctly rounded square root of the variance. For a
  list of numbers, `xs.variance()` is therefore exactly
  `xs.average(x -> (x - xs.average()) * (x - xs.average()))` and
  `xs.stdev()` exactly `Math.sqrt` of it, and large values with a small
  spread keep their spread: `[1000000001, 1000000002, 1000000003].variance()`
  is `0.6666666666666666`, where the mean of the squares minus the square of
  the mean gives `0`.
- An empty receiver gives NaN. A value that converts to NaN, and an
  infinite value, make both results NaN. An undefined value makes them
  undefined, which a declaration stores as `null`.
- They follow [NUC 84](nuc-0084.md)'s `median`, beside
  [NUC 68](nuc-0068.md)'s `sum` and [NUC 80](nuc-0080.md)'s `average`:
  the same receivers, numeric conversion, selector rules, NaN and undefined
  results, and liveness under [NUC 61](nuc-0061.md)'s shared query rules. A
  supplied selector that is not a function raises
  `TypeError("variance expects a function")` or
  `TypeError("stdev expects a function")`.
- At the accepted revision these calls are run-time errors,
  `TypeError("<receiver>.variance is not a function")` and
  `TypeError("<receiver>.stdev is not a function")`. Only inputs that were
  errors change. No syntax, keyword, token or error type is added, and
  bindings, properties and methods named `variance` or `stdev` keep their
  accepted results.
- The authoritative contract is
  [List and population variances and standard deviations](../nucleoid.spec.md#list-and-population-variances-and-standard-deviations).

## Motivation

- World models summarize their populations. Lists and class populations
  already have their sums, extremes, averages, medians and modes, but not
  their spread. A model that asks how far readings, prices or scores
  scatter must write the variance out from two averages, as a case of
  NUC 80 does with
  `team.scores.average(score -> score * score) - team.mean() ** 2`, and a
  standard deviation wraps that in `Math.sqrt`.
- The written-out formula repeats the receiver and the selector, is easy
  to get wrong, and loses precision when large values spread little,
  because subtracting two close averages cancels their leading digits.
  `variance()` and `stdev()` name the statistic once, as `average()` and
  `median()` do, and can compute it from the deviations themselves.
- The cancellation is not rare. At the accepted revision the mean of the
  squares minus the square of the mean gives `0` for
  `[1000000001, 1000000002, 1000000003]`, whose variance is
  `0.6666666666666666`; `8.666666666666664` for the levels `3`, `5` and
  `10`, whose variance is `26 / 3`, `8.666666666666666`; and
  `7.111111111111107` for `[2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9, 11]`, whose variance is
  `7.111111111111111`.
- The accepted spellings are long. The shortest,
  `spread = scores.average(** 2) - scores.average() ** 2`, is 17 lexer
  tokens and cancels; the shortest that keeps the precision,
  `spread = scores.average(x -> (x - scores.average()) ** 2)`, is 20, where
  `spread = scores.variance()` is 7. Over a population,
  `spread = Reading.average(x -> (x.level - Reading.average(.level)) ** 2)`
  is 24 where `spread = Reading.variance(reading -> reading.level)` is 12.
  In a class-level rule over a filtered population the accepted spelling
  states the filter twice:
  `$Room.spread = Sensor.filter(sensor -> sensor.room == $Room).stdev(sensor -> sensor.temp)`
  is 25 tokens where its two-pass spelling is 53.
- Python's `statistics.pvariance` and `statistics.pstdev`, NumPy's `var`
  and `std`, Excel's `VAR.P` and `STDEV.P` and SQL's `VAR_POP` and
  `STDDEV_POP` name the operation. JavaScript has none, and LLM-written code
  such as `readings.stdev()` raises
  `TypeError("readings.stdev is not a function")` at the accepted revision.

  standard deviation, and a case of NUC 80 already writes the population

- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Two more aggregates.** `variance` and `stdev` join the aggregate arm of
  `Runtime::list_query` beside `median`, so lists however they are reached
  and class instance lists accept them through the route and the receiver,
  argument and liveness rules they already share. Rejected: list-only
  methods, which would leave `Reading.variance(...)` raising where
  `Reading.median(...)` runs.
- **The population statistics.** Both divide by the number of values `n`. A
  list or a class population holds every value it describes, so its spread
  is a population's, and a single value has variance `0`.
- Rejected: the sample statistics, which divide by `n - 1`, as R's `var`
  and `sd`, pandas' defaults, Excel's `VAR.S` and `STDEV.S`, SQL's
  `VAR_SAMP` and `STDDEV_SAMP` and Python's `statistics.variance` and
  `statistics.stdev` do. They estimate the spread of a larger population
  that the values are drawn from, which a list or a class does not name,
  and they have no value for one item. Also rejected: a number argument
  choosing the divisor, as NumPy's `ddof`; `xs.variance(1)` stays an error
  rather than a silent sample statistic.
- **The names.** `variance` is the statistic's name, and `stdev` is the
  abbreviation of Python's `statistics` module and of spreadsheets' `STDEV`.
  Python's `statistics.variance` and `statistics.stdev` are its sample
  statistics, so the Specification states the divisor where the name alone
  might suggest them. Rejected: `std`, `var`, `pvariance`, `pstdev`,
  `stddev` and `standardDeviation` as names or aliases; `xs.std()` keeps
  raising `TypeError("xs.std is not a function")`.
- **Two passes.** The mean comes first, computed as `average` computes it:
  the values added in receiver order from `0`, divided by their number. The
  variance then adds the squares of the deviations from that mean in
  receiver order from `0` and divides by the number of values. The
  deviations are small where the values are close, so their squares keep
  the digits that the one-pass formula cancels, and a list of numbers gets
  exactly the variance of its accepted two-pass spelling
  `xs.average(x -> (x - xs.average()) * (x - xs.average()))`.
- Rejected: the one-pass formula `average(x * x) - average() ** 2`, which
  cancels as [Motivation](#motivation) shows; Welford's running update and
  compensated or corrected sums, which round differently from `average`
  and from every accepted spelling, so no accepted program reproduces them;
  and scaling the deviations before squaring them, as `hypot` does, which
  would also round differently.
- **The square is `d * d`.** It rounds once, as the accepted spelling's
  `(x - m) * (x - m)` does. `**` computes a power with the platform's
  `pow`, which on the accepted release build used here differs from
  `d * d` in the last bit for 3 of 4,000 random deviations.
- **The standard deviation is the square root of the variance,** the
  correctly rounded square root that `Math.sqrt` gives. Rejected:
  `variance ** 0.5`, which on the same build differs from `Math.sqrt` in the
  last bit for 8 of 6,000 random values.
- **A single value gives `0`, an empty receiver NaN.** One value deviates
  `0` from its own mean. An empty receiver gives `0 / 0`, the NaN that
  `average` gives, so the result stays a number, a declaration over a
  population that starts empty has a value rather than failing, and
  `Number.isNaN` tells it apart from `null`. Rejected: raising an error, as
  Python's `statistics.pvariance([])` does, which would fail a live
  declaration over an empty class, and `null`.
- **NaN and infinities.** A value that converts to NaN makes both results
  NaN. An infinite value makes them NaN too, because the mean is then
  infinite or NaN and the value's deviation from it, `Infinity - Infinity`,
  is NaN; the accepted spelling gives the same. Rejected: skipping NaN or
  infinite values, which would silently change which values the statistic
  is over.
- **Overflow and underflow follow 64-bit floating point.** A square or a
  total beyond the largest number overflows to `Infinity`, so
  `[1e200, -1e200].stdev()` is `Infinity` although the deviation `1e200` is
  representable, and `[1e308, 1e308].variance()` is `Infinity` because the
  total of the values already overflows; a square below the smallest
  positive number underflows to `0`. These are the results of the accepted
  spelling.
- **Undefined stays unknown.** An undefined item or selector result makes
  both results undefined, which a declaration stores as `null`, as it makes
  `sum`, `average` and `median` undefined, so a declaration waits for every
  value. Every item is still visited, so every item's reads are
  dependencies. An undefined value wins over a NaN value, whatever their
  order, as in `average` and `median`.
- **The conversion of `sum`.** Each value is converted once with the
  numeric conversion of `-`, `*`, the Math helpers, `sum`, `average` and
  `median`: `null`, `false`, empty strings and empty lists are `0`, `true` is
  `1`, numeric strings and one-item lists are their number, a date is its
  time in milliseconds, and objects, other strings and longer lists are
  NaN.
- **No artificial null read.** The queries read items, not bindings, so a
  `null` item counts `0` without settling a declaration to `null`, as in
  `sum` and `median`: `p = [2, null].variance()` stores `1`. The accepted
  spelling reads each item through its callback's parameter, so
  `p = xs.average(x -> (x - xs.average()) * (x - xs.average()))` with
  `xs = [2, null]` stores `null`. A selector's own reads follow the existing
  rules.
- **The selector is called like `median`'s.** It runs once per item, in
  order, with only the item; further parameters stay `null`. The accepted
  spelling with a selector `f`,
  `xs.average(x -> (f(x) - xs.average(f)) * (f(x) - xs.average(f)))`, calls
  it `2n + 2n * n` times for `n` items, so the queries and their accepted
  spelling differ for a selector with side effects.
- **A supplied selector must be a function.** Any other first argument,
  including `null`, raises `TypeError("variance expects a function")` or
  `TypeError("stdev expects a function")` after the arguments are
  evaluated, even on an empty receiver, as `median` raises
  `TypeError("median expects a function")`. Rejected: property-name
  selectors such as `Order.variance("total")` and the divisor argument
  above.
- **Extra arguments** are evaluated once, left to right, and ignored, as
  every list query does.
- **Instance lists.** On a class name, `Name.variance(...)` and
  `Name.stdev(...)` are the population queries even when the class declares
  instance methods with those names, as NUC 84 made `Name.median(...)`.
  For a class `Team` that declares `def variance(): return 7`,
  `Team.variance()` raised `TypeError("Team.variance is not a function")` at
  the accepted revision, while its instance `red` answers `red.variance()`
  with `7`, as it still does.
- **The method call only.** Neither name is added to
  [NUC 75](nuc-0075.md)'s noun queries, so `variance of xs` keeps raising
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected keyword 'of'")`. Rejected: global `variance(...)`
  and `stdev(...)` functions and `Math.variance`. They would add names
  beside the list queries, while `variance` is a common name of user
  functions and bindings, such as NUC 80's `def variance(values)`, which
  keep their meaning.
- **The receiver is never written.** The queries read a private list of the
  converted numbers.
- **Bounded cost.** The receiver and the arguments are evaluated once per
  call and the selector once per item. Each value is converted once, and
  two linear passes over the numbers, without recursion, give the mean and
  the total of the squared deviations, so a call is linear in the receiver.
- **Rendered as written.** The call keeps its ordinary rendering:
  `spread = scores.variance()` explains as `spread = scores.variance()`, a
  lambda selector renders as `function()`, and a query and its accepted
  spelling are different statements and graph keys.
- **Pre-existing defects stay.** A callback in a function body cannot read

- **Requires.** [NUC 61](nuc-0061.md), whose shared list query, population

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [List and population variances and standard deviations](../nucleoid.spec.md#list-and-population-variances-and-standard-deviations).

### Calls

- `RECEIVER.variance()`, `RECEIVER.variance(SELECTOR, ...)`,
  `RECEIVER.stdev()` and `RECEIVER.stdev(SELECTOR, ...)` are list queries
  when `RECEIVER` evaluates to a list, however it is reached: a variable, a
  property, an index, a slice, a call result such as `filter(...)`,
  `map(...)`, `slice(...)` or `concat(...)`, a literal, a `.value` snapshot
  or a selected version. The receiver may also be a class's instance list,
  named by the class, by a variable that holds the class or by `$Name`, in
  population order; a range such as `Reading[-3:]` and `Name.list()` are
  lists.
- The receiver is read once, before the arguments. Every supplied argument
  is then evaluated once, left to right, and only the first is used.
- A `null` or undefined receiver keeps the accepted method route: the
  arguments are not evaluated and the call is undefined, so a declaration
  holding it stores `null`.
- On a class name the calls are the population queries even when the class
  declares instance methods `variance` or `stdev`; `instance.variance(...)`
  and `instance.stdev(...)` keep calling the methods.

### Variances and standard deviations

- The values are the receiver's items, or `selector(item)` for every item,
  each converted to a number once.
- With `n` values, the mean `m` is the total of the values added in receiver
  order from `0`, divided by `n`, exactly as `average` computes it. The
  variance is the total of `d * d` for the deviation `d = value - m` of every
  value, added in receiver order from `0`, divided by `n`. The standard
  deviation is the square root of the variance, correctly rounded, as
  `Math.sqrt` gives it. All arithmetic is 64-bit floating point.
- For a list of numbers the variance is exactly what
  `xs.average(x -> (x - xs.average()) * (x - xs.average()))` gives, bit for
  bit, and the standard deviation exactly `Math.sqrt` of it. With a selector
  `f` that has no side effects and gives numbers, they are what the same
  spelling gives with `f(x)` for each `x` and `xs.average(f)` for the mean.
  Where the two differ, as for `null` items in a declaration, rendering and
  the number of selector calls, [Rationale](#rationale) says so.

```
scores = [2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9]
spread = scores.variance()
deviation = scores.stdev()
assert([spread, deviation], [4, 2])
scores.push(11)
assert([spread, deviation], [7.111111111111111, 2.6666666666666665])
scores = [6]
assert([spread, deviation], [0, 0])
team = {laps: [61, 58, 64]}
wobble = team.laps.stdev()
assert(wobble, 2.449489742783178)
team.laps.push(57)
assert(wobble, 2.7386127875258306)
```

- A single value gives `0`, the variance of `scores = [6]`.

### Precision

- The deviations are taken from the mean, so values that are large beside
  their spread keep it, where the mean of the squares minus the square of
  the mean, `xs.average(x -> x * x) - xs.average() ** 2`, gives `0` for the

  square and partial total is rounded again. It is not correctly rounded in
  general. `[2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9]` gives exactly `4`, the stamps give
  `0.6666666666666666`, the number nearest `2 / 3`, and the levels `3`, `5`
  and `10` give `8.666666666666666`, the number nearest `26 / 3`, where the
  one-pass formula gives `8.666666666666664`.

### Conversion and special values

- Each value, an item or a selector result, is converted with the numeric
  conversion of `sum`: a number is itself; `null`, `false`, an empty or
  whitespace-only string and an empty list are `0`; `true` is `1`; a numeric
  string, after trimming, is its number; a one-item list is its item's
  number; a date is its time in milliseconds; objects, instances, classes,
  functions, regular expressions, other strings and longer lists are NaN.
- An empty receiver gives NaN, `0 / 0`. A NaN value or an infinite value
  makes both results NaN. A square or a total beyond the largest number
  overflows to `Infinity`, and a square below the smallest positive number
  underflows to `0`.
- An undefined value makes both results undefined, which a declaration
  stores as `null`; every item is still visited, and an undefined value wins
  over NaN.
- The queries read items, not bindings: a `null` item counts `0` without
  settling a declaration to `null`. Reads inside a selector follow the
  existing rules, so a selector that reads a `null` binding or property
  settles a declaration to `null`, as it would in `map`, `sum` or `median`.
- The variance is never `-0`: the total of the squares starts from `0` and
  adds no negative zero.

### Selectors

- A selector is called once for every item, in order, with the item as its
  only argument; further parameters are `null`. The call order and the
  argument are those of `map`, `sum`, `average` and `median`.
- A supplied first argument that is not a function, including `null`, raises
  `TypeError("variance expects a function")` or
  `TypeError("stdev expects a function")`, even on an empty receiver.
- Every supplied argument, including unused extras, is evaluated once, left
  to right, after the receiver and before the first selector call.

```
class Reading(level):
    this.level = level
Reading(3)
Reading(5)
spike = Reading(10)
gain = 2
spread = Reading.variance(reading -> reading.level)
recent = Reading[-3:].stdev(reading -> reading.level * gain)
assert([spread, recent], [8.666666666666666, 5.887840577551898])
Reading(6)
assert([spread, recent], [6.5, 4.320493798938574])
spike.level = 4
gain = 3
assert([spread, recent], [1.25, 2.449489742783178])
```

- `spread` follows the new reading and the changed one, and `recent`, the
  spread of the last three readings scaled by `gain`, also follows `gain`,
  which its selector reads.

### Liveness and class-level rules

- The queries never write their receiver. A declaration that uses them
  depends on the list or the population, on its argument expressions and on
  whatever the selector reads for every item, as for `sum` and `median`.
- Pushing, popping or replacing a list, creating or deleting an instance,
  and changing a property or a variable that a selector reads re-evaluate
  the declaration.
- In a class-level rule, each instance evaluates its own query, and
  `$Class` inside a filter or a selector is the instance the rule is
  evaluated for.

```
class Machine(name, readings):
    this.name = name
    this.readings = readings
$Machine.drift = $Machine.readings.stdev()
$Machine.unstable = $Machine.drift > 2
press = Machine("press", [50, 54, 46])
lathe = Machine("lathe", [30, 31, 29])
assert([press.drift, lathe.unstable], [3.265986323710904, false])
lathe.readings.push(35)
press.readings.push(50)
[lathe.unstable, press.drift]
```

- This is the `docs/examples.md` case, which returns
  `[true, 2.8284271247461903]`: every machine keeps the standard deviation
  of its own readings, and a rule derived from it follows every reading.

### Transactions

- Selector errors propagate through the existing transaction, so a failed
  standing re-evaluation rolls back the change that triggered it, including
  a list write or a new instance, and a rolled-back transaction restores the
  results of its declarations.

```
def checked(level):
    if level < 0:
        throw "NEGATIVE_LEVEL"
    return level
levels = [3, 5, 10]
spread = levels.variance(checked)
failure = ""
try:
    levels.push(-1)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert([failure, levels, spread], ["NEGATIVE_LEVEL", [3, 5, 10], 8.666666666666666])
try:
    levels.push(6)
    assert(spread, 6.5)
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert([levels, spread], [[3, 5, 10], 8.666666666666666])
class Gauge(level): this.level = level
Gauge(4)
Gauge(8)
wobble = Gauge.stdev(gauge -> checked(gauge.level))
failure = ""
try:
    Gauge(-2)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert([failure, Gauge.length, wobble], ["NEGATIVE_LEVEL", 2, 2])
Gauge(12)
wobble
```

- This returns `3.265986323710904`: the failed push, the undone push and the
  failed new gauge leave the levels, the gauges and both results as they
  were.

### Rendering and graph keys

- Statements, `why`, `affects` and graph and block keys show the call as
  written, with function arguments rendered as `function()`: the rule that
  `why spread` reports is `spread = scores.variance()`, and `why` lists the
  receiver in the declaration's `from` list. A query and its accepted

### Cost

- The receiver and the arguments are evaluated once per call and the
  selector once per item. Each value is converted once into a list of
  numbers, which two linear passes read, so a call is linear in the
  receiver, as an average is, and a standing query is re-derived once per
  change of what it reads.
- The accepted two-pass spelling re-evaluates the mean inside its
  callback, twice per item, so its cost grows with the square of the
  receiver.

## Errors

- No error type or message format is added. The selector errors reuse the
  message format of the other list queries.

| Condition | Error |
| --- | --- |
| A supplied first argument of `variance` that is not a function, including `null`, even on an empty receiver | `TypeError("variance expects a function")` |
| A supplied first argument of `stdev` that is not a function, including `null`, even on an empty receiver | `TypeError("stdev expects a function")` |
| A supplied argument, including an unused extra, reads an undefined name | `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`, raised before the selector is validated |
| The selector raises an error or throws a value | That error or thrown value, unchanged; the triggering change rolls back |
| `variance` or `stdev` on a string, number, boolean, date, function or regular expression | Its accepted `TypeError`, unchanged |
| `variance` or `stdev` on a built-in namespace, such as `Math.variance` | Its accepted `TypeError` or `ReferenceError`, unchanged |
| `variance of OPERAND` or `stdev of OPERAND` | `SyntaxError("Unexpected keyword 'of'")`, unchanged |

- Calls that raised `TypeError("<receiver>.variance is not a function")` or
  `TypeError("<receiver>.stdev is not a function")` on lists and instance
  lists now return their results; with a non-function first argument they
  raise `TypeError("variance expects a function")` or
  `TypeError("stdev expects a function")` instead.
- An expression that uses such a result now raises the error of its own
  operation, quoting the call: `xs.variance()(1)` raises
  `TypeError("xs.variance() is not a function")`, and a quantifier over it
  raises `TypeError("xs.variance() is not a collection")`, where both raised
  `TypeError("xs.variance is not a function")` at the accepted revision.
- A callback in a function body that reads the function's parameters, as in
  `def scaled(xs, k) => xs.variance(x -> x * k)`, raises
  `ReferenceError("k is not defined")`, the pre-existing defect, where it
  raised `TypeError("xs.variance is not a function")`.
- Expression, statement and propagation limits keep their accepted errors.

    other's or its opposite zero, and they become equal at the first value
    that is not `-0`. They differ at the end only for an empty receiver,
    whose variance is `0 / 0`, NaN, either way, and when every value is
    `-0`: the mean is then `+0` or `-0`, every deviation, `-0 - +0` or
    `-0 - -0`, is a zero, every square is `+0`, and both results are the
    same `+0`. Nothing else reads the mean.
  - *The deviation `mean - value`.* Subtraction rounds to nearest, which is
    symmetric, and the difference of two numbers is zero only when they are
    equal, so `mean - value` is exactly the negation of `value - mean`

    every NaN reads as NaN.
