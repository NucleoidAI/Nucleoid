
## Abstract

- `selection |> holds` gives the values that the steps of a reasoning
  selection hold, as a list in the selection's order. With `price = 10`,
  `qty = 3`, `shipping = 5`, `subtotal = price * qty` and
  `total = subtotal + shipping`, `total |> why |> holds` is
  `[35, 30, 5, 10, 3]`, `total |> why |> stated |> holds` is `[5, 10, 3]`,
  the values of the facts the total rests on, and
  `price |> affects |> holds` is `[30, 35]`, the values that a change of the
  price reaches.
- It completes [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s projection: `nodes` names the
  steps of a selection, and `holds` gives what they hold. It is exactly the
  accepted `(selection).map(step => step.holds)`, where `selection` is the
  selection that the pipeline denotes, read as the accepted stages read
  their source, so a reference alone denotes its own one-step selection, as
  for `nodes`, and `total |> holds` is `[35]`. Each value is the one that
  the step's record holds, unchanged, and the list stays live as that
  spelling does: a declaration `values = total |> why |> holds` follows
  later changes of the values and of the graph, and settles to `null` when
  a value it gives is `null`, as that spelling's declaration does. One
  limitation remains: over a step that holds undefined, which only an
  assignment used as an expression stores, a class-level `if` that reads
  the stage decides where the spelling's condition stands aside.
- The stage ends the pipeline, as `nodes` does: its result is a list of
  values, not a selection, so `total |> why |> holds |> why` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain total|>why|>holds")`.
- It has no prefix form, because `holds total` already reads as two
  juxtaposed statements. `holds` stays an ordinary name everywhere else, as
  a variable, function, property, object key or the `holds` field of a step
  record, and a local or parameter, state variable, function or class named
  `holds` makes the pipeline raise `TypeError("holds is not an operation")`,
  as bindings shadow the other stages.
- No keyword, lexer token, error type or message is added. Every accepted
  program keeps its parse and behavior. Only programs that were
  whole-program parse errors at the accepted revision change. The ones
  measured raised the parse-time `TypeError("holds is not an operation")`;
  where the statement parser backtracks from an unpacking or
  object-destructuring pattern, `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` or
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` first; or, in a program that also holds a
  form that only a later pass of the parser reads, that pass's first error.
- The public `Stage` enum gains the variant `Stage::Holds`, which needs human
  SemVer review. No project version is changed. The authoritative contract
  is
  [Held values of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#held-values-of-reasoning-selections).

## Motivation

- A world model explains a conclusion by what it rests on, and often needs
  the values themselves: the figures behind a total, the readings behind an
  alarm, or the values a change moves. `why`, `affects` and their stages
  select the steps, and NUC 112's `nodes` names them; their values have no
  stage.
- Today the values are spelled
  `(tax |> why |> stated).map(step => step.holds)`, or with the selector of
  [NUC 79](nuc-0079.md), `(stated why tax).map(.holds)`, which wraps the
  pipeline in parentheses and adds a call: 11 lexer tokens, six more than
  `stated why tax |> holds`. At the accepted revision, the specification,
  the language reference, the README, the examples and the synthesized
  records spell such a projection 38 times outside the generated export, as
  a selector, a lambda or a comprehension: 28 in `nucleoid.spec.md`, two each
  in `README.md`, `docs/README.md`, `docs/examples.md` and
  `tests/reference.md`, and two in the synthesized records. The same count
  finds eight projections of the `rule` field.
- At the accepted revision, `c |> why |> holds` raises
  `TypeError("holds is not an operation")` while the program is parsed, so
  no statement of the program runs.
- A stage keeps the question inside the pipeline that asks it, so a world
  model reads its explanations as the rest of its reasoning reads, left to
  right: `frost |> why |> stated |> holds` gives the readings that a frost
  warning rests on.
- The accepted spelling also builds a record object for every step and then
  reads one field of each. Inside a declaration, each read of a fresh
  record's `holds` field adds a pending graph node: with `a = 1`,
  `b = a + 2` and `c = b * 2`, after `values = (why c).map(.holds)` and two
  writes of `a`, `--graph` lists nine pending nodes, `obj:0.holds` to
  `obj:8.holds`, of which only the last three are still read. A standing
  declaration so grows the graph by one node for every step each time it is
  evaluated.
- The most compact accepted spellings of one explanation's values,
  `(why c).map(.holds)` and the comprehension `[s.holds for s in why c]`,
  are 10 lexer tokens, and `(c |> why |> stated).map(.holds)` is 13,
  against 4 for `why c |> holds` and 7 for `c |> why |> stated |> holds`.
  The lambda spelling `(why c).map(step => step.holds)` is 13.
- Prior art: the paired keys and values of a map, as in JavaScript's
  `Object.keys` and `Object.values` and Python's `dict.keys()` and
  `dict.values()`, which `nodes` and `holds` follow for the names and the
  values of a selection's steps; and the accepted stages of NUC 48, NUC 82,
  NUC 88, NUC 106, NUC 112 and NUC 120, which shape selections inside the
  same pipeline.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `226268f4` on 18-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform draw
  over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks eligible for
  automatic selection and selected Reasoning enhancement, index 3, with
  probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the caller chose this bounded
  enhancement because it is the sibling of NUC 112's `nodes`, which left the
  projections of the other step fields out on purpose, and the documents
  and datasets ask a selection for its values far more often than for any
  other field but its names. No NUC proposes it. Token comparison is
  required; runtime applicability does not apply to this category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("holds is not an operation")` for `holds`, whatever follows it,
  and only that parser consumes `|>`. Giving `|> holds` a meaning therefore
  changes only inputs that were parse errors. Where the statement parser
  backtracks, some of those inputs reported another parse error first:
  `x, y = c |> why |> holds` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` and
  `{length} = c |> why |> holds` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as
  for NUC 88's `path`, NUC 106's set stages, NUC 112's `nodes` and NUC 120's
  `union`. A program that also holds a form that only a later pass of the
  accepted parser reads, such as a receiver dot, an operator section of
  [NUC 109](nuc-0109.md) or a Python-style `self` parameter of
  [NUC 107](nuc-0107.md), reports that pass's first error instead, and is
  still a whole-program parse error. Inside a template interpolation the
  accepted parser ignores trailing tokens, so `` `v ${c why c |> holds}` ``
  keeps its accepted result.
- **A final stage, not a member, call or prefix.** The values are the last
  question asked of a selection, so they end the pipeline that asks it.
  Rejected:
  - a member `SELECTION.holds`: `(why c).holds` reads a missing property of
    a list today and gives undefined, which would change;
  - a call `holds(SELECTION)`, which is an ordinary call today, answered by
    a function named `holds` or raising `ReferenceError("holds is not defined")`;
  - a prefix form `holds c`, which is two juxtaposed statements today;
  - a general projection stage such as `|> pluck(holds)` or `|> map(f)`,
    which the request does not ask for and which `.map` already spells.
- **It ends the pipeline, as `nodes` does.** Its result is a list of values,
  as the accepted spelling's is, so whatever follows it reads that list as
  whatever follows the accepted spelling does. Every NUC 11 operation and
  every accepted stage but `nodes` returns a selection; this one does not,
  so a stage after it has a source that is neither a reference nor a
  selection and raises `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, and an
  `intersect`, `except`, `union` or `path` argument that ends with it raises
  the same. Rejected: reading the values as references again, which no
  accepted spelling does and which would give a value the meaning of a
  graph node.
- **The accepted spelling by construction.** The stage selects exactly as
  the pipeline before it selects, builds the step records that the accepted
  spelling builds, and gives the `holds` field of each, in the same order.
  It walks nothing a second time.
- **Values as the records hold them.** Each value is the record's `holds`
  field, which is the value of the step's node when the selection is made:
  an instance's own step gives the instance, a list or an object is given as
  the record holds it, and the step of a class, a function declaration or an
  `if` block gives `null`, as its record does. Rejected: rendering, copying
  or filtering the values, which no accepted spelling does.
- **The records, without their reads.** Building the records keeps the
  numbering of anonymous objects as it is under the accepted spelling, and
  programs observe that numbering: with `a = 1`, `b = a + 1` and
  `c = b * 2`, after `x = (c |> why).map(step => step.holds)` the object
  literal `p = {k: 1}` is `obj:3`, so `String(p)` is `"[object obj:3]"`,
  where without the three records it would name `obj:0`. Rejected: giving
  the values from the steps without building records, which would change
  that numbering in every program that creates anonymous objects after the
  stage. The stage does not record its reads of the records' `holds` fields,
  which no program can write because no program holds the records, so a
  declaration that holds it adds none of the pending graph nodes that the
  accepted spelling adds on each evaluation. Those nodes show only in
  `--graph`.
- **The null rule of stored declarations.** A stored declaration whose
  evaluation reads a null binding or property settles to `null`, and the
  accepted spelling reads each record's `holds` field as a property, so its
  declaration settles to `null` whenever a selected step holds `null`: in a
  model that holds a class or a function declaration,
  `values = (model |> depth(0)).map(step => step.holds)` stores `null`, and
  so does a declaration over a step that a deletion has made `null`. The
  stage keeps that rule: it notes each value it gives as the accepted read
  notes it, without recording the read in the graph, so its declarations
  settle exactly as the spelling's do. It notes no undefined value, which
  declarations settle to `null` and only an assignment used as an
  expression stores in a step; the
  [limitation](#observation-liveness-and-transactions) that this leaves in
  class-level `if` conditions is measured below. Rejected: exempting the
  stage from the rule, which would store a list where the accepted spelling
  stores `null`.
- **Undefined selections stay undefined.** A `depth` stage with a `null`
  limit makes a selection undefined ([NUC 82](nuc-0082.md)), and the
  accepted spelling's `.map` on it gives undefined, so the stage gives
  undefined, which a declaration stores as `null`.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `holds` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("holds is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md),
  NUC 82, [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md), NUC 88, [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md), NUC 112 and
  [NUC 120](nuc-0120.md) do for their stages. A model that defines its own
  `holds` never has it silently replaced by a stage. The check runs with the
  pipeline's other checks, before the source is selected. At the accepted
  revision these programs raised the same message while parsing; under this
  NUC the statements before the pipeline run first, and a `try` can catch
  it.
- **One path for both final stages.** NUC 112 gives `nodes` a path of its
  own: the pipeline stops at a `nodes` link, and a top-level `nodes` stage
  checks its binding, selects its source and builds the names through the
  records. `holds` takes that same path, which this NUC generalizes to the
  two projections through a crate-private, closed enum of the projected
  step field, matched by name and statically dispatched. The two stages so
  share the binding check, the selection, the undefined selection, the
  record building, the unrecorded reads and the null rule, and differ only
  in the field they give; a node name is never `null`, so the rule leaves
  `nodes` as it was. Rejected: a copy of the path for `holds`, which would let the
  two final stages drift apart. NUC 112's behavior is unchanged.
- **Naming.** `holds` is the name of the step field the stage gives, which
  every selection record already carries, so `SELECTION |> holds` reads as
  what the selection holds, as `nodes` reads as its nodes. Rejected:
  - `values`, which 29 programs of the four executable documents and the
    synthesized records bind, 34 times in all, as a variable or a parameter,
    where every binding would shadow the stage; `holds` is bound in one of
    them, `synth-20-0009`, whose `holds = 2` counts a ship's holds and
    writes no pipeline;
  - `value`, which reads as one value and is the name of the accepted
    `.value` snapshot;
  - `vals` and `data`, which abbreviate or read as an object;
  - `facts`, which names only the stated steps of a selection.
- **Requires.** NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 83, NUC 88, NUC 106, NUC 112 and
  NUC 120, whose stages it ends and whose source, shadowing and
  undefined-selection rules it shares; NUC 112's `nodes` is the final stage
  whose contract and path it shares, and NUC 120's `union` is among the
  stages it ends. Those already require everything else it relies on, such
  as pipelines, `why`, `affects` and `model`.
- **Not added.** A prefix form, a stage that keeps the selection,
  projections of the other step fields `rule`, `state` and `from`, a general
  projection stage, and any change to the accepted stages, `nodes` included.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Held values of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#held-values-of-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> holds` is a pipeline stage: `|>` and then the identifier
  `holds`, with no argument. It binds at the precedence of `|>`, and it may
  follow a prefix stage, so `why total |> holds` is `(why total) |> holds`.
- Everywhere else `holds` is an ordinary identifier, and so is the `holds`
  field of a step record. There is no prefix form, so `holds total` stays
  the two statements `holds` and `total`, and `holds(selection)` stays an
  ordinary call. `|> Holds`, `|> hold` and every other name after `|>` that
  is not a stage keep the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("<name> is not an operation")`.
- What follows the name reads as it reads after `nodes`: `|> holds(1)` and
  `|> holds[0]` are the stage followed by the juxtaposed statements `(1)`
  and `[0]`, and `|> holds.length` raises `SyntaxError("Unexpected '.'")` at
  the `.`, as `|> nodes(1)`, `|> nodes[0]` and `|> nodes.length` do at the
  accepted revision. The length of the list is written
  `(c |> why |> holds).length`.
- The stage parses to the public `Expr::Reason { stage: Stage::Holds, source }`.
  `Stage` gains `Stage::Holds`, after `Stage::Nodes`, which `Stage::as_str`
  names `"holds"`. `Stage::from_name` is unchanged: it also reads the prefix
  stages, and `holds` has none.

### Values

- The stage selects what its source selects, through every stage before
  it, and gives the `holds` field of each selected step, in the selection's
  order. It is exactly `(SELECTION).map(step => step.holds)`, where
  `SELECTION` is the selection that the pipeline before `holds` denotes,
  read as the accepted stages read their source, for every source and stage
  the pipeline accepts.
- Each value is the one that the step's record holds in its `holds` field,
  which is the value of the step's node when the selection is made: a
  number, string, boolean, list, object or `null`. An instance's own step
  gives the instance itself, a variable that holds a function gives the
  function, and the step of a class, a function declaration or an `if` block
  gives `null`, as their records do. So after `xs = [1, 2]`,
  `n = xs.length` and `label = "n" + n`, `label |> why |> holds` is
  `["n2", 2, [1, 2]]`.
- A reference alone denotes its own one-step selection, not its value, as it
  does before `stated` or `derived` and for `nodes`. So `total |> holds` is
  `[35]` in the example below, and its accepted spelling writes that
  selection, as in `(total |> depth(0)).map(step => step.holds)`. In a
  declaration, the stage reads and observes `total` alone, as that spelling
  and `total |> nodes` do, while
  `(total |> why |> depth(0)).map(step => step.holds)`, with the same
  values, also reads and observes every step of the explanation that it
  walks. The value `(total).map(step => step.holds)` of a number is not
  that spelling: it raises `TypeError("number.map is not a function")`.
- `model` selects every node of the model, in model order, without pending
  nodes and reasoning observers, so `model |> holds` excludes every
  declaration that holds a stage. An empty selection gives `[]`: after
  `o = {}`, the absent property's `o.gone |> holds` is `[]`.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
shipping = 5
subtotal = price * qty
total = subtotal + shipping
values = total |> why |> holds
facts = total |> why |> stated |> holds
rules = total |> why |> derived |> holds
assert(values, [35, 30, 5, 10, 3])
assert([facts, rules], [[5, 10, 3], [35, 30]])
base = 4
shipping = base + 1
assert(values, [35, 30, 5, 4, 10, 3])
assert(facts, [4, 10, 3])
price = 20
assert(values, [65, 60, 5, 4, 20, 3])
```

### Ending a pipeline

- The stage's result is a list of values, not a selection. A stage written
  after it has that list as its source, which is neither a reference nor a
  selection, so after the checks of its own binding it raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, where `<source>` renders the
  pipeline up to `holds`: `c |> why |> holds |> why`,
  `c |> why |> holds |> depth(1)`, `c |> why |> holds |> holds` and
  `c |> why |> holds |> nodes` raise
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>holds")`, as the same stages raise
  after `(why c).map(step => step.holds)` for its own source.
  `c |> why |> nodes |> holds` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>nodes")`, as any stage after `nodes`
  does.
- An `intersect`, `except` or `union` argument that ends with `holds` is not
  a selection, and a `path` target that ends with it is not a reference, so
  `intersect(f |> why |> holds)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain f|>why|>holds")` and
  `path(a |> why |> holds)` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain a|>why|>holds")`.
- A name that holds the list is a reasoning observer, which no selection
  holds, so after `other = f |> why |> holds`, `intersect(other)` keeps
  nothing, as for a name that holds the accepted spelling's list.

### Sources and undefined selections

- The source is written as a pipeline's source is, and it keeps that
  source's diagnostics: `1 |> holds` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`,
  `missing |> why |> holds` raises `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`
  and, outside a `suppose` block, `c |> why |> diff |> holds` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- A property path whose base is `null` or undefined raises
  `ReferenceError("<base> is not defined")`, as in
  `ReferenceError("n is not defined")` for `n.p |> holds` after `n = null`,
  and one whose base is a number or a string raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, as in
  `TypeError("Cannot explain k.p")` after `k = 5`.
- A `depth` stage with a `null` or undefined limit makes the selection
  undefined, and then the stage gives undefined, which a declaration stores
  as `null` until the limit is known, a loop over it raises
  `TypeError("<source> is not iterable")`, and reads from it give `null`, as
  for the accepted spelling.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Bindings

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `holds` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("holds is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are bindings; methods,
  properties and object keys are not, and neither is the `holds` field of a
  step record.
- The check runs first, then the checks of the stages before it, from the
  outermost inward, then the source is selected, as for the accepted stages
  and `nodes`.
- A binding declared after a standing declaration makes that declaration's
  next evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails
  and rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and
  of `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff`, `path`, `intersect`, `except`,
  `union` and `nodes`, which a binding shadows, is unchanged. A binding named
  `nodes` does not shadow `holds`, nor one named `holds` `nodes`.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its selection
  made, including those of the steps a filter drops, so a standing
  declaration re-derives whenever its selection would: through a value,
  which every element of the list is, a rule, a new or deleted node, a
  rewiring or a class-level rule. An empty result still follows its source.
- It builds the step records that the accepted spelling builds, so the
  anonymous objects that later statements create are numbered as after that
  spelling, and it reads their `holds` fields without recording the reads,
  so a declaration that holds it depends only on what its selection observes
  and gains no pending graph node for the records.
- Those reads keep the null rule of stored declarations, as the accepted
  spelling's do: a declaration, property or class-level rule whose stage
  gives a `null` value settles to `null`, as one does that reads a null
  property. So in a model that holds a class or a function declaration,
  `values = model |> holds` stores `null` and the expression
  `model |> holds` gives the list with its `null` elements, and after
  `x = 2`, `b = a + x` and `delete x`, which make `b` and `c` `null`, a
  declaration `c |> why |> holds` stores `null`, as the accepted spelling's
  declarations do.
- **Limitation: undefined step values.** A step's value is the variable or
  property that its node holds, and `null` for every other node.
  Declarations, properties and class-level rules settle an undefined value
  to `null`, but an assignment used as an expression does not: with
  `o = {k: 1}`, `p = {x: 0}`, `p.x = 1`, `f = v -> p.x = v` and
  `f(o.missing)`, the property `p.x` holds undefined, and so does its step.
  The stage then gives the accepted spelling's values, `[undefined]` for
  `p.x |> holds`, and a declaration over it stores the same list, but the
  stage does not note the undefined read that the spelling's read of the
  `holds` field notes. Only a class-level `if` consults that note: after
  `class T(n)` with `this.n = n`, the class-level
  `if $T.n > 0 and (p.x |> holds).length > 0:` with `$T.flag = true` sets
  `flag` on `t = T(1)` to `true`, while the same `if` over
  `(p.x |> depth(0)).map(step => step.holds)` stands aside and leaves `flag`
  undefined, as one does that reads `p.x` itself. Over `q |> why` with
  `q = [p.x, 1]` and over `model` the two differ alike. A top-level `if`, a
  stored declaration and an expression give the same results.
- An expression that holds the stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding
  it is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and that
  `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in place, and
  class-level rules give each instance its own list.
- `.value` snapshots the list. When a declaration is stored, a `.value`
  read in a `depth` limit inside the stage's source is frozen, as it is in
  the accepted spelling's receiver.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed statements
  behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores the declarations that
  hold the stage, their observations and queued refreshes.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
subtotal = price * qty
values = subtotal |> why |> holds
failure = ""
try:
    values = 1 |> holds
catch error:
    failure = error
assert(failure, TypeError("Cannot explain 1"))
assert(values, [30, 10, 3])
try:
    fee = 2
    subtotal = price * qty + fee
    assert(values, [32, 10, 3, 2])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert(values, [30, 10, 3])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>holds` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an expression, with the
  source rendered once, so `c |> why |> holds` renders as `c|>why|>holds`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, except where the
  accepted renderer already abbreviates part of its source: a pipeline that
  is the receiver of a member read renders without its parentheses, so
  `(c |> why |> holds).length` renders as `c|>why|>holds.length`, as
  `(c |> why |> nodes).length` renders as `c|>why|>nodes.length`.
- A stage and its accepted spelling render differently, so they are
  different graph keys, conditions and `rule` texts, and an error that quotes
  one quotes `c|>why|>holds` where the other quotes
  `c|>why.map(function())`, or `c|>why.map(.holds)` for the selector
  spelling.

### Findings

- **Medium, public API (F1).** `Stage` is public and not
  `#[non_exhaustive]`, so adding `Stage::Holds` breaks downstream code that
  matches it exhaustively, as [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s `Stage::Nodes` did,
  which Rust's semantic versioning treats as a major change. The version
  decision is human-owned, and no version number changes.
- **Low, semantics and normative documentation (F2).** The limitation that
  [Observation, liveness and transactions](#observation-liveness-and-transactions)
  records is real, and this document states it accurately. Over a step that
  holds undefined, which only an assignment used as an expression stores, a
  class-level `if` whose condition reads the stage, directly, through a
  function call, through `??` or a quantifier, or with an `else`, decides
  where every accepted spelling, with a lambda, a selector or a
  comprehension, stands aside. Method and constructor bodies, functions,
  top-level `if`s, declarations, class-level property rules, loops and
  expressions are unaffected, and the two agree again once the value is
  defined. But the `Held values of reasoning selections` section of
  `nucleoid.spec.md` and the subsection of `docs/README.md` state that the
  stage is exactly `(SELECTION).map(step => step.holds)`, and the
  specification lists the kinds of value without undefined, without this
  limitation. The caller reproduced the difference with its own final build,
  and the accepted spelling's result on the accepted release binary.
  Qualifying the normative text, or noting the undefined read in the
  runtime, needs a fresh proposal and evaluation.
- **Info, compatibility documentation (F3).** Besides
  `c |> why |> holds.length`, which [Compatibility](#compatibility) names,
  `c |> why |> holds == [4, 2, 1]` also changes from the parse-time
  `TypeError("holds is not an operation")` to
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '=='")` at line 7, column 22, as `nodes` reads at
  the accepted revision, and the line-REPL entries of the Syntax Summary
  block change from one error to another. Each stays an error, so no
  accepted program changes.
- **Info, implementation (F4).** The `Stage::Holds` arms of
  `Stage::direction`, `Runtime::apply_stage` and `Runtime::apply_ranked`
  cannot be reached, because `pipeline` stops at a stage with a projection;
  they keep those matches exhaustive, as the three surviving mutants of

- **Info, cost (F5).** The evaluator's whole-process spot check at 1,000
