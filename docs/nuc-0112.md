# NUC 112 -- Node Names of Reasoning Selections

```
NUC: 112
Title: Node Names of Reasoning Selections
Author: Can Mingir <canmingir@gmail.com>
Status: Accepted
Type: Standards Track
Category: Reasoning enhancement
Created: 17-Nov-2026
Requires: 48, 82, 83, 88, 106
```

## Abstract

- Adds a final reasoning stage, `SELECTION |> nodes`, that gives the node
  names of a selection's steps, in the selection's order, as a list of
  strings. With `a = 1`, `b = a + 2` and `c = b * 2`, `c |> why |> nodes` is
  `["c", "b", "a"]`, `c |> why |> stated |> nodes` is `["a"]` and
  `a |> affects |> nodes` is `["b", "c"]`.
- It is exactly the accepted `(SELECTION).map(step => step.node)`, where
  `SELECTION` is the selection that the pipeline denotes, read as the
  accepted stages read their source, so a reference alone is its own
  one-step selection and `c |> nodes` is `["c"]`. It stays live as that
  spelling does: a declaration `names = total |> why |> nodes` follows later
  changes of the graph.
- It ends a pipeline over any selection source the accepted stages take: a
  reference, `model`, a prefix stage such as `why c`, or a pipeline of
  accepted stages such as `why`, `affects`, `stated`, `derived`, `depth`,
  `path`, `intersect`, `except` and `diff`. What follows it reads a list of
  names, not a selection, as after the accepted spelling, so
  `c |> why |> nodes |> why` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>nodes")`.
- It has no prefix form, because `nodes c` already reads as two juxtaposed
  statements. A local or parameter, state variable, function or class named
  `nodes` makes the pipeline raise `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")`,
  as bindings named `stated` and `derived` do for theirs.
- No keyword, lexer token, error type or message is added. Every accepted
  program keeps its parse and behavior. Only inputs that were parse errors at
  the accepted revision change: most raised the parse-time
  `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")`, and where the statement parser
  backtracks from an unpacking or object-destructuring pattern, some raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` or `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` first.
- The public `Stage` enum gains the variant `Stage::Nodes`, which needs human
  SemVer review. No project version is changed. The authoritative contract is
  [Node names of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#node-names-of-reasoning-selections).

## Motivation

- An explanation is a list of steps, and the question most programs ask of
  it is which nodes take part: which facts support a conclusion, and what a
  change reaches. The accepted spelling maps the steps, as in
  `(total |> why |> stated).map(step => step.node)`, and the executable
  documents write that mapping 194 times: 132 in the specification's cases,
  25 in the executable reference, 21 in the examples and 8 each in the two
  READMEs.
- At the accepted revision, `c |> why |> nodes` raises
  `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")` while the program is parsed.
- A stage keeps the question inside the pipeline that asks it, so a world
  model reads its explanations as the rest of its reasoning reads, left to
  right: `alarm |> why |> stated |> nodes` names the facts that raise an
  alarm.
- The accepted spelling also builds a record object for every step and then
  reads one field of each. Inside a declaration, each read of a fresh
  record's `node` field adds a pending graph node: after
  `names = (why c).map(.node)` and two writes of `a`, `--graph` lists nine
  pending nodes, `obj:0.node` to `obj:8.node`, of which only the last three
  are still read. A standing declaration so grows the graph by one node for
  every step each time it is evaluated.
- The most compact accepted spelling, `(why c).map(.node)` with a
  [NUC 79](nuc-0079.md) selector, is 10 lexer tokens, and
  `(c |> why |> stated).map(.node)` is 13, against 4 for `why c |> nodes` and
  7 for `c |> why |> stated |> nodes`. The lambda spelling
  `(why c).map(step => step.node)` is 13.
- Prior art: the node lists that graph and logic tools return for a
  derivation, and the accepted stages of NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 88 and
  NUC 106, which shape selections inside the same pipeline.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `92cbd316` on 17-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform draw
  over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks eligible for
  automatic selection and selected Reasoning enhancement, index 3, with
  probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the caller chose this bounded
  enhancement because the documents ask a selection for its node names more
  than for anything else. No other NUC proposes it: [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md)
  and [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md) speak of node names only descriptively. Token
  comparison is required; runtime applicability does not apply to this
  category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")` for `nodes`, and only that parser
  consumes `|>`. Giving `|> nodes` a meaning therefore changes only inputs
  that were parse errors. Where the statement parser backtracks, some of
  those inputs reported another parse error first:
  `x, y = c |> why |> nodes` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` and
  `{length} = c |> why |> nodes` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as
  for NUC 88's `path` and NUC 106's set stages. Inside a template
  interpolation the accepted parser ignores trailing tokens, so
  `` `v ${c why c |> nodes}` `` keeps its accepted result, `"v 6"`.
- **A final stage, not a member, call or prefix.** The names are the last
  question asked of a selection, so they end the pipeline that asks it.
  Rejected:
  - a member `SELECTION.nodes`: `(why c).nodes` reads a missing property of
    a list today and gives undefined, which would change;
  - a call `nodes(SELECTION)`, which is an ordinary call today, answered by a
    function named `nodes` or raising `ReferenceError("nodes is not defined")`;
  - a prefix form `nodes c`, which is two juxtaposed statements today;
  - a general projection stage such as `|> pluck(node)`, which the request
    does not ask for and which `.map` already spells.
- **It ends the pipeline.** Its result is a list of strings, as the accepted
  spelling's is, so whatever follows it reads that list as whatever follows
  the accepted spelling does. Every NUC 11 operation and every accepted stage
  returns a selection; this one does not, so a stage after it has a source
  that is neither a reference nor a selection and raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, and an `intersect`, `except` or
  `path` argument that ends with it raises the same. Rejected: reading the
  names as references again, so that `|> nodes |> why` walks from the named
  nodes, which no accepted spelling does and which would make the stage an
  identity on selections.
- **The accepted spelling by construction.** The stage selects exactly as the
  pipeline before it selects, builds the step records that the accepted
  spelling builds, and gives the `node` field of each, in the same order. It
  walks nothing a second time.
- **The records, without their reads.** Building the records keeps the
  numbering of anonymous objects as it is under the accepted spelling, and
  programs observe that numbering: with `a`, `b` and `c` as above, after
  `x = (why c).map(.node)` the object literal `p = {k: 1}` is `obj:3`, so
  `String(p)` and `` `${p}` `` are `"[object obj:3]"`, and the rule of
  `q = p.value` reads `q = obj:3`, where without the three records they would
  name `obj:0`. Rejected: giving the
  names from the steps without building records, which would change those
  values in every program that creates anonymous objects after the stage.
  The stage does not record its reads of the records' `node` fields, which
  no program can write because no program holds the records, so a
  declaration that holds it adds none of the pending graph nodes that the
  accepted spelling adds on each evaluation. Those nodes show only in
  `--graph`.
- **Undefined selections stay undefined.** A `depth` stage with a `null`
  limit makes a selection undefined ([NUC 82](nuc-0082.md)), and the accepted
  spelling's `.map` on it gives undefined, so the stage gives undefined,
  which a declaration stores as `null`.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `nodes` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md),
  NUC 82, NUC 83, NUC 88 and [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md) do for their stages. A
  model that defines its own `nodes` never has it silently replaced by a
  stage. The check runs with the pipeline's other checks, before the source
  is selected. At the accepted revision these programs raised the same
  message while parsing; under this NUC the statements before the pipeline
  run first, and a `try` can catch it.
- **Naming.** `nodes` is the plural of the step field it reads, and NUC 83
  and NUC 88 call these names node names. Rejected:
  - `names`, which 17 lines of the documents and the dataset bind as a
    variable, where a binding would shadow the stage;
  - `keys` and `ids`, which name object keys and identifiers;
  - `node`, which reads as one node;
  - `pluck`, which names an operation rather than its result.
- **Requires.** NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 83, NUC 88 and NUC 106, whose stages it
  ends and whose source, shadowing and undefined-selection rules it shares.
  Those already require everything else it relies on, such as pipelines,
  `why`, `affects` and `model`.
- **Not added.** A prefix form, a stage that keeps the selection, projections
  of the other step fields `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from`, and any
  change to the accepted stages.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Node names of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#node-names-of-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> nodes` is a pipeline stage: `|>` and then the identifier
  `nodes`, with no argument. It binds at the precedence of `|>`, and it may
  follow a prefix stage, so `why total |> nodes` is `(why total) |> nodes`.
- Everywhere else `nodes` is an ordinary identifier. There is no prefix
  form, so `nodes total` stays the two statements `nodes` and `total`, and
  `nodes(selection)` stays an ordinary call. `|> Nodes`, `|> node` and every
  other name after `|>` that is not a stage keep the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("<name> is not an operation")`.
- The stage parses to the public `Expr::Reason { stage: Stage::Nodes, source }`.
  `Stage` gains `Stage::Nodes`, which `Stage::as_str` names `"nodes"`.
  `Stage::from_name` is unchanged: it also reads the prefix stages, and
  `nodes` has none.

### Names

- The stage selects what its source selects, through every stage before it,
  and gives the `node` field of each selected step as a string, in the
  selection's order. It is exactly `(SELECTION).map(step => step.node)`,
  where `SELECTION` is the selection that the pipeline before `nodes`
  denotes, read as the accepted stages read their source, for every source
  and stage the pipeline accepts.
- A reference alone denotes its own one-step selection, not its value, as
  it does before `stated` or `derived`: `c |> derived` is the step of `c`,
  `a |> stated` the step of `a`, and `c |> stated` is `[]`. So
  `total |> nodes` is `["total"]`, and its accepted spelling writes that
  selection, as in `(total |> why |> depth(0)).map(step => step.node)`. The
  value `(total).map(step => step.node)` of a number is not that spelling:
  it raises `TypeError("number.map is not a function")`.
- `model` selects every node of the model, in model order, without pending
  nodes and reasoning observers, so `model |> nodes` excludes every
  declaration that holds a stage. An empty selection gives `[]`.
- The names are those of the graph's keys: a variable's name, an instance
  property's path such as `first.total`, an instance's name and a class's
  `$Name`.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
shipping = 5
subtotal = price * qty
total = subtotal + shipping
steps = total |> why |> nodes
facts = total |> why |> stated |> nodes
rules = total |> why |> derived |> nodes
assert(steps, ["total", "subtotal", "shipping", "price", "qty"])
assert([facts, rules], [["shipping", "price", "qty"], ["total", "subtotal"]])
base = 4
shipping = base + 1
assert(steps, ["total", "subtotal", "shipping", "base", "price", "qty"])
assert(facts, ["base", "price", "qty"])
```

### Ending a pipeline

- The stage's result is a list of names, not a selection. A stage written
  after it has that list as its source, which is neither a reference nor a
  selection, so after the checks of its own binding it raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, where `<source>` renders the
  pipeline up to `nodes`: `c |> why |> nodes |> why`,
  `c |> why |> nodes |> depth(1)` and `c |> why |> nodes |> nodes` raise
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>nodes")`, as the same stages raise after
  `(why c).map(step => step.node)` for its own source.
- An `intersect` or `except` argument that ends with `nodes` is not a
  selection, and a `path` target that ends with it is not a reference, so
  `intersect(d |> why |> nodes)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain d|>why|>nodes")` and
  `path(a |> why |> nodes)` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain a|>why|>nodes")`.
- A name that holds the list is a reasoning observer, which no selection
  holds, so after `other = d |> why |> nodes`, `intersect(other)` keeps
  nothing and `except(other)` keeps every step, as for a name that holds the
  accepted spelling's list.

### Sources and undefined selections

- The source is written as a pipeline's source is, and it keeps that
  source's diagnostics: `1 |> nodes` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`,
  `missing |> why |> nodes` raises `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`
  and, outside a `suppose` block, `c |> why |> diff |> nodes` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- A `depth` stage with a `null` or undefined limit makes the selection
  undefined, and then the stage gives undefined, which a declaration stores
  as `null` until the limit is known, a loop over it raises
  `TypeError("<source> is not iterable")`, and reads from it give `null`, as
  for the accepted spelling.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Bindings

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `nodes` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("nodes is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are bindings; methods,
  properties and object keys are not.
- The check runs first, then the checks of the stages before it, from the
  outermost inward, then the source is selected, as for the accepted stages.
- A binding declared after a standing declaration makes that declaration's
  next evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails
  and rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and
  of `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff`, `path`, `intersect` and `except`,
  which a binding shadows, is unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its selection made,
  including those of the steps a filter drops, so a standing declaration
  re-derives whenever its selection would: through a value, a rule, a new or
  deleted node, a rewiring or a class-level rule. An empty result still
  follows its source.
- It builds the step records that the accepted spelling builds, so the
  anonymous objects that later statements create are numbered as after that
  spelling, and it reads their `node` fields without recording the reads, so
  a declaration that holds it depends only on what its selection observes
  and gains no pending graph node for the records.
- An expression that holds the stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding
  it is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and that
  `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in place, and
  class-level rules give each instance its own list.
- `.value` snapshots the list. When a declaration is stored, a `.value` read
  in a `depth` limit inside the stage's source is frozen, as it is in the
  accepted spelling's receiver.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed statements
  behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores the declarations that
  hold the stage, their observations and queued refreshes.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
subtotal = price * qty
names = subtotal |> why |> nodes
failure = ""
try:
    names = 1 |> why |> nodes
catch error:
    failure = error
assert(failure, TypeError("Cannot explain 1"))
assert(names, ["subtotal", "price", "qty"])
try:
    fee = 2
    subtotal = price * qty + fee
    assert(names, ["subtotal", "price", "qty", "fee"])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert(names, ["subtotal", "price", "qty"])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>nodes` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an expression, with the
  source rendered once, so `c |> why |> nodes` renders as `c|>why|>nodes`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, except where the
  accepted renderer already abbreviates part of its source: a pipeline that
  is the receiver of a member read renders without its parentheses, so
  `(c |> why |> nodes).length` renders as `c|>why|>nodes.length`, as
  `(c |> why |> stated).length` renders as `c|>why|>stated.length`.
- A stage and its accepted spelling render differently, so they are
  different graph keys, conditions and `rule` texts, and an error that quotes
  one quotes `c|>why|>nodes` where the other quotes `c|>why.map(.node)`, or
  `c|>why.map(function())` for the lambda spelling.

### Cost

- Pipelines without the stage take their accepted paths and do no work for
  it.
- The stage costs its selection, the step records that the accepted spelling
  also builds and one pass over them. The accepted spelling also calls its
  function once per record and, inside a declaration, records a read of each
  record and adds one pending graph node per step on each evaluation. Both
  keep the records they build in the program's state, as every materialized
  selection does.
- The figures below were measured on release builds of the candidate and of
  the accepted revision, through the library with `std::time::Instant`, on
  one Windows 11 machine with 16 logical processors. Each is the best of
  three runs of one statement after its setup program, and the best of three
  rounds. They compare spellings on one machine and are not guarantees. The
  questions are:
  - chain: `v0 = 1` and `v1 = v0 + 1` onward, n nodes, asking
    `v{n-1} |> why |> nodes`;
  - fan-out: `a` and n nodes `x{i} = a + i`, asking `a |> affects |> nodes`;
  - model: the fan-out's model, asking `model |> nodes`;
  - each against its accepted spelling with a lambda,
    `(SELECTION).map(step => step.node)`, where `SELECTION` is
    `model |> depth(0)` for the model, and the chain also against the
    selector spelling `(SELECTION).map(.node)` and the walk's records alone,
    `v{n-1} |> why`.

| Question | Nodes | Records alone | `\|> nodes` | `.map(step => step.node)` | `.map(.node)` | Accepted build, `.map(step => step.node)` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| chain | 1,000 | 3.76 ms | 3.61 ms | 4.40 ms | 4.41 ms | 4.54 ms |
| chain | 10,000 | 44.6 ms | 43.9 ms | 52.3 ms | 53.3 ms | 53.0 ms |
| fan-out | 1,000 | | 3.66 ms | 4.33 ms | | 4.31 ms |
| fan-out | 10,000 | | 44.1 ms | 50.4 ms | | 52.5 ms |
| model | 1,000 | | 3.52 ms | 4.21 ms | | 4.41 ms |
| model | 10,000 | | 40.9 ms | 51.1 ms | | 51.3 ms |

- The stage takes 0.80 to 0.87 times its accepted spelling's time, about the
  time of the walk's records alone, which it also builds. Both grow about
  linearly: 10,000 nodes take 11.6 to 12.2 times as long as 1,000 for either
  spelling. On every question both spellings give a list of the same
  length in JSON.
- A standing declaration costs one evaluation on each write that changes it.
  These figures are the time of one write, averaged over 10,000 writes of `a`
  in the chain `a`, `b`, `c` and over 1,000 writes of `v0` in a chain of 100
  nodes, with the size of the graph before the first write and after the
  last:

| Declaration | Writes | Time per write | Graph before | Graph after |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `names = c \|> why \|> nodes` | 10,000 | 29.1 µs | 4 | 4 |
| `names = (c \|> why).map(step => step.node)` | 10,000 | 37.2 µs | 7 | 30,007 |

- The accepted spelling's graph grows by one pending node per step on every
  evaluation, three per write of the three-node chain and 100 per write of
  the 100-node chain, so after the writes it holds 30,003 and 100,100
  pending nodes, of which `names` still reads only the last 3 and the last
  100. The stage's graph keeps its size.
- Without the stage, the candidate measured as the accepted revision did:
  every accepted spelling above took between 0.955 and 1.013 times the
  accepted build's time on the candidate build.
- These figures predate the Rust improvement pass. A spot-check after it,
  on release builds of the final tree and of the accepted revision in fresh
  target directories, agrees with them within noise: on the final build the
  10,000-node questions took 0.95 to 1.03 times the times above and the
  standing declarations 0.99 to 1.02 times, with the same graph sizes and
  result lengths, and the stage took 0.85 to 0.86 times its accepted
  spelling's time on the 10,000-node questions.

### Compatibility

- **Why this shape.** `Runtime::reason` evaluates only selection pipelines,
  and a `nodes` stage never becomes one of its links, so every way a `nodes`
  stage reaches a selection, a stage after it, an `intersect` or `except`
  argument or a `path` target, falls to the accepted diagnostic of a
  non-selection without new error code, and no later caller of
  `Runtime::reason` can treat the names as a selection. The other shape, a
  `nodes` link that passes its selection through and that only the
  outermost position may hold, needs its own checks: in `Runtime::reason`
  for a `nodes` link with a stage after it, in `other_selection` for an
  `intersect` or `except` argument and before a `path` target, each raising
  the accepted `Cannot explain` message itself, where a missed check would
  read the names as the selection.

    be reached: `apply_stage`, `apply_ranked` and the crate-private
