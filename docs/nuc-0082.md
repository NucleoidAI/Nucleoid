# NUC 82 -- Depth-Limited Reasoning Selections

```

Title: Depth-Limited Reasoning Selections
Author: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>
Status: Accepted
Type: Standards Track
Category: Reasoning enhancement
Created: 04-Nov-2026
Requires: 8, 12, 14, 45, 48, 57, 62, 65
```

## Abstract

- Add the pipeline stage `SELECTION |> depth(LIMIT)`. It keeps the steps of
  `SELECTION` that lie within `LIMIT` steps of where the walk that selected
  them began, in their order and with every field unchanged, so
  `alarm |> why |> depth(1)` is the alarm and the facts it reads directly,
  and `price |> affects |> depth(1)` is what reads the price directly.
- A step's distance is the length of the shortest path by which the most
  recent `why` or `affects` reached it from that stage's input. `why` keeps
  its inputs at distance 0; `affects` never selects a node by the empty path,
  so its steps are at distance 1 or more. Steps that no walk produced are at
  distance 0, and `stated`, `derived` and `depth` keep distances.
- `LIMIT` is an expression evaluated in place, so `depth(n)` follows `n`. It
  must be a non-negative integer; other values raise
  `TypeError("<limit> is not a non-negative integer")`. A `null` or undefined
  limit makes the selection undefined, which a declaration stores as `null`
  until the limit is known.
- Only `|>` followed by `depth` and a `(` token is the stage. `depth` stays an
  ordinary identifier everywhere else, and a binding named `depth` shadows the
  stage as bindings shadow NUC 48's `stated` and `derived`. Selections stay
  live, observe what their sources observed, and render as
  `SOURCE|>depth(LIMIT)`.
- No keyword, token or error type is added. Every accepted program keeps its
  parse and behavior; only inputs that were errors at the accepted revision
  change. The public `Expr` enum gains `Expr::Depth`, which needs human SemVer
  review. No project version is changed. The authoritative contract is
  [Depth-limited reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#depth-limited-reasoning-selections).
- **Outcome: Accepted.** The independent evaluation and the caller's
  integrated-tree checks passed all three hard criteria, and the independent
  evaluator decided Accepted. The required token comparison ran in
  continuous mode: 11 cases use fewer tokens, 937 the same and none more. No
  policy check failed. The evaluation corrects one claim below: subscript
  slices give shorter fair accepted spellings than the recorded
  counterparts, so the margins are 10 to 69 tokens rather than 10 to 88;
  every case still uses fewer tokens than any fair accepted spelling found.

## Motivation

- A world model is read through its explanations. `why` and `affects` are
  transitive: `why total` lists every fact a total was ever derived from, and
  `affects price` everything that rests on the price, however far away. In a
  model of any size that closure is long, and most of it is not what a
  question about the total is asking.
- An LLM that maintains a model needs explanations sized for its context:
  the direct causes of a value, or what a change touches first. Handing it
  the whole closure spends tokens on steps it did not ask about and hides the
  ones it did.
- Accepted Nucleoid can only filter a closure after the fact. The direct
  causes of `c` are `c` and the steps named in its `from` list, so the
  accepted spelling is
  `trace = why c` with `near = trace.slice(0, trace[0].from.length + 1)`,
  which depends on the walk's listing order, or a `filter` over `from` lists.
  Two steps out needs a quantifier over the first ring, and a limit held in a
  variable needs one spelling per distance. Impact needs
  `(affects a).filter(s -> s.from.includes("a"))`, which repeats the source
  as a string.
- NUC 11 plans `depth(n)`, "the selection narrowed to nodes within `n` steps of
  where the chain began", as part of its fourth stage. This NUC specifies that
  one stage on its own, without NUC 11's other stages or its `depth(x, n)`
  call form.
- Each of the eleven new cases uses 10 to 88 fewer lexer tokens than the most
  compact behavior-equivalent accepted program found for it.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The caller selected Reasoning enhancement by one
  tool-backed uniform draw over the five categories NUC 1's registry marks
  eligible for automatic selection: index 3 with probability 1/5 and no
  redraw, in continuous mode. Within it the caller chose NUC 11's planned
  `depth(n)` stage: the next item of NUC 11's roadmap, bounded, sizing
  explanations for LLM context, with live accepted counterparts. The
  category's token gate therefore applies; runtime applicability does not.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored (`missing_rubric`), not zero. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **A stage, not a function or a prefix.** `depth` narrows a selection, as
  `stated` and `derived` do, so it is a pipeline stage. It is the first stage
  with an argument, written `depth(LIMIT)`, as NUC 11 writes it.
  - Rejected: a prefix form `depth c`, which would need a second operand.
  - Rejected: NUC 11's call form `depth(x, 2)`. `depth(c, 1)` is an ordinary
    call today and stays one, and a call cannot tell which node produced its
    first argument, as NUC 11 notes for `why`.
  - Rejected: `depth` without parentheses. A bare `|> depth` keeps its
    accepted error.
- **Shortest distances, walk order kept.** The accepted walks list steps in
  the order a depth-first worklist discovers them, which is not distance
  order: with `s = u + 1`, `q = s * 2`, `p = u * 3` and `t = p + q`, `why t`
  is `t, p, q, s, u`. It reaches `u` through `q` and `s`, three edges, although
  `p` reads `u` directly, two edges from `t`. Distance is therefore measured
  separately, by a breadth-first walk over the same edges, and the listing
  order is kept, so `depth` only removes steps and `t |> why |> depth(2)`
  keeps `u`.
  - Rejected: the depth of a step in the depth-first walk, which would make
    `u` three steps away in that example and depend on declaration order.
  - Rejected: reordering by distance, which would change the order of every
    step that `depth` keeps.
- **Where the walk began.** `why` includes its inputs, so they are at
  distance 0. `affects` never includes a node for the empty path, so the
  shortest path that counts is a non-empty one: every `affects` step is at
  distance 1 or more, and an input that another input reaches is at the
  length of that path. `depth(0)` after `affects` is therefore empty.
- **Steps without a walk.** A reference's one-step selection and `model` were
  not reached by any walk, so their steps are at distance 0 and `depth`
  keeps them all.
- **Filters keep distances.** `stated`, `derived` and `depth` keep the steps'
  distances, so they compose in either order:
  `price |> why |> depth(2) |> stated` and `price |> why |> stated |> depth(2)`
  select the same steps. A later walk measures from its own input, so
  `price |> why |> stated |> affects |> depth(1)` is what the stated facts
  behind the price touch directly.
- **A live limit.** `LIMIT` is an ordinary expression evaluated in place, so a
  declaration depends on what it reads, as an operand would. Its value must
  be a non-negative integer: steps are counted, so fractions, negatives, `NaN`
  and infinities have no meaning, and no value is coerced.
  - Rejected: treating infinity as "no limit". The full selection is the
    stage omitted, which is shorter.
- **An unknown limit is an unknown selection.** A `null` or undefined limit
  yields undefined. A declaration stores it as `null`, as the accepted rules
  store undefined values and declarations that read `null`, so
  `near = c |> why |> depth(plan.depth)` is `null` until the plan names a
  depth and then fills in. Quantifiers already treat a `null` or undefined
  collection the same way, and reads from the result give `null`.
  - Rejected: an empty selection, which would claim that nothing lies within
    the limit when the limit is not known.
  - Rejected: a `TypeError`, which would fail a standing declaration when it
    is stated instead of letting it wait for its limit.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `depth` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("depth is not an operation")`, as NUC 48 does for `stated` and
  `derived`, rather than ignoring the binding as `why` and `affects` do.
  - NUC 11's design states that keywords lose to variables and that a
    shadowed stage cannot be reached by any spelling. NUC 48 follows it for
    the newest stages; `why` and `affects` predate it and keep their accepted
    behavior.
  - `depth` is a common name in world models: the accepted specification
    already declares `depth = goldenRatio.value * altitude` and objects with a
    `depth` property. A model that defines its own `depth` function never has
    it silently replaced by the stage.
  - NUC 11 reads `a |> f(b)` as `f(a, b)`. If a later proposal adds that call
    form for user functions, a bound `depth` can become a call without
    changing a program that runs today; a stage that ignored bindings would
    have to change meaning.
  - The cost is that `depth = 2` followed by `c |> why |> depth(depth)` raises;
    the limit can be named anything else. A `depth` binding declared after a
    standing selection also makes its next refresh raise and roll back the
    statement that caused it, which the accepted state filters already do.
- **Observations as for filters.** `depth` keeps every value and structural
  observation its source selection made, including those of the steps it
  drops, as NUC 48's filters do. A selection whose dropped ancestor gains a
  new edge may gain a step, so it must refresh when that ancestor changes.
- **Programs without the stage are untouched.** Distances are computed only
  when a pipeline holds a depth stage. Every other pipeline is selected,
  walked and filtered exactly as before, so accepted programs gain no cost.
- **Not added.** NUC 11's other stages, its `depth(x, n)` call form, a prefix
  form, a step field that exposes the distance, distances along edges other
  than value dependencies, per-path enumeration, and an infinite limit.

## Specification

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> depth(LIMIT)` is a pipeline stage: `|>`, the identifier
  `depth`, then a `(` token, one expression, and `)`. Whitespace may separate
  `depth` from `(`; a line break may only where the lexer suppresses it,
  inside parentheses and brackets.
- `LIMIT` is parsed as a parenthesized expression is. `|> depth()` and
  `|> depth(1, 2)` report the errors that `()` and `(1, 2)` report.
- The stage takes part in the pipeline as the other stages do: it binds at
  the precedence of `|>`, may be followed by further stages, and may follow a
  prefix stage, so `why c |> depth(1)` is `(why c) |> depth(1)`.
- Everywhere else `depth` is an ordinary identifier, and `|> depth` not
  followed by `(` keeps the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("depth is not an operation")`. There is no prefix form, and
  `depth(c, 1)` is an ordinary call.
- The stage parses to the public variant
  `Expr::Depth { source: Box<Expr>, limit: Box<Expr> }`. The accepted
  `Expr::Reason` and the public `Stage` enum are unchanged.

### Selection and distance

- The stage evaluates its source selection, then its limit, and keeps the
  source's steps whose distance is at most the limit, in their order, with
  their `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields unchanged.
- A step's distance is the number of edges on the shortest path by which the
  most recent walking stage reached it from that stage's input selection.
  `why` follows each node's dependencies and `affects` its dependents, both
  skipping reasoning observers, exactly as the walks do. Edges count whether
  or not the nodes along them are selected: pending nodes, which no selection
  lists, are walked as before.
- `why` keeps its inputs at distance 0. Every step `affects` selects is at
  distance 1 or more, and an input that is also a dependent of another input
  is at the length of its shortest non-empty path.
- The steps of a reference's one-step selection and of `model` are at
  distance 0. `stated`, `derived` and `depth` keep the distances of the steps
  they keep. A later `why` or `affects` measures distances from its own input.

```
base = 1
seed = base + 1
step = seed + 1
next = step * 2
result = next + step
near = result |> why |> depth(1)
wide = result |> why |> depth(2)
assert(near.map(s -> s.node), ["result", "next", "step"])
assert(wide.map(s -> s.node), ["result", "next", "step", "seed"])
```

### Limits

- The limit is evaluated in place, in the scope of the pipeline, each time the
  selection is evaluated, after its source selection. Its reads are recorded
  as the declaration's dependencies, so a declaration follows its limit.
- A number that is a non-negative integer is the limit; `-0` is `0`, and a
  limit at least as large as every distance keeps every step.
- `null` and undefined make the reasoning expression undefined: the stages
  after it are not applied and their limits are not evaluated. A declaration
  stores the undefined value as `null` until the limit is known, a quantifier
  over it tests no member and yields undefined, and reads from it, such as
  `.length` and `.map(...)`, give `null` as reads from a `null` binding do.
- Every other value raises `TypeError("<limit> is not a non-negative integer")`,
  where `<limit>` is the limit's path or canonical source, named as the
  accepted `<collection> is not a collection` names a quantifier's
  collection, so `$Plan.depth` is named `Plan.depth`. This covers negative
  numbers, fractions, `NaN`, infinities, booleans, strings, lists, objects,
  instances, classes, functions, dates and regular expressions. The error is
  positioned at the running top-level statement and rolls back its
  transaction.

```
cause = 4
effect = cause * 2
class Plan
plan = Plan()
pending = effect |> why |> depth(plan.depth)
assert(pending, null)
plan.depth = 0
assert(pending.map(s -> s.node), ["effect"])
```

### Bindings and sources

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `depth` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("depth is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are all bindings.
- A binding declared after a standing selection makes that selection's next
  evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails and
  rolls back, exactly as the accepted state filters behave after
  `stated = 5`.
- The checks of a pipeline's stages run from the outermost stage inward, then
  the source is selected, then the stages apply from the innermost outward,
  each depth stage evaluating its limit as it applies. Accepted pipelines keep
  this order.
- An invalid source keeps its accepted diagnostic and is reported before the
  limit is evaluated: `1 |> depth(1)` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`
  and `missing |> depth(-1)` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and of
  `stated` and `derived`, which a binding shadows, is unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its source
  selection made, including those of the steps it drops, so a selection
  refreshes when a dropped ancestor's declaration, value or edges change, and
  when what its limit reads changes.
- An expression that contains the stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding it
  is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and that
  `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in place, and
  class-level rules give each instance its own selection.
- `.value` snapshots a selection as for the accepted stages. A `.value` read
  inside a limit is frozen when its declaration is stored, as a `.value`
  argument of a call is, so `near = c |> why |> depth(n.value)` keeps the
  limit `n` had then.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed statements
  behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores selections,
  observations and queued refreshes.
- Distances are computed only when a pipeline holds a depth stage, by one
  breadth-first walk per walking stage beside the accepted walk. Cost stays
  linear in the nodes and edges walked.

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>depth(LIMIT)` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions and `rule` text, with its source and its limit rendered once
  each, so rendered size stays linear: a chain of `|> depth(1)` stages, or of
  limits nested in limits, grows by exactly the ten characters of
  `|>depth(1)` per level.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, as
  `c|>why|>depth((n>0?1:0))` and `a|>affects|>depth(n+1)|>why|>depth(2)` do,
  except where the accepted renderer already abbreviates part of it: a member
  access on a pipeline renders without its parentheses, as `(why c).length`
  renders `c|>why.length`, and a function renders as its label, as
  `function()`.

| A source that reads an undefined name | `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`, as before |
| The limit raises an error or throws a value | That error or value, unchanged; the transaction rolls back |

- No error type is added. `depth is not an operation` is the accepted
  pipeline diagnostic, and `<limit> is not a non-negative integer` is the one
  new message.

## Reference Implementation

- `src/lang/ast/mod.rs`: the public variant
  `Expr::Depth { source: Box<Expr>, limit: Box<Expr> }`. `contains_reasoning`
  counts it as reasoning, and `Ast::convert` sends it to the existing
  `Ast::Reason` evaluator.
- `src/lang/ast/parser.rs`: `stage` takes the stage's source and returns the
  stage expression. A known stage name builds `Expr::Reason` as before; the
  name `depth` followed by a `(` token parses one expression with
  `expression` and expects `)`; every other name returns the accepted
  `<name> is not an operation` error without a position. `Stage::from_name`
  does not know `depth`, so prefix stages and every other use of the name are
  unchanged.
- `src/lang/ast/reason.rs`: the crate-private `Link`, a stage or a depth
  limit, and `pipeline`, which splits a pipeline into its links and its source
  with a loop that descends one link per iteration. `Runtime::reason` checks
  every link's binding from the outermost inward, with `check_operation` and
  `binds`, the accepted rule of `stated` and `derived`, then selects the
  source through `base_selection`, which is `model` or the accepted
  `selection_key`. Without a depth link it applies the accepted `apply_stage`
  to each stage from the innermost outward and computes no distances. With
  one, `ranked` carries distances and returns `None` for an unknown limit,
  which `Reason::resolve` turns into `Value::Undefined`. `depth_limit`
  evaluates the limit with `evaluate` and matches every `Value` variant, and
  `not_a_limit` names the limit with `Expr::describe`.
- `src/reasoning.rs`: the crate-private `Ranked` selection keeps each step's
  distance beside it through `unwalked`, `within`, `filtered`, `retained` and
  `into_selection`. `apply_ranked` filters, or walks with `walk`, which runs
  the accepted depth-first `traverse` for the steps and their order and
  `distances`, a breadth-first worklist over the same edges in which each node
  is queued at most once, for the shortest distances. `neighbors` gives both
  walks the same edges. Debug assertions check that steps and distances stay
  aligned and that every walked step has a distance.
- `src/number.rs`: `non_negative_integer` converts a limit, beside the
  existing conversions, with a unit test.
- `src/nuc/expression.rs`: `freeze_pipeline` returns a clone of a pipeline

  the stages around a cloned source and freezes only the depth limits.
- `src/lang/ast/generator.rs`: `Expr::Depth` renders as
  `SOURCE|>depth(LIMIT)`, and `groups_power_exponent` groups it as it groups
  `Expr::Reason`. The quantifier arm of `Display for Expr` moves unchanged
  into `write_quantified`, which keeps that function within the 60-line
  limit.
- `src/expression.rs`: `visit_children` visits the source and the limit, and

### Independent findings

- The semantics match the specification. The evaluator's probes covered
  shortest distances against the walk's listing order; `affects` distances,
  including an input that another input reaches only through a middle node,
  at distance 2; filters in either order and later walks that measure from
  their own input; limits of every value kind, including `-0`, `2.0` and
  `1e300`; `null` and undefined limits, after which later stages are not
  applied; observers, rollback, `suppose`, `.value`, class-level rules,
  methods and quantifiers; and shadowing by a variable, parameter, lambda
  parameter, loop variable, block local, function and class. For example,
  with `u = 1`, `s = u + 1`, `q = s * 2`, `p = u * 3` and `t = p + q`,
  `t |> why |> depth(2)` selects `t`, `p`, `q`, `s` and `u`, where the
  accepted revision raises `TypeError("depth is not an operation")` while
  parsing.
- A backtracking context that the preparation grid did not cover:
  `{node} = c |> why |> depth(1)` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` at
  line 3, column 10 at the accepted revision and now runs, returning `null`.
  It was an error, and the Compatibility section's "such as" covers it.
- The Compatibility tables give the positions of sources without a final
  line break. With one, `c |> why |> depth()` reports column 19 rather than
  20, and `c |> why |> depth(1` and `c |> why |> depth(` report line 4,
  column 1, as `x = ()` does at the accepted revision.
- Bindings: after `near = c |> why |> depth(1)` and `depth = 5`, the next
  statement that refreshes `near`, such as `a = 3`, fails with
  `TypeError("depth is not an operation")` at its own position and rolls
  back, as the Rationale states and as the accepted `stated` filter behaves;
  `delete depth` recovers.
- Rendering: each chained stage adds exactly ten characters and renderings
  round-trip, with one inherited abbreviation that the Rendering section
  does not list: `(c |> why |> depth(1))[0]` renders
  `c|>why|>depth(1)[0]`, which parses as a different program, as the
  accepted `(why c)[0]` renders `c|>why[0]`.

  about a quarter of a plain walk, and a stage that keeps every step adds
  about 5 to 15 percent. Refreshing a standing walk by reassigning a chain's
  head grows quadratically at both revisions, about 3.3 to 3.9 times per
  doubling, and a standing depth selection behaves the same.

  limits behind the existing guard, and has no panic path, comment or trait
