# NUC 88 -- Derivation Path Selections

```

Author: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>
Status: Accepted
Type: Standards Track
Category: Reasoning enhancement
Created: 07-Nov-2026
Requires: 8, 12, 45, 48, 57, 59, 62, 65, 82, 83
```

## Abstract

- Add the pipeline stage `SELECTION |> path(TARGET)`. It keeps the steps of
  `SELECTION` that lie on a dependency path between the walk that selected
  them and one target node, in their order and with every field unchanged, so
  `total |> why |> path(price)` is how the price reaches the total, and
  `price |> affects |> path(total)` is what the price moves on its way to the
  total.
- After `why`, a step is kept when it is the target or depends on the target
  transitively: exactly the steps whose node is the target's or one that
  `affects TARGET` selects. After `affects`, a step is kept when it is the
  target or the target depends on it transitively: exactly the steps whose
  node `why TARGET` selects. A selection that no walk produced, a reference's
  one-step selection and `model`, uses the direction of `why`.
- `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff` and `path` keep the direction and the
  steps' [NUC 82](nuc-0082.md) distances, so a path stage composes with each
  of them in either order. A target that no step reaches gives an empty
  selection.
- `TARGET` is one node, resolved as the source of a pipeline is. A target that
  is not a node of the model selects nothing and fills in when it is defined;
  every other argument keeps the diagnostics of a pipeline source, such as
  `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`.
- Only `|>` followed by `path` and a `(` token is the stage. `path` stays an
  ordinary name everywhere else, and a binding named `path` shadows the stage
  as bindings shadow [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md)'s `stated` and `derived`. The
  selection stays live through its source's observations, the structure
  between its steps and the target, and the target itself, and renders as
  `SOURCE|>path(TARGET)`.
- No keyword, token or error type is added. Every accepted program keeps its
  parse and behavior; only inputs that were errors at the accepted revision
  change. The public `Expr` enum gains `Expr::Path`, which needs human SemVer
  review. No project version is changed. The authoritative contract is
  [Derivation path selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#derivation-path-selections).
- **Outcome: Accepted.** The independent evaluation and the caller's
  integrated-tree checks passed all three hard criteria, and the independent
  evaluator decided Accepted. The required token comparison ran in
  continuous mode: 13 cases use fewer tokens, 997 the same and none more. No
  policy check failed.

## Motivation

- A world model is read through its explanations, and an LLM that maintains
  one asks "how does X depend on Y?" as often as "why X?". The answer is the
  causal chain between two named nodes, not every ancestor: with
  `subtotal = price * qty` and `total = subtotal + shipping`, `why total`
  lists five steps, and how the price reaches the total is three of them.
- [NUC 82](nuc-0082.md) sizes an explanation by distance, which keeps what is
  near and drops what is far whether or not it leads to the node the question
  names. No accepted stage keeps a chain to a named node.
- Accepted Nucleoid can only spell the chain with a membership test against
  the opposite walk. A lambda cannot read an enclosing lambda's parameter, so
  `[1, 2].map(a -> [3].map(b -> a + b))` raises
  `ReferenceError("a is not defined")`, and the test needs a quantifier:
  `(why total).filter(s -> s.node == "price" || any d in affects price: d.node == s.node)`,
  21 lexer tokens more than `total |> why |> path(price)`. That spelling:
  - repeats the target as a string beside the reference, and a string that no
    longer matches silently drops the target's own step;
  - walks `affects price` once for every step of the explanation, so one
    question takes time quadratic in the explanation: 21 seconds for a chain
    of 1,600 nodes on the accepted release build, where the stage takes 7 ms;
  - needs each instance's node name in a class-level rule, built from
    `$Item.id` or matched by suffix;
  - cannot run in a method, where a lambda cannot read `this`:
    `[1, 2].filter(v -> any w in [this.n]: w == v)` raises
    `ReferenceError("this is not defined")`, and assignments in a method body
    declare state variables, so a method that asks needs model-changing
    helpers.
- In the 997 cases of the accepted executable corpus, the specification, the
  executable reference and the `nuc` blocks of the README and the examples, no
  case narrows an explanation or an impact to a target. 13 lookups in 7 cases
  ask by node name whether a selection holds a node or which step it is, such
  as `trace.some(step => step.node == "pro.rate")`,
  `inventory.find(step => step.node == "trigger")` and
  `any s in why c: s.node == "d"`: 8 in the specification and 5 in the
  executable reference. The 1,341 synthesized records hold 2 more in 1
  record, among 21 records that use `why` or `affects`. Each asks whether or
  where one node appears in a selection, part of a question whose full answer
  is the chain.
- Prior art: Bazel's `allpaths(S, E)` and `somepath(S, E)` queries over a
  build graph, Cypher's variable-length path patterns
  `(a)-[*]->(b)`, SPARQL 1.1 property paths such as `?x :dependsOn+ ?y`,
  spreadsheet formula auditing that traces precedents and dependents, and
  the why-provenance of database query results. This NUC keeps every node on
  any path, as `allpaths` does, in the walk's order.
- Draft [NUC 11](nuc-0011.md) plans the stages `why`, `why not`, `affects`,
  `rules`, `conflicts`, `describe`, `export` and `depth(n)`; none narrows a
  selection to a target. This NUC implements none of them.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The caller made one tool-backed uniform draw
  over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks eligible for automatic
  selection, at 2026-11-07T03:23:06-05:00 with PowerShell's
  `Get-Random -Minimum 0 -Maximum 5`. It drew index 3, Reasoning enhancement,
  with probability 1/5 and no redraw, in continuous mode, so token comparison
  is required. Runtime applicability does not apply to this category. Within
  it the caller chose this stage: a graph query in the category's own terms,
  bounded, and a parse error today that no test pins.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Other candidates in this category.** Before this one the caller set
  aside:
  - a `none` quantifier, which saves one token over `all ...: !cond`;
  - NUC 11's `conflicts` and `rules`, which have no fair accepted counterpart
    because class-level rules cannot be enumerated in accepted Nucleoid;
  - `why not`, because the prefix `why not x` is an accepted parse of
    `why (not x)`;
  - NUC 11's `export`, which is too large for one revision.
- **Only a former error.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("path is not an operation")` for `path`, and only that parser
  consumes `|>`. No test pins that message. Giving `|> path(` a meaning
  therefore changes no program that the accepted parser accepts.
- **A stage, not a function or a prefix.** `path` narrows a selection, as
  `stated`, `derived`, `depth` and `diff` do, so it is a pipeline stage. Its
  argument is written as [NUC 82](nuc-0082.md) writes `depth(LIMIT)`.
  Rejected:
  - a prefix form `path c`, which would need a second operand and is two
    juxtaposed statements today;
  - a call form `path(x, y)`, which is an ordinary call today and stays one;
    a call cannot tell which node produced its first argument, and NUC 11
    reads `a |> f(b)` as `f(a, b)`, so a bound `path` may later become that
    call;
  - a bare `|> path` with the target elsewhere, which keeps its accepted
    error.
- **The direction of the walk.** The most recent `why` or `affects` decides
  which way a path runs: an explanation keeps what depends on the target, an
  impact what the target depends on. Both are the definitional composition
  with the opposite walk, so `X |> why |> path(T)` keeps exactly the steps of
  `why X` that are `T` or that `affects T` selects, and
  `X |> affects |> path(T)` exactly the steps of `affects X` that `why T`
  selects.
  - A reference's one-step selection and `model` were reached by no walk, and
    NUC 82 puts their steps at distance 0, as `why` puts its inputs. They
    take the direction of `why`, so `price |> path(price)` keeps the price,
    and `model |> path(price)` is the price and everything that rests on it.
  - Rejected: a fixed direction, which would make
    `price |> affects |> path(total)` keep nothing.
  - Rejected: both directions at once, which would keep a step that depends
    on the target and one the target depends on, in a selection that only
    walked one way.
- **Reachability over the whole graph.** A step is kept by what the graph
  says about it, not by which other steps survived earlier stages, so a path
  that runs through a step an earlier filter dropped still counts, and the
  stage commutes with every filter:
  `x |> why |> path(y) |> stated` and `x |> why |> stated |> path(y)` select
  the same steps. Edges are the walks' edges, and reasoning observers are
  never on a path, as they are never walked.
- **Order, fields and distances kept.** The stage only removes steps, so the
  walk's order and every field stay, including a kept step's `from`, which
  may name a dropped step. It keeps NUC 82's distances, so
  `x |> why |> path(y) |> depth(1)` and `x |> why |> depth(1) |> path(y)`
  select the same steps.
- **One target, a node.** The argument is a reference, resolved as a
  pipeline's source is, because a path ends at a node, not at a value.
  - Rejected: a selection or a pipeline as the target, such as
    `path(price |> why)`, which would ask about several targets at once. It
    raises `TypeError("Cannot explain price|>why")`, the error of a source
    that is not a reference, so a later proposal can give it a meaning
    without changing a program that runs.
  - Rejected: a string naming the node, such as `path("price")`, which would
    make the same argument a value in one stage and a node in every other.
    It raises `TypeError("Cannot explain \"price\"")`.
  - Rejected: no argument or two, which raise the `SyntaxError`s of `()` and
    `(a, b)`, as `depth()` and `depth(1, 2)` do.
- **A target that is not in the model.** An absent property, a pending
  placeholder that rules still read after its name was deleted, and a
  reasoning observer select nothing, because `why` and `affects` of such a
  node are empty at the accepted revision. The stage observes the target, so
  the selection fills in when it is defined, as an explanation of an absent
  property does.
  - Rejected: counting a pending placeholder as reachable, which would keep
    the readers of a deleted name, while `affects` of that name keeps none.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `path` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("path is not an operation")`, as NUC 48, NUC 82 and
  [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md) do for their stages. NUC 11 states that keywords lose
  to variables, and a model that defines its own `path`, as 4 of the 1,341
  synthesized records do for a URL or a route, never has it silently replaced
  by the stage. Methods, properties and object keys named `path` are not
  bindings.
- **Observations.** The stage keeps every observation its source made,
  including those of the steps it drops, as the filters do. Whether a step
  reaches the target depends on edges its source need not have walked, such
  as the ancestors of a reference, so the stage also subscribes to the
  structure of every node it walks and to the target. A standing selection
  therefore refreshes when a rule is rewired so that a path appears or
  disappears, and when the target is declared, replaced or deleted.
- **Walking from the steps.** The stage walks from the selection's steps in
  the walk direction and then back from the target over the edges it found.
  That region is the closure of the steps, which lies within the region the
  source's own walk covered when one produced it, so the cost stays within
  the explanation's closure and a standing selection observes no node
  outside it.
  - Rejected: walking from the target in the opposite direction, which costs
    the target's whole downstream, often much larger than the explanation,
    and would refresh the selection on every structural change anywhere
    below the target.
- **Naming.** `path` is the word that graph query languages use, Bazel's
  `allpaths`, Cypher's paths and SPARQL's property paths, and it reads in
  both directions: the path from the total to the price, and the price's
  path to the total. Rejected:
  - `between`, which suggests two arguments and a symmetric relation, and
    hints that the endpoints are left out;
  - `via` and `through`, which name a node a path passes through, the
    opposite of an endpoint, and are better kept for that question;
  - `to` and `toward`, which read well only after `affects`;
  - `trace` and `chain`: `trace` is the most common name for an explanation
    in the corpus, assigned 27 times in the 997 cases, so a binding would
    shadow it constantly, and `chain` is NUC 11's word for a pipeline.
- **Programs without the stage are untouched.** Parsing changes only after
  `|>` followed by `path` and `(`, and pipelines without a path stage are
  selected, walked and filtered exactly as before.
- **Not added.** A selection or several targets, a string target, shortest
  paths only, an edge list or path length in a step, excluding the
  endpoints, a prefix form, a call form, and NUC 11's other stages.

## Specification

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> path(TARGET)` is a pipeline stage: `|>`, the identifier
  `path`, then a `(` token, one expression and `)`. Whitespace may separate
  `path` from `(`; a line break may only where the lexer suppresses it,
  inside parentheses and brackets.
- `TARGET` is parsed as a parenthesized expression is. `|> path()` and
  `|> path(a, b)` report the errors that `()` and `(a, b)` report.
- The stage takes part in the pipeline as the other stages do: it binds at
  the precedence of `|>`, may be followed by further stages, and may follow a
  prefix stage, so `why total |> path(price)` is `(why total) |> path(price)`.
- Everywhere else `path` is an ordinary identifier, and `|> path` not
  followed by `(` keeps the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("path is not an operation")`. There is no prefix form, and
  `path(total, price)` is an ordinary call.
- The stage parses to the public variant
  `Expr::Path { source: Box<Expr>, target: Box<Expr> }`. The accepted
  `Expr::Reason`, `Expr::Depth` and the public `Stage` enum are unchanged.

### Selection

- The stage evaluates its source selection, then resolves its target, and
  keeps the source's steps from which the target is reachable, in their
  order, with their `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields and
  their distances unchanged.
- A step reaches the target when it is the target, or when a path of one or
  more edges leads from it to the target in the direction of the most recent
  walking stage: along dependencies after `why`, along dependents after
  `affects`, and along dependencies when no walk produced the selection.
  Paths pass through no reasoning observer, as the walks do, and count
  whether or not the nodes along them are selected.
- So after `why` the kept steps are those whose node is the target's or one
  that `affects TARGET` selects, and after `affects` those whose node
  `why TARGET` selects.
- `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff` and `path` keep the direction; a later
  `why` or `affects` sets its own. A reference's one-step selection and
  `model` take the direction of `why`, so `price |> path(price)` keeps the
  price and `model |> path(price)` is the price and every node that rests on
  it, in model order.
- With the walk's own input as the target, `c |> why |> path(c)` keeps only
  the step of `c`, and `a |> affects |> path(a)` keeps nothing, as `affects`
  never selects its input. A target that no step reaches gives an empty
  selection.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
shipping = 5
subtotal = price * qty
total = subtotal + shipping
link = total |> why |> path(price)
reach = price |> affects |> path(total)
assert(link.map(s -> s.node), ["total", "subtotal", "price"])
assert(reach.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "total"])
assert(link[0].from, ["subtotal", "shipping"])
```

### Target

- `TARGET` names one node and is resolved as the source of a pipeline is,
  when the stage applies, after the source selection and the stages before
  it: a name, a property path such as `order.price`, `this.price` in a
  method, or `$Class.property` inside a class-level rule, which names the
  instance's property.
- A target that is not a node of the model selects nothing: an absent
  property, a pending placeholder that rules still read after its name was
  deleted, and a reasoning observer. The selection fills in when the target
  is defined.
- A literal, a property path whose base holds no object, and any other
  expression that is not a reference raise
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <target>")`, where `<target>` is the target's
  canonical source: `path(1)`, `path("price")`, `path(price + 1)`,
  `path(price.x)`, `path(this)`, `path(price |> why)` and `$Class.property`
  outside a class-level rule.
- A name that nothing defines raises `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`,
  including `path(model)` when no node is named `model`, and the first name
  of a property path, as `path(ghost.x)` does.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Bindings and sources

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `path` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("path is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are all bindings; methods,
  properties and object keys are not.
- A binding declared after a standing selection makes that selection's next
  evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails and
  rolls back, as for the accepted filters.
- The checks of a pipeline's stages run from the outermost stage inward, then
  the source is selected, then the stages apply from the innermost outward,
  each path stage resolving its target as it applies. Accepted pipelines keep
  this order.
- An invalid source keeps its accepted diagnostic and is reported before the
  target is resolved: `1 |> path(price)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")` and `missing |> path(ghost)` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and of
  `stated`, `derived`, `depth` and `diff`, which a binding shadows, is
  unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its source made,
  including those of the steps it drops.
- It also observes the structure of every node it walks while deciding which
  steps reach the target, and of the target, so a standing selection
  refreshes when an edge on or toward a path appears or disappears, and when
  the target is declared, replaced or deleted, whether or not the target was
  reachable before.
- An expression that contains the stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding it
  is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and that
  `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in place, and
  class-level rules give each instance its own selection.
- `.value` snapshots a selection as for the accepted stages. The target is a
  node, not a value, so it is not frozen when a declaration is stored, as the
  operand of `why` is not.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed statements
  behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores selections,
  observations and queued refreshes.

```
base = 2
rate = base * 3
fee = 4
cost = rate + fee
via = cost |> why |> path(base)
assert(via.map(s -> s.node), ["cost", "rate", "base"])
rate = 6
assert(via, [])
fee = base + 1
assert(via.map(s -> s.node), ["cost", "fee", "base"])
delete base
assert([via, fee], [[], null])
base = 5
assert(via.map(s -> s.holds), [12, 6, 5])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>path(TARGET)` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions and `rule` text, with its source and its target rendered once
  each, so rendered size stays linear: each chained `|> path(a)` adds exactly
  the nine characters of `|>path(a)`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, as
  `total|>why|>path(price)|>derived` does, except where the accepted renderer
  already abbreviates part of it: a member or index access on a pipeline
  renders without its parentheses, as `(why c).length` renders
  `c|>why.length`, and a function renders as its label.

### Cost

- Pipelines without a path stage take their accepted paths and do no work for
  it.
- Each evaluation of the stage subscribes to its target and, when the target
  is a node of the model, walks once from the steps of its source over the
  nodes they reach in the walk direction, each node queued once, recording
  the edges it crosses, and then once back from the target over those edges.
  Time and memory are linear in the nodes and edges of that closure. When a
  walk produced the source, the closure lies within the region that walk
  covered, so the stage visits each of its nodes and edges at most once more.
  It never walks the target's closure in the opposite direction.
- Measured in process on release builds of the candidate and of the accepted
  revision, run one after the other, timing only the measured statement after
  building its graph, best of three runs, except the accepted counterpart,
  which ran once:
  - one question over a chain or a two-link lattice grows 1.8 to 2.3 times
    per doubling from 400 to 12,800 nodes: at 12,800,
    `last |> why |> path(v0)` takes 74 ms on the chain and 78 ms on the
    lattice, `v0 |> affects |> path(last)` 79 ms, and `(why last).length` 61
    and 62 ms on the candidate and 66 and 68 ms on the accepted build;
  - the accepted counterpart grows 4.1 to 5.1 times per doubling and takes

  `Expr::Path { source: Box<Expr>, target: Box<Expr> }`. `contains_reasoning`

  `Ast::Reason` evaluator.
