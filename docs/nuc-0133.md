## Abstract

- A reasoning pipeline gains the final stage `SELECTION |> edges`. It lists
  the dependency edges that the steps of `SELECTION` record: for each step,
  in the selection's order, and for each name in its `from` list, in that
  order, the two-item list `[INPUT, NODE]` of the input's name and the
  step's node name. After `price = 10`, `quantity = 3`, `rate = 0.2`,
  `subtotal = price * quantity`, `tax = subtotal * rate` and
  `total = subtotal + tax`, `total |> why |> edges` gives
  `[["subtotal", "total"], ["tax", "total"], ["price", "subtotal"], ["quantity", "subtotal"], ["subtotal", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"]]`.
- It joins [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s `|> nodes` and
  [NUC 121](nuc-0121.md)'s `|> holds` as a projection of the step records,
  this time of the `from` field that both left out, paired with each
  step's `node`. It gives exactly what the accepted
  `(SELECTION).flatMap(step => step.from.reduce((pairs, input) => pairs.concat([[input, pairs[0]]]), [step.node])[1:])`
  gives, where `SELECTION` is read as the accepted stages read their
  source, so a reference alone denotes its own one-step selection and
  `total |> edges` lists the two edges into the total.
- The stage lists every input of each step it keeps, whether the selection
  keeps that input's own step or not, and a stated step has no inputs, so
  `total |> why |> stated |> edges` is `[]`. A declaration that holds the
  stage is a reasoning observer and follows the graph: redefining `tax` as
  `price * rate + fee` drops the edge from the subtotal into the tax and
  adds those from the price and the fee, while a change of a value alone
  leaves the edges as they are.
- The stage ends the pipeline, as `nodes` and `holds` do:
  `total |> why |> edges |> why` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain total|>why|>edges")`. It has no prefix form,
  `edges` stays an ordinary name everywhere else, and a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `edges` makes the pipeline raise
  `TypeError("edges is not an operation")` at run time, as bindings shadow
  the other stages.
- No keyword, lexer token, error type or message is added. Every accepted
  program keeps its parse and behavior. Only programs that were
  whole-program parse errors at the accepted revision change. The ones
  measured raised the parse-time `TypeError("edges is not an operation")`;
  where the statement parser backtracks from an unpacking or
  object-destructuring pattern, `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` or
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` first; or, in a program that also holds a
  form that only a later pass of the parser reads, that pass's first error.
  The list of messages is the measured one, not a proof that no other
  message occurs.
- The public `Stage` enum gains the variant `Stage::Edges`, after
  `Stage::Holds`, which needs human SemVer review; standing there, it also
  moves the implicit discriminants of `Stage::Origins` and `Stage::Outcomes`
  from 7 and 8 to 8 and 9. No project version is changed. The authoritative
  contract is
  [Edge lists of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#edge-lists-of-reasoning-selections).

## Motivation

- A world model that explains a conclusion often needs the structure of the
  explanation, not only its nodes and values: which inputs feed which
  results, to render the explanation as a graph, to check which rule reads
  which fact, or to hand the graph to another tool. The step records hold
  it in their `from` fields, and no stage projects it. `nodes` names the
  steps and `holds` gives their values; neither says which step reads which.
- Accepted programs cannot spell the edge list directly. The natural
  `(total |> why).flatMap(s -> s.from.map(f -> [f, s.node]))` raises
  `ReferenceError("s is not defined")`, because a callback cannot read the
  parameter of the callback around it, and so do
  `(total |> why).flatMap(s -> [[f, s.node] for f in s.from])` and
  `(total |> why).flatMap(s -> s.from.zip(s.from.map(f -> s)))`.
  Comprehensions take one `for` clause, so
  `[[f, s.node] for s in why total for f in s.from]` raises
  `SyntaxError("Expected ']' but found keyword 'for'")`, and
  `[[f, s.node] for s in why total]` raises
  `ReferenceError("f is not defined")`. Lists have no repetition to pair
  each input with a copy of its node: `[1] * 3` gives `3`, `repeat` and
  `fill` are not list methods, and `Array(3)` gives `[3]`. A loop cannot
  collect the pairs either: a `for` loop runs its body only for the
  instances of declared classes in what it iterates, as
  `Nucleoid loops through only defined objects in a for of statement`
  specifies, and a step record is an anonymous object, so
  `for s of why total:` runs no iteration, and a function that pushes the
  pairs in such loops returns `[]`. A loop would not follow a rewiring
  anyway.
- What works today is a detour over strings that pairs each input with
  copies of the node's name,
  `(why total).flatMap(s -> s.from.zip((s.node + "|").repeat(s.from.length).split("|")))`,
  which holds only while no selected node name contains the separator, or
  a general spelling that carries the node in the accumulator of a
  `reduce`, whose initial value the outer callback evaluates,
  `(why total).flatMap(s -> s.from.reduce((acc, f) -> acc.concat([[f, acc[0]]]), [s.node])[1:])`.
  Node names can hold the separators: an `if` block's step is named after
  its condition, such as `if(b.w>0)`, so a condition with `||`, or a string
  with a space, breaks the detour for that separator. The per-step
  adjacency `(total |> why).map(s -> [s.node, s.from])` is another shape.
- The detour is 38 lexer tokens and the `reduce` spelling 48, against 4 for
  `why total |> edges` and 5 for `total |> why |> edges`.
- Both spellings build a record object for every step and then read two
  fields of each. Inside a declaration, each read of a fresh record's
  `from` and `node` fields adds a pending graph node: with `a = 1`,
  `b = a + 2` and `c = b * 2`, after
  `wiring = (why c).flatMap(s -> s.from.zip((s.node + "|").repeat(s.from.length).split("|")))`
  and two writes of `a`, `--graph` lists 22 nodes on the accepted release
  binary, the four of the program and the 18 pending nodes `obj:0.from` to
  `obj:8.node`, of which only the last six are still read; the `reduce`
  spelling adds the same 18. A standing declaration so grows the graph by
  two nodes for every step each time it is evaluated, where the same
  declaration over `c |> why |> nodes` keeps four nodes.
- At the accepted revision, `total |> why |> edges` raises
  `TypeError("edges is not an operation")` while the program is parsed, so
  no statement of the program runs.
- A stage keeps the question inside the pipeline that asks it, so a world
  model reads its explanations as the rest of its reasoning reads, left to
  right: `alarm |> why |> edges` gives the wiring behind an alarm, and
  `delay |> affects |> edges` the edges a supplier's delay travels along.
- Prior art: the edge lists of graph libraries, which give a directed edge
  as the pair of its tail and head, as NetworkX's `G.edges()`, igraph's
  `get_edgelist()` and Mathematica's `EdgeList` do; and the accepted stages
  of NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 88, NUC 106, NUC 112, NUC 120, NUC 121 and
  NUC 128, which shape and project selections inside the same pipeline.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `008cc5b3` on 22-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform draw
  at 00:35:33 over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks eligible
  for automatic selection and selected Reasoning enhancement, index 3, with
  probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the caller chose one bounded
  enhancement that no NUC proposes: the dependency edges that a selection's
  steps record, the third projection of the step records after NUC 112's
  `nodes` and NUC 121's `holds`. [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md) left "an edge list"
  out, and NUC 112 and NUC 121 left the projection of the `from` field out.
  The caller ruled out `|> rules`, whose name [NUC 11](nuc-0011.md)'s plan
  already uses for another stage and whose content is NUC 11's planned
  `export`, and `|> state`, which `stated` and `derived` already answer.
  Token comparison is required; runtime applicability does not apply to
  this category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("edges is not an operation")` for `edges`, whatever follows it,
  and only that parser consumes `|>`. Giving `|> edges` a meaning therefore
  changes only inputs that were parse errors. Where the statement parser
  backtracks, some of those inputs reported another parse error first:
  `x, y = c |> why |> edges` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` and
  `{length} = c |> why |> edges` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as
  for NUC 88's `path`, NUC 106's set stages, NUC 112's `nodes`, NUC 120's
  `union` and NUC 121's `holds`. A program that also holds a form that only
  a later pass of the accepted parser reads, such as a receiver dot, an
  operator section of [NUC 109](nuc-0109.md) or a Python-style `self`
  parameter of [NUC 107](nuc-0107.md), reports that pass's first error
  instead, and is still a whole-program parse error. Inside a template
  interpolation the accepted parser ignores trailing tokens, so
  `` `v ${c why c |> edges}` `` keeps its accepted result.
- **A final stage, not a member, call or prefix.** The edges are the last
  question asked of a selection, so they end the pipeline that asks it.
  Rejected:
  - a member `SELECTION.edges`: `(c |> why).edges` reads a missing property
    of a list today and gives undefined, which would change;
  - a call `edges(SELECTION)`, which is an ordinary call today, answered by
    a function named `edges` or raising `ReferenceError("edges is not defined")`;
  - a prefix form `edges c`, which is two juxtaposed statements today;
  - a general projection or flattening stage such as `|> pluck(from)` or
    `|> flatMap(f)`, which the request does not ask for and which cannot
    pair an input with its node any better than `.flatMap` does.
- **Pairs from input to node.** An edge is the list `[INPUT, NODE]`, in the
  direction in which a value flows, from the node read to the node that
  reads it, as a step's `from` field names its inputs and as graph libraries
  give an edge as its tail and its head. Rejected:
  - `[NODE, INPUT]`, which reads against the flow and against `from`;
  - an object such as `{from: INPUT, to: NODE}`, which would create an
    anonymous object for every edge, renumbering every later anonymous
    object, and which a program spells no more clearly than `edge[0]`;
  - the per-step adjacency `[NODE, FROM]`, which the accepted
    `(SELECTION).map(step => [step.node, step.from])` already spells.
- **It ends the pipeline, as `nodes` and `holds` do.** Its result is a list
  of pairs, as the accepted spelling's is, so whatever follows it reads that
  list as whatever follows the accepted spelling does. Every NUC 11
  operation and every accepted stage but `nodes` and `holds` returns a
  selection; this one does not, so a stage after it has a source that is
  neither a reference nor a selection and raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, and an `intersect`, `except`,
  `union` or `path` argument that ends with it raises the same. Rejected:
  reading the pairs as references again, which no accepted spelling does.
- **Every input of a kept step.** The stage projects the records of the
  steps the selection keeps, as `nodes` and `holds` project them, and a
  step's record lists every input of the step in its `from` field, which no
  stage rewrites: NUC 88 keeps "a kept step's `from`" as it is. So the
  stage lists an edge whose input the selection does not keep:
  `total |> why |> depth(1) |> edges` lists the edges from the price, the
  quantity and the rate, which lie two steps away, and
  `total |> why |> except(subtotal) |> edges` keeps the edges from the
  subtotal. Such an edge shows where the selection's part of the graph
  meets the rest, which a world model needs to know when it asks what a
  bounded explanation leaves out. A program that wants only the edges
  between kept steps filters by their names, as in
  `(S |> edges).filter(edge => kept.includes(edge[0]))` after
  `kept = S |> nodes`. Rejected: dropping the edges whose input is outside
  the selection, which would make the stage a second notion of a
  selection's graph that no accepted spelling of the records gives, and
  which the filter above already spells.
- **The accepted spelling by construction.** The stage selects exactly as
  the pipeline before it selects, builds the step records that the accepted
  spelling builds, and gives, for each record in order, a pair for each
  name of its `from` field in order, with the record's `node` field. It
  walks nothing a second time.
- **Names as the records hold them.** Each input and node is the string the
  record holds: an instance property as `o.total`, a property of an
  anonymous object as `obj:0.gone`, the step of an `if` block as
  `if(b.w>0)`. An input that a step reads twice appears once, as in its
  `from` field, and an input may be a node that no selection selects, a
  pending property or a reasoning observer, as the field names it.
  Rejected: resolving, renaming or filtering the names, which no accepted
  spelling does.
- **The records, without their reads.** Building the records keeps the
  numbering of anonymous objects as it is under the accepted spelling, and
  programs observe that numbering: after `a = 1`, `b = a + 1`, `c = b * 2`,
  `d = a + 3`, `e = 7` and `f = e + d`, the accepted
  `x = (c |> why).flatMap(...)` makes the later object literal
  `p = {k: 1}` `obj:3`, so `String(p)` is `"[object obj:3]"`. Rejected:
  giving the pairs from the steps without building records, which would
  change that numbering in every program that creates anonymous objects
  after the stage. The stage does not record its reads of the records'
  `from` and `node` fields, which no program can write because no program
  holds the records, so a declaration that holds it adds none of the
  pending graph nodes that the accepted spellings add on each evaluation.
  Those nodes show only in `--graph`.
- **No null rule to keep.** A stored declaration whose evaluation reads a
  `null` binding or property settles to `null`, and NUC 121's stage keeps
  that rule for `holds` values, which can be `null`. The `node` field of a
  record is always the step's node name and its `from` field always the
  list of the names of its inputs, both taken from the graph, never from a
  value, so neither the null rule nor NUC 121's undefined-step limitation,
  which concerns an undefined value that an assignment used as an
  expression stores in a step's `holds`, can reach them, and the accepted
  spellings' declarations never settle to `null` through them. Every pair
  holds two strings, so the stage gives no `null` or undefined item and
  needs no note of one.
- **Undefined selections stay undefined.** A `depth` stage with a `null`
  limit makes a selection undefined ([NUC 82](nuc-0082.md)), and the
  accepted spelling's `.flatMap` on it gives undefined, so the stage gives
  undefined, which a declaration stores as `null`.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `edges` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("edges is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md),
  NUC 82, [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md), NUC 88, [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md), NUC 112,
  [NUC 120](nuc-0120.md), NUC 121 and [NUC 128](nuc-0128.md) do for their
  stages. A model that defines its own `edges` never has it silently
  replaced by a stage. The check runs with the pipeline's other checks,
  before the source is selected. At the accepted revision these programs
  raised the same message while parsing; under this NUC the statements
  before the pipeline run first, and a `try` can catch it.
- **One path for the three final stages.** NUC 112 gives `nodes` a path of
  its own, and NUC 121 generalizes it to `holds` through the crate-private,
  closed `StepField`, of which each projection gives one value per step.
  `edges` gives one item per input instead, so this NUC generalizes the
  projection itself: `Stage::projection` returns a crate-private, closed
  `Projection`, either `Projection::Field(StepField)` for `nodes` and
  `holds` or `Projection::Edges`, matched by name and statically
  dispatched. `Runtime::projected` sizes its list once from the selection,
  one item per step for a field and one per input for the edges, builds
  each record as before, and adds the step's items: the field's value,
  noted for the null rule as before, or the step's pairs, built by the
  helpers that also write the record's `from` and `node` fields, so the
  pairs equal the record's fields by construction. The binding check, the
  selection, the undefined selection, the end of the pipeline, the early
  return of `Reason::resolve`, `Runtime::projection_of`, the unrecorded
  reads and the freeze paths stay one path for all three stages. Rejected:
  a copy of the path for `edges`, which would let the final stages drift
  apart; a third `StepField` whose value is a list per step that
  `projected` would flatten, which would no longer be a field of the
  record; and a function pointer or trait object, which the Power of 10
  adaptation forbids. NUC 112's and NUC 121's behavior, the null rule
  included, is unchanged.
- **Naming.** `edges` is the name graph theory and graph libraries give the
  list a graph's edges form, and with `nodes` it names the two halves of a
  graph: `S |> nodes` and `S |> edges` are the vertices and the edges of a
  selection's part of the dependency graph. At the accepted revision
  `edges` occurs in no program of the four executable documents, the
  synthesized records or the ARC instruction datasets: only in prose, 13
  times in `nucleoid.spec.md` and four times in `docs/README.md`, in the
  description of the export record `spec-0424` and of the synthesized record
  `synth-21-0120`, and in two comments of an ARC instruction dataset, so no
  binding shadows the stage. Rejected:
  - `from`, the field the stage reads, whose projection would be the
    per-step input lists, another shape;
  - `links`, `arcs` and `wiring`, which say the same less precisely, and
    `wiring` reads as a variable name that the examples use;
  - `dependencies`, which reads as the inputs of one node, and `graph`,
    which reads as nodes and edges together.
- **Requires.** NUC 112, whose final-stage contract and path the stage
  shares, and NUC 121, which generalized that path to a second projection.
  Those already require everything else it relies on: the stages it ends,
  among them NUC 48's, NUC 82's, NUC 83's, NUC 88's, NUC 106's and
  NUC 120's, and pipelines, `why`, `affects` and `model`. NUC 128's
  `origins` and `outcomes`, which a pipeline may hold before the stage, are
  accepted and need no dependency of their own.
- **Not added.** A prefix form, a stage that keeps the selection, edges
  restricted to the selection, edge objects, projections of the other step
  fields `rule` and `state`, a general projection stage, and any change to
  the accepted stages, `nodes` and `holds` included.

### Counterparts

- Every new case is paired with the most compact behavior-equivalent
  accepted program found in the general family below: the same document,
  title, canonical assertions in the same order and expected return,
  executed by the accepted runtime with every assertion running. Each
  counterpart differs from its case only where an `edges` stage is written,
  and, where that is shorter, in one added line that defines a helper,
  right after the title in the specification and the reference and first
  in the two snippets:

```
e = sel -> sel.flatMap(s -> s.from.zip((s.node + "|").repeat(s.from.length).split("|")))
```

- Every case writes its stages in declarations or in its last expression,
  never inside an `assert`, so the canonical assertions of a case and its
  counterpart are the same. Every case rewires a declaration, adds a node
  or removes one after its stages are declared, and the edges of every
  stage change after it is declared and are asserted or returned, so a
  counterpart that wrote the pairs into its code fails: the hard-coded
  column below.
- The counterparts write `SELECTION |> edges` as the detour over
  `(SELECTION)`, or as `e(SELECTION)` with the helper, with `SELECTION` in
  its most compact accepted spelling: the prefix stages `why x` and
  `affects x` where a case writes `x |> why` and `x |> affects`, and
  `model |> why` for `model`, which has no prefix
  (`(model).flatMap(...)` raises `ReferenceError("model is not defined")`)
  and is not a value.
- `model |> why` selects the model's own steps, in model order, as NUC 112's
  and NUC 121's model counterparts relied on. Measured on the accepted
  release binary in every state of
  `Nucleoid lists the edges that a change travels and the model with edges`,
  with the helper and without it,
  `(model |> why).map(step => step.node)` lists exactly what
  `(model |> depth(0)).map(step => step.node)` lists. The helper `e` is a
  node of that model, but its step has no inputs and adds no edge, so the
  helper pairs the model case, where NUC 121's helper could not.
- The detour is fair for a case when no node name of the case holds its
  separator. No node name of the seven cases holds `|` or a space: their
  nodes are variables and instance properties, whose names cannot hold
  either character, and no case holds an `if` block, whose step is named
  after its condition. A space separator, with `split(" ")`, counts and
  passes as `|` does; `split()` without an argument does not split.
- The failing redefinition `wiring = 1 |> edges` of
  `Nucleoid rolls back failed updates through edge lists` is written
  `wiring = 1 |> why`, the shortest accepted statement that raises the same
  error at the same place: inside the case's `try`, each leaves `wiring`
  unchanged with the caught `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`. Spelled out as
  `(1 |> why).flatMap(...)` or `e(1 |> why)`, which raise the same error,
  that counterpart is 235 or 209 tokens, 36 or 10 more.
- The helper costs 40 tokens with its line break, and a call such as
  `e(why total)` or `e(affects price)` costs as much as
  `total |> why |> edges` or `price |> affects |> edges`, so the helper is
  the most compact spelling of a counterpart that writes two stages, as
  four of the seven do, and inline spellings are more compact for the
  other three. `def e sel => ...` costs as much as the helper, and
  `def e(sel) => ...` two tokens more.
- Selectors, comprehensions, `zip` and membership tests are accepted syntax
  that the whole-corpus checks admit only in their own document cases. The
  extra baselines are not document cases, so those checks do not apply to
  them, and a more compact counterpart only makes the comparison stricter.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Edge lists of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#edge-lists-of-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> edges` is a pipeline stage: `|>` and then the identifier
  `edges`, with no argument. It binds at the precedence of `|>`, and it may
  follow a prefix stage, so `why total |> edges` is `(why total) |> edges`.
- Everywhere else `edges` is an ordinary identifier. There is no prefix
  form, so `edges total` stays the two statements `edges` and `total`, and
  `edges(selection)` stays an ordinary call. `|> Edges`, `|> edge` and
  every other name after `|>` that is not a stage keep the accepted
  parse-time `TypeError("<name> is not an operation")`.
- What follows the name reads as it reads after `nodes` and `holds`:
  `|> edges(1)` and `|> edges[0]` are the stage followed by the juxtaposed
  statements `(1)` and `[0]`, and `|> edges.length` raises
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '.'")` at the `.`, as `|> nodes(1)`,
  `|> nodes[0]` and `|> nodes.length` do at the accepted revision. The
  number of edges is written `(c |> why |> edges).length`.
- A pipeline that holds `|> edges` and then a name that is no stage, or a
  `|>` with nothing after it, is still a parse error, now reported for that
  later name or `|>`: `c |> why |> edges |> Edges` raises
  `TypeError("Edges is not an operation")`, as `c |> why |> nodes |> Nodes`
  raises `TypeError("Nodes is not an operation")` at the accepted revision.
- The stage parses to the public `Expr::Reason { stage: Stage::Edges, source }`.
  `Stage` gains `Stage::Edges`, after `Stage::Holds`, which `Stage::as_str`
  names `"edges"`. `Stage::from_name` is unchanged: it also reads the
  prefix stages, and `edges` has none.

### Values

- The stage selects what its source selects, through every stage before
  it, and gives, for each selected step in the selection's order and for
  each name in that step's `from` field in its order, the two-item list
  `[INPUT, NODE]` of that name and the step's `node` field. It is exactly
  `(SELECTION).flatMap(step => step.from.reduce((pairs, input) => pairs.concat([[input, pairs[0]]]), [step.node])[1:])`,
  where `SELECTION` is the selection that the pipeline before `edges`
  denotes, read as the accepted stages read their source, for every source
  and stage the pipeline accepts.
- Both items are strings, exactly as the record holds them. An instance
  property is named as `o.total`, a property of an anonymous object as
  `obj:0.gone`, and the step of an `if` block after its condition, as
  `if(b.w>0)`, which lists the names its condition reads. An input that a
  step reads twice appears once, as in its `from` field: after `a = 1` and
  `g = a + a`, `g |> edges` is `[["a", "g"]]`. An input may be a node that
  no selection selects, such as a pending property that a step reads or a
  reasoning observer: after `w = why total` and `n = w.length`,
  `model |> edges` holds `["w", "n"]`.
- A step is stated exactly when it has no inputs and no class governs it,
  so `SELECTION |> stated |> edges` is always `[]` and
  `SELECTION |> derived |> edges` always gives what `SELECTION |> edges`
  gives. The step of a class and of a function declaration has no inputs
  either, so neither contributes an edge.
- A reference alone denotes its own one-step selection, not its value, as
  it does before `stated` or `derived` and for `nodes` and `holds`. So
  `total |> edges` lists the edges into the total,
  `[["subtotal", "total"], ["tax", "total"]]`, as the accepted spelling over
  `total |> depth(0)` does, while the value `(total).flatMap(...)` of a
  number raises `TypeError("number.flatMap is not a function")`. In a
  declaration, the stage reads and observes `total` alone, as that spelling
  and `total |> nodes` do.
- `model` selects every node of the model, in model order, without pending
  nodes and reasoning observers, so `model |> edges` lists the edges into
  those nodes and excludes every declaration that holds a stage. An empty
  selection gives `[]`, and so does a selection whose steps have no inputs:
  after `o = {}`, the absent property's `o.gone |> edges` is `[]`.
- The stage lists every input of each step it keeps, whether the selection
  keeps that input's own step or not: `total |> why |> depth(1) |> edges`
  lists the six edges of `total |> why |> edges`, and
  `total |> why |> except(subtotal) |> edges` is
  `[["subtotal", "total"], ["tax", "total"], ["subtotal", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"]]`.
  After `kept = S |> nodes`,
  `(S |> edges).filter(edge => kept.includes(edge[0]))` keeps the edges
  between the steps of `S`: `[["tax", "total"], ["rate", "tax"]]` for the
  `except` selection above.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
rate = 0.2
subtotal = price * quantity
tax = subtotal * rate
total = subtotal + tax
wiring = total |> why |> edges
basis = tax |> why |> edges
assert(wiring, [["subtotal", "total"], ["tax", "total"], ["price", "subtotal"], ["quantity", "subtotal"], ["subtotal", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"]])
assert(basis, [["subtotal", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"], ["price", "subtotal"], ["quantity", "subtotal"]])
fee = 2
tax = price * rate + fee
assert(wiring, [["subtotal", "total"], ["tax", "total"], ["price", "subtotal"], ["quantity", "subtotal"], ["price", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"], ["fee", "tax"]])
assert(basis, [["price", "tax"], ["rate", "tax"], ["fee", "tax"]])
price = 12
wiring.filter(edge -> edge[0] == "price")
```

### Ending a pipeline

- The stage's result is a list of pairs, not a selection. A stage written
  after it has that list as its source, which is neither a reference nor a
  selection, so after the checks of its own binding it raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, where `<source>` renders the
  pipeline up to `edges`: `c |> why |> edges |> why`,
  `c |> why |> edges |> depth(1)`, `c |> why |> edges |> edges` and
  `c |> why |> edges |> nodes` raise
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>edges")`, as the same stages raise
  after the accepted spelling for its own source.
  `c |> why |> nodes |> edges` and `c |> why |> holds |> edges` raise
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>nodes")` and
  `TypeError("Cannot explain c|>why|>holds")`, as any stage after `nodes`
  or `holds` does.
- An `intersect`, `except` or `union` argument that ends with `edges` is not
  a selection, and a `path` target that ends with it is not a reference, so
  `intersect(f |> why |> edges)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain f|>why|>edges")` and
  `path(a |> why |> edges)` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain a|>why|>edges")`.
- A name that holds the list is a reasoning observer, which no selection
  holds, so after `other = f |> why |> edges`, `intersect(other)` keeps
  nothing, as for a name that holds the accepted spelling's list.

### Sources and undefined selections

- The source is written as a pipeline's source is, and it keeps that
  source's diagnostics: `1 |> edges` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`,
  `missing |> why |> edges` raises `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`
  and, outside a `suppose` block, `c |> why |> diff |> edges` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- A property path whose base is `null` or undefined raises
  `ReferenceError("<base> is not defined")`, as in
  `ReferenceError("n is not defined")` for `n.p |> edges` after `n = null`,
  and one whose base is neither an object, `null` nor undefined, such as a
  number or a string, raises `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, as in
  `TypeError("Cannot explain k.p")` after `k = 5`.
- A `depth` stage with a `null` or undefined limit makes the selection
  undefined, and then the stage gives undefined, which a declaration stores
  as `null` until the limit is known, a loop over it raises
  `TypeError("<source> is not iterable")`, and a member or index read of it
  gives undefined, which `--json` prints as `null`, as for the accepted
  spelling.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Bindings

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `edges` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("edges is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are bindings; methods,
  properties and object keys are not.
- The check runs first, then the checks of the stages before it, from the
  outermost inward, then the source is selected, as for the accepted stages,
  `nodes` and `holds`.
- A binding declared after a standing declaration makes that declaration's
  next evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails
  and rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and
  of `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff`, `path`, `intersect`, `except`,
  `union`, `nodes`, `holds`, `origins` and `outcomes`, which a binding
  shadows, is unchanged. A binding named `nodes` or `holds` does not shadow
  `edges`, nor one named `edges` the other stages.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its selection
  made, including those of the steps a filter drops, so a standing
  declaration re-derives whenever its selection would: through a value, a
  rule, a new or deleted node, a rewiring or a class-level rule. A
  rewiring, a new or deleted node and a replaced class-level rule add and
  drop edges; a change of a value alone re-derives the list with the same
  edges. An empty result still follows its source.
- It builds the step records that the accepted spelling builds, so the
  anonymous objects that later statements create are numbered as after that
  spelling, and it reads their `from` and `node` fields without recording
  the reads, so a declaration that holds it depends only on what its
  selection observes and gains no pending graph node for the records.
- Every pair holds two strings, so the stage gives no `null` or undefined
  item, and the null rule of stored declarations, which NUC 121's stage
  keeps for the `holds` values it gives, never applies to the pairs.
- An expression that holds the stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding
  it is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and that
  `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in place, and
  class-level rules give each instance its own list, which names the
  instance's properties.
- `.value` snapshots the list. When a declaration is stored, a `.value`
  read in a `depth` limit inside the stage's source is frozen, as it is in
  the accepted spelling's receiver.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed statements
  behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores the declarations that
  hold the stage, their observations and queued refreshes.

```
price = 10
qty = 3
subtotal = price * qty
wiring = subtotal |> why |> edges
failure = ""
try:
    wiring = 1 |> edges
catch error:
    failure = error
assert(failure, TypeError("Cannot explain 1"))
assert(wiring, [["price", "subtotal"], ["qty", "subtotal"]])
try:
    fee = 2
    subtotal = price * qty + fee
    assert(wiring, [["price", "subtotal"], ["qty", "subtotal"], ["fee", "subtotal"]])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert(wiring, [["price", "subtotal"], ["qty", "subtotal"]])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>edges` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an expression, with the
  source rendered once, so `c |> why |> edges` renders as `c|>why|>edges`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, except where the
  accepted renderer already abbreviates part of its source: a pipeline that
  is the receiver of a member read renders without its parentheses, so
  `(c |> why |> edges).length` renders as `c|>why|>edges.length`, as
  `(c |> why |> nodes).length` renders as `c|>why|>nodes.length`.
- A stage and its accepted spelling render differently, so they are
  different graph keys, conditions and `rule` texts, and an error that quotes
  one quotes `c|>why|>edges` where the other quotes
  `c|>why.flatMap(function())`.
