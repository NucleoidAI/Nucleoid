## Abstract

- A reasoning pipeline gains the stage `SELECTION |> xor(OTHER)`, the
  symmetric difference of two selections. It keeps the steps of `SELECTION`
  whose nodes `OTHER` does not select, in `SELECTION`'s order, followed by
  the steps of `OTHER` whose nodes `SELECTION` does not select, in
  `OTHER`'s order. Each step keeps its fields and the distance it has in its
  own selection, as `union` keeps them, so the stage is exactly
  `SELECTION |> except(OTHER) |> union(OTHER |> except(SELECTION))`, with
  each selection written and evaluated once.
- After `price = 10`, `quantity = 3`, `rate = 0.2`,
  `subtotal = price * quantity`, `tax = subtotal * rate`,
  `shipping = quantity * 2`, `total = subtotal + tax` and
  `freight = shipping + rate`,
  `total |> why |> xor(freight |> why) |> nodes` gives
  `["total", "subtotal", "tax", "price", "freight", "shipping"]`: what one
  explanation rests on and the other does not, without the quantity and
  the rate that both share.
- It completes [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md)'s `intersect` and `except` and
  [NUC 120](nuc-0120.md)'s `union`, both of which left the symmetric
  difference out. It keeps a selection, so every later stage applies to it,
  and a declaration that holds it follows the graph: once
  `shipping = price + 1` rewires the shipping, the price stands behind both
  explanations and leaves the result, and the quantity, which only the
  total still rests on, joins it.
- Only programs that were whole-program parse errors at the accepted
  revision change. The ones measured raised the parse-time
  `TypeError("xor is not an operation")`; where the statement parser
  backtracks from an unpacking or object-destructuring pattern,
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` or `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` first;
  or, in a program that also holds a form that only a later pass of the
  parser reads, that form's first error. The list of messages is the
  measured one, not a proof that no other message occurs. Variables,
  functions, properties and keys named `xor` keep their readings, and a
  binding named `xor` shadows the stage, as bindings shadow the other
  stages.
- No keyword, lexer token kind, error type or message is added; the grammar
  gains the contextual word `xor`. The public `Expr` enum gains one
  variant, `Expr::Xor`, which needs human SemVer review. No project version
  is changed. The authoritative contract is
  [Symmetric difference reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#symmetric-difference-reasoning-selections).

## Motivation

- A world model that explains two conclusions often needs what tells them
  apart: the facts and rules behind one alarm but not the other, the
  findings that separate two diagnoses, or the inputs that a price and a
  cost do not share. That is a contrastive question, and the symmetric
  difference of their explanations answers it in one selection. The same
  holds for impact: `affects price |> xor(affects rate)` is what a change of
  exactly one of the two inputs reaches.
- Accepted programs spell it as `S |> except(O) |> union(O |> except(S))`,
  which writes both selections twice:
  `why total |> except(why freight) |> union(why freight |> except(why total)) |> nodes`
  takes 22 lexer tokens, against 10 for
  `why total |> xor(why freight) |> nodes`. Writing each selection twice
  also walks it twice each time the spelling is evaluated, and a standing
  declaration evaluates it again on every change of either explanation.
- The other accepted spellings are longer or wrong:
  - `(S |> except(O)).concat(O |> except(S))` is a list rather than a
    selection, so no stage can follow it, and it is two tokens longer;
  - a filter over the step records cannot compare them by node in one
    expression, because a callback cannot read the parameter of the
    callback around it:
    `(why total).filter(s -> !(why freight).some(t -> t.node == s.node))`
    raises `ReferenceError("s is not defined")`, and a `filter` callback
    receives the item alone, so no index or list argument can stand in for
    the comparison;
  - filters that compare the records by value, such as
    `(why total).filter(s -> !(why freight).includes(s))` with its mirror
    concatenated, write both selections twice and take 44 tokens.
- Prior art: lodash's `_.xor`, the symmetric difference of arrays;
  JavaScript's `Set.prototype.symmetricDifference`; Python's
  `set.symmetric_difference` and `^`; Ruby's `Set#^`; Rust's
  `HashSet::symmetric_difference` and `^` on sets; and C#'s
  `HashSet.SymmetricExceptWith`. lodash and JavaScript keep the first
  collection's own elements in their order and then the second's, as this
  stage does.
- Draft [NUC 11](nuc-0011.md) plans no operation that combines two
  selections. NUC 106 and NUC 120 list a symmetric difference under "Not
  added"; this NUC adds it with the rules of both.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `6c8cbc67` on 23-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform
  draw at 13:14:19 over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks
  eligible for automatic selection and selected Reasoning enhancement,
  index 3, with probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the caller chose
  one bounded enhancement that no NUC proposes: the symmetric difference of
  two selections, which NUC 106 and NUC 120 both list under "Not added".
  The caller ruled out the operations that NUC 11 already plans (`why not`,
  `rules`, `conflicts`, `describe`, `export`, `all`, `any`, `retract` and
  `require`), a distance field, which would change the step records, and a
  `count` stage, whose name is a common binding. Token comparison is
  required; runtime applicability does not apply to this category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("xor is not an operation")` for `xor`, whatever follows it,
  and only that parser consumes `|>`. Giving `|> xor(` a meaning therefore
  changes only inputs that were parse errors. Where the statement parser
  backtracks, some of those inputs reported another parse error first:
  `x, y = c |> why |> xor(f |> why)` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")`
  and `{length} = c |> why |> xor(f |> why)` raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as for NUC 88's `path`, NUC 106's set
  stages, [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s `nodes` and NUC 120's `union`. A program
  that also holds a form that only a later pass of the accepted parser
  reads reports that form's first error instead, and is still a
  whole-program parse error: with an operator section of
  [NUC 109](nuc-0109.md) before the stage, as in
  `big = [1, 5, 9].filter(> 4)`, it raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '>'")`,
  and with a Python-style `self` parameter of [NUC 107](nuc-0107.md), it
  raised `SyntaxError("Expected a parameter but found keyword 'this'")`.
  Inside a template interpolation the accepted parser ignores trailing
  tokens, so `` `v ${quantity why total |> xor(why freight)}` `` keeps its
  accepted result, `"v 3"`.
- **A stage, not a function, prefix or operator.** A symmetric difference
  combines two selections into one, as `intersect`, `except` and `union`
  do, so it is a pipeline stage with its other selection written as theirs
  is. Rejected:
  - an infix operator such as Python's, Ruby's and Rust's `a ^ b`: `^` is
    not a character the accepted lexer reads (`1 ^ 2` raises
    `SyntaxError("Unexpected character '^'")`), so it would add a token, and
    Bazel query writes `^` for the intersection of two sets;
  - an infix word `a xor b`, which reads today as juxtaposed statements
    (`true xor false` raises `ReferenceError("xor is not defined")` at run
    time), so it would change accepted programs, and which PHP and Perl
    read as a logical operator on two values;
  - a call form `xor(a, b)`, which is an ordinary call today and stays one;
  - a prefix form `xor a b`, which is juxtaposed statements today;
  - a bare `|> xor` with the other selection elsewhere, which keeps its
    accepted error.
- **The order.** The steps of `SELECTION` that `OTHER` does not select come
  first, in their order, and then the steps of `OTHER` that `SELECTION`
  does not select, in `OTHER`'s order. This is the order of the accepted
  spelling, of lodash's `_.xor` and of JavaScript's
  `Set.prototype.symmetricDifference`, and it begins the result with
  `SELECTION |> except(OTHER)`, so the part of the result that belongs to
  `SELECTION` reads as `except` gives it. Rejected:
  - model order, which would lose the walk order that `why` and `affects`
    give;
  - interleaving the two walks, which no accepted spelling gives and no
    reader can predict;
  - `OTHER`'s steps first, which would make the source of the pipeline
    come second.
- **Membership by node, fields unchanged.** A step is kept when the other
  selection has no step for the same graph node, whatever the steps hold,
  as NUC 106 and NUC 120 compare steps. Every field of a step is a property
  of its node at that moment, so two selections never disagree about a node
  they share, and each kept step keeps its `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state`
  and `from` fields. The comparison goes through sets of the nodes, linear
  in the sizes of both selections. Rejected: comparing by value, which
  would keep a step whose value is NaN, which equals nothing, although both
  selections hold its node.
- **Distances from the walk that selected each step.** The steps of
  `SELECTION` keep their NUC 82 distances, and the steps of `OTHER` the
  distances they have in `OTHER`'s own selection, so
  `S |> xor(O) |> depth(n)` is
  `S |> depth(n) |> except(O) |> union(O |> depth(n) |> except(S))`: each
  side is measured from where its own walk began. No node belongs to both
  sides of the result, so no rule is needed for a node that both select,
  where `union` keeps the distance in `SELECTION`. Rejected: distances from
  the result's steps, which would put every step at distance 0, and
  distance 0 for the steps of `OTHER`, which would make `depth(0)` keep all
  of them.
- **The direction of `SELECTION`'s most recent walk.** A selection has one
  walk direction, which [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md)'s `path` uses for every step,
  and the stage keeps `SELECTION`'s, as `intersect`, `except` and `union`
  keep it. Rejected: a direction per step, which no accepted stage has, and
  `OTHER`'s direction, which would make the order of the two sides change
  what `path` keeps of `SELECTION`'s own steps.
- **Evaluated in place, once per application.** `OTHER` is evaluated when
  its stage applies, after the source selection and the stages before it,
  as NUC 106 and NUC 120 evaluate it, once per application, and also when
  `SELECTION` is empty, so an empty `SELECTION` still follows `OTHER` and
  gives its steps. When the pipeline holds a `depth` stage, `OTHER` is
  evaluated with its distances.
- **An undefined `OTHER` makes the selection undefined.** NUC 82 makes a
  selection undefined when a `depth` limit is `null` or undefined, and NUC
  106 and NUC 120 make their stages undefined when `OTHER` is. The stage
  follows them, and so does the accepted spelling, whose first `except`
  meets the undefined `OTHER`. Rejected: reading an undefined `OTHER` as
  empty, which would give a known answer from a value that is not known.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `xor` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("xor is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md), NUC 82,
  [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md), NUC 88, NUC 106, NUC 112, NUC 120,
  [NUC 121](nuc-0121.md), [NUC 128](nuc-0128.md) and
  [NUC 133](nuc-0133.md) do for their stages, through the existing
  binding check. A model that defines its own `xor` never has it silently
  replaced by a stage. The check runs with the pipeline's other checks,
  before the source is selected. At the accepted revision these programs
  raised the same message while parsing; under this NUC the statements
  before the pipeline run first, and a `try` can catch it.
- **A syntax-tree variant of its own.** The stage parses to a new public
  variant `Expr::Xor { source, other }`, with a matching crate-private
  `Link::Xor`, as NUC 120 gave `union` its own `Expr::Union`. Rejected:
  - a third `SetOperation` in `Expr::Combine`: `intersect` and `except` only
    filter the steps of `SELECTION`, where the stage adds the steps and
    distances of a second walk, and the accepted whole-corpus check
    `no_earlier_document_case_writes_a_set_stage` in
    `tests/selection_intersections.rs` counts the set stages of every
    document case and pins nine such cases, so a symmetric difference
    inside `Expr::Combine` would fail that accepted test for every new
    case;
  - a variant whose name holds `Union`: `tests/union_selections.rs` counts
    `Union {` in the parse of every document case and pins seven;
  - a `Stage` variant, because the stages of `Stage` take no argument;
  - lowering the stage while parsing into
    `SOURCE |> except(OTHER) |> union(OTHER |> except(SOURCE))`, which would
    copy both expressions into the tree, evaluate each twice, render the
    spelling in graph keys and errors, make a binding named `except` or
    `union` shadow the stage, and trip both audits above.
  `SetOperation` and `Expr::Union` stay unchanged.
- **The accepted machinery, without a copy.** The stage evaluates `OTHER`
  through the existing `other_selection` and `other_ranked`, keeps the
  steps of each side through the existing
  `combined(SetOperation::Except, ...)` against the other side, and joins
  the two parts through the existing `union`, in both the plain and the
  ranked evaluation. So it is the accepted spelling by construction, with
  its distances and direction, while each selection is walked once.
- **Observations.** A standing symmetric difference must change when either
  side does, so the stage keeps every observation that `SELECTION` made,
  including those of the steps its filters drop and of the steps it leaves
  out, and adds every observation that evaluating `OTHER` makes, including
  those of the steps it leaves out.
- **Naming.** `xor` is lodash's name for the symmetric difference of
  arrays, and the symmetric difference is what Python's and Ruby's sets
  write `^`, the exclusive-or operator, and what Rust's sets implement
  through `BitXor`. It is one short word that reads as a verb in a pipeline,
  as `intersect`, `except` and `union` do. At the accepted revision `xor`
  occurs in no program, document, test, dataset record, ARC dataset or
  source file of the repository, only in this Draft, so no binding shadows
  the stage. `xor` stays an ordinary identifier outside `|> xor(`, so this
  stage leaves an infix `a xor b` to any later proposal. Rejected:
  - `symmetricDifference`, JavaScript's method name, which is long for a
    pipeline stage and reads as a property;
  - `difference`, which is Python's name for what `except` does;
  - `diff`, which [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md) already uses for a stage, and
    `delta`, which reads as a change of value;
  - `either`, `apart`, `contrast` and `only`, which read as quantities or
    objects and are common variable names, as `apart` and `contrast` are in
    this NUC's cases;
  - `^`, which the lexer does not read, as above.
- **Requires.** NUC 106 and NUC 120, whose stages the symmetric difference
  combines and whose `OTHER`, distance, direction, shadowing and
  undefined-selection rules it shares. Those already require everything
  else the stage relies on, such as NUC 48's state filters, NUC 82's
  distances, NUC 83's `diff`, NUC 88's direction, NUC 112's `nodes`,
  pipelines, `why`, `affects` and `model`. NUC 121's `holds`, NUC 128's
  `origins` and `outcomes` and NUC 133's `edges`, which may follow the
  stage, are accepted and need no dependency of their own.
- **Not added.** An infix, call or prefix form; a `^` operator; several
  selections in one stage; a symmetric difference of lists of values;
  matching by value or by node name; a per-step direction; and any change
  to the accepted stages.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Symmetric difference reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#symmetric-difference-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> xor(OTHER)` is a pipeline stage: `|>`, the identifier
  `xor`, then a `(` token, one expression and `)`. Whitespace may separate
  the name from `(`; a line break may only where the lexer suppresses it,
  inside parentheses and brackets.
- `OTHER` is parsed as a parenthesized expression is, and an `OTHER` that is
  exactly the name `model` is the model, as for `intersect`, `except` and
  `union`. `|> xor()` and `|> xor(a, b)` report the errors that `()` and
  `(a, b)` report.
- The stage takes part in a pipeline as the other stages do: it binds at the
  precedence of `|>`, may be followed by further stages, and may follow a
  prefix stage, so `why total |> xor(why freight)` is
  `(why total) |> xor(why freight)`.
- Everywhere else `xor` is an ordinary identifier, and `|> xor` not followed
  by `(` keeps the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("xor is not an operation")`. There is no prefix form, so
  `xor total` stays the two statements `xor` and `total`, and `xor(a, b)`
  stays an ordinary call.
- The stage parses to the new public variant
  `Expr::Xor { source: Box<Expr>, other: Box<Expr> }`. The accepted
  `Expr::Reason`, `Expr::Depth`, `Expr::Path`, `Expr::Combine`,
  `Expr::Union`, `Stage` and `SetOperation` are unchanged.

### Selection

- The stage evaluates its source selection and the stages before it, then
  evaluates `OTHER` as a selection, and selects the steps of the source
  whose node `OTHER` does not select, in their order, followed by the steps
  of `OTHER` whose node the source does not select, in `OTHER`'s order,
  every step with its `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields
  unchanged.
- For every source and stage the pipeline accepts, that is exactly what the
  accepted `SELECTION |> except(OTHER) |> union(OTHER |> except(SELECTION))`
  gives, with the same distances and direction, where `SELECTION` is the
  selection that the pipeline before `xor` denotes, read as the accepted
  stages read their source: a reference alone denotes its own one-step
  selection, and `model` the model's steps in model order. That includes
  the undefined selections below.
- Steps are compared by graph node, whatever they hold, so a node that both
  selections select is left out, also when its value is NaN, which equals
  nothing: with `x = 0 / 0`, `y = x + 1` and `z = x * 2`,
  `why y |> xor(why z)` selects the steps of `y` and `z`.
- `A |> xor(B)` and `B |> xor(A)` select the same nodes, each beginning with
  its own source's steps.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
rate = 0.2
subtotal = price * quantity
tax = subtotal * rate
shipping = quantity * 2
total = subtotal + tax
freight = shipping + rate
apart = total |> why |> xor(freight |> why)
contrast = why freight |> xor(why total)
assert(apart.map(s -> s.node), ["total", "subtotal", "tax", "price", "freight", "shipping"])
assert(contrast.map(s -> s.node), ["freight", "shipping", "total", "subtotal", "tax", "price"])
shipping = price + 1
assert(apart.map(s -> s.node), ["total", "subtotal", "tax", "quantity", "freight", "shipping"])
assert(contrast.map(s -> s.node), ["freight", "shipping", "total", "subtotal", "tax", "quantity"])
quantity = 5
assert(apart.map(s -> s.holds), [60, 50, 10, 5, 11.2, 11])
```

### Other selections

- `OTHER` denotes a selection, as the source of a pipeline does, and as it
  does for `intersect`, `except` and `union`:
  - a reference selects its own node: a name, a property path such as
    `order.price`, `this.price` in a method, or `$Class.property` inside a
    class-level rule for the instance's property;
  - `model` selects every node of the model, in model order;
  - a prefix stage such as `why freight`, or a pipeline such as
    `freight |> why |> stated`, selects what it selects, and may itself
    hold `xor`, `union`, `intersect` and `except`.
- A reference to a node that is not in the model, such as an absent
  property, selects nothing, and the result fills in once the property is
  defined.
- A name whose declaration holds a reasoning expression, such as a
  selection, the list that a final stage gives or
  `(why freight).map(s -> s.node)`, is a reasoning observer, which no
  selection holds, so as `OTHER` it selects nothing and leaves `SELECTION`
  as it is: after `causes = why freight` or `names = why freight |> nodes`,
  `why total |> xor(causes)` and `why total |> xor(names)` select what
  `why total` selects. That is how the accepted revision reads such a name
  as a source. The pipeline is written in place instead, as in
  `why total |> xor(why freight)`. A name that holds an ordinary value, a
  list included, is a reference that selects its own node: after
  `xs = [1, 2]`, `why total |> xor(xs)` selects the steps of `why total`
  and then the step of `xs`, as the accepted spelling does.
- `OTHER` is evaluated when its stage applies, after the source selection
  and the stages before it, and before the stages after it, once per
  application, even when `SELECTION` is empty. When the pipeline holds a
  `depth` stage, `OTHER` is evaluated with its distances.
- An invalid `OTHER` keeps the diagnostics of a pipeline source:
  - a literal, a property path whose base holds no object, and any other
    expression that is neither a reference nor a selection raise
    `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`, where `<other>` is the canonical
    source of `OTHER`: `xor(1)`, `xor("price")`, `xor(price + 1)`,
    `xor(this)`, and `$Class.property` outside a class-level rule;
  - an `OTHER` that ends with `nodes`, `holds` or `edges` gives a list
    rather than a selection and raises `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`,
    such as `TypeError("Cannot explain freight|>why|>nodes")` for
    `xor(freight |> why |> nodes)`;
  - a name that nothing defines raises `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`,
    including the first name of a property path;
  - a pipeline in `OTHER` raises what it raises as a pipeline, including the
    shadowing error of a stage in it and, outside a `suppose` block,
    `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Composition

- The result is a selection, and every stage applies to it as to any other
  selection.
- `stated`, `derived`, `diff`, `intersect(X)` and `except(X)` keep a step by
  a property of its node, given a fixed `X`, so after the stage they keep
  what they keep of each side, and they apply to both sides alike:
  `S |> xor(O) |> stated` is `S |> stated |> xor(O |> stated)`, and
  `S |> xor(O) |> intersect(X)` is
  `S |> intersect(X) |> xor(O |> intersect(X))`.
- A later `why` or `affects` walks from every step of the result, `union`
  and `xor` combine it with another selection, `nodes` names its steps,
  `holds` gives their values, `edges` lists their edges, and `origins` and
  `outcomes` keep its ends as one selection.
- **Distances.** Each step keeps the distance of the walk that selected it:
  the steps of `SELECTION` their own, and the steps of `OTHER` the
  distances they have in `OTHER`'s own selection. So
  `S |> xor(O) |> depth(n)` is
  `S |> depth(n) |> except(O) |> union(O |> depth(n) |> except(S))`. A
  selection that no walk produced, a reference's own step and `model`, puts
  its steps at distance 0, as NUC 82 does.
- **Direction.** The stage keeps the direction of `SELECTION`'s most recent
  walk, and a later `path(t)` uses it for every step. After
  `why x |> xor(affects y)`, `path(t)` keeps the steps of either side that
  are `t` or rest on it, as after `why`; after `affects y |> xor(why x)` it
  keeps the steps that are `t` or that `t` rests on, as after `affects`. An
  empty `SELECTION` still sets the direction, so
  `affects total |> xor(why price)`, whose source is empty because nothing
  reads the total, gives the steps of `why price` with the direction of
  `affects`.
- **Identities.**
  - `S |> xor(S)` selects nothing, for the same pipeline `S` written twice.
  - An `OTHER` that selects nothing leaves `SELECTION` as it is, and an
    empty `SELECTION` gives the steps of `OTHER`.
  - `S |> xor(model)` and `model |> xor(S)` both select the model's nodes
    that `S` does not select, in model order, as `model |> except(S)` does,
    because every step of a selection is a node of the model.
  - `S |> xor(O) |> xor(O)` selects the nodes of `S` again: the steps of
    `S` whose node `O` does not select, followed by the steps of `O` whose
    node `S` selects, in `O`'s order. With `x = 1`, `p = x + 1`,
    `q = p + x`, `y = 2` and `r = q + y`, `why r` selects `r`, `q`, `y`,
    `p` and `x`, while `why r |> xor(why q) |> xor(why q)` selects `r`, `y`,
    `q`, `p` and `x`.
  - `A |> xor(B) |> xor(C)` and `A |> xor(B |> xor(C))` select the same
    nodes, those that an odd number of the three selections select, though
    not always in the same order: with `x = 1`, `a1 = x + 1`, `b1 = x + 2`
    and `c1 = x + 3`, the first selects `a1`, `b1`, `c1` and `x` and the
    second `a1`, `x`, `b1` and `c1`.
  - `S |> xor(O) |> except(O)` and `S |> xor(O) |> intersect(S)` are
    `S |> except(O)`, and `S |> xor(O) |> except(S)` selects the steps of
    `O |> except(S)`, with their distances and the direction of `S`.
  - `S |> union(O) |> except(S |> intersect(O))` is `S |> xor(O)`.
- **Order still shows.** A filter before the stage applies to `SELECTION`
  alone: `S |> stated |> xor(O)` compares the stated steps of `S` with all
  of `O`, so it keeps the derived steps of `O` that `S` selects, where
  `S |> xor(O) |> stated` keeps only stated steps. When two stages would
  fail, the one applied first reports its error.

### Undefined selections

- A `depth` stage whose limit is `null` or undefined makes a selection
  undefined, as NUC 82 specifies.
- When `OTHER` is undefined for that reason, the stage makes the reasoning
  expression undefined: the stages after it are not applied and their
  arguments are not evaluated, and a declaration stores the undefined value
  as `null`, as for NUC 106's and NUC 120's stages. The accepted spelling
  gives the same: with `a = 1`, `b = a + 1`, `c = b * 2`, `d = a + 3`,
  `e = 7` and `f = e + d`,
  `why c |> except(why f |> depth(null)) |> union(why f |> depth(null) |> except(why c))`
  is undefined, `null` under `--json`, because its first `except` meets the
  undefined selection.
- When `SELECTION` is undefined before the stage, `OTHER` is not evaluated,
  and the selection is undefined, as the accepted spelling is: after the
  same chain, `why c |> depth(null) |> except(missing) |> union(missing |> except(why c |> depth(null)))`
  is undefined, and the undefined name `missing` is never read.

### Bindings and sources

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `xor` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("xor is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders, block locals and an enum declaration named
  `xor` are bindings; methods, properties, object keys and enum members are
  not, so after `enum Gate: xor, nand`, `Gate.xor` is `"xor"` and the stage
  still applies.
- The checks of a pipeline's stages run from the outermost stage inward,
  then the source is selected, then the stages apply from the innermost
  outward, each `xor` evaluating its `OTHER`, whose own checks run when it
  is evaluated. Accepted pipelines keep this order.
- A binding declared after a standing selection makes that selection's next
  evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails and
  rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- Bindings named `except`, `union` or `intersect` shadow only their own
  stages: the symmetric difference does not read them, where the accepted
  spelling then raises `TypeError("except is not an operation")` or
  `TypeError("union is not an operation")`.
- An invalid source keeps its accepted diagnostic and is reported before
  `OTHER` is evaluated: `1 |> xor(missing)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")` and `missing |> xor(1)` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and
  of `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff`, `path`, `intersect`, `except`,
  `union`, `nodes`, `holds`, `edges`, `origins` and `outcomes`, which a
  binding shadows, is unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its source made,
  including those of the steps its filters drop and of the steps it leaves
  out because `OTHER` selects their node.
- It adds every observation that evaluating `OTHER` makes, including those
  of the steps `OTHER`'s own stages drop, of the steps it leaves out
  because `SELECTION` holds their node, and of model membership when
  `OTHER` is `model`, so a standing selection refreshes when either side
  changes through a value, a rule, a new or deleted node or a class-level
  rule, and an empty result still follows both sides. So does a
  declaration that calls a function evaluating a stage, because
  subscriptions are replaced on every evaluation.
- An expression that contains a stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding
  it is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and
  that `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in
  place, and class-level rules give each instance its own selection.
- `.value` snapshots a selection as for the accepted stages. When a
  declaration is stored, a `.value` read in the limit of a `depth` stage
  inside `OTHER` is frozen, as it is in a limit elsewhere, while the sources
  in `OTHER` stay unfrozen, as a pipeline's source does.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed
  statements behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores
  selections, observations and queued refreshes.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
subtotal = price * quantity
shipping = quantity * 2
apart = why subtotal |> xor(why shipping)
failure = ""
try:
    apart = why subtotal |> xor(1)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert(failure, TypeError("Cannot explain 1"))
assert(apart.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "price", "shipping"])
try:
    shipping = price / 2
    assert(apart.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "quantity", "shipping"])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert(apart.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "price", "shipping"])
quantity = 6
assert(apart.map(s -> s.holds), [60, 10, 12])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>xor(OTHER)` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an expression, with the
  source and `OTHER` rendered once each, so rendered size stays linear.
  `why total |> xor(why freight)` renders as
  `total|>why|>xor(freight|>why)`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, except where the
  accepted renderer already abbreviates part of it, as NUC 88 records.
- A stage and its accepted spelling render differently, so they are
  different graph keys, conditions and `rule` texts, and the errors that
  quote them quote their own spelling, such as
  `SyntaxError("Cannot delete total|>why|>xor(freight|>why)")`.

- Pipelines without the stage take their accepted paths and do no work for
  it.

  them through sets of their nodes, in time and memory linear in the sizes
  of both selections, besides the walks themselves. The accepted spelling
  evaluates each selection twice, once on each side of `union`.

## Errors

| Condition | Error |
| --- | --- |
| An `OTHER` that is a literal, a property path whose base holds no object, or any other expression that is neither a reference nor a selection | `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`, at the running top-level statement |
| An `OTHER` that ends with `nodes`, `holds` or `edges` | `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`, at the running top-level statement |
| An `OTHER` that names nothing, or a property path whose first name names nothing | `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`, at the running top-level statement |
| A pipeline in `OTHER` that fails | its own error as a pipeline, such as `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")` |
| A local or parameter, state variable, function or class named `xor` | `TypeError("xor is not an operation")`, at the running top-level statement, before the source is selected |
| `\|> xor` not followed by `(` | `TypeError("xor is not an operation")`, while parsing, as before |
| `\|> xor()` | `SyntaxError("Unexpected ')'")`, at the `)` |
| `\|> xor(` with more than one expression, or a trailing comma | `SyntaxError("Expected ')' but found ','")`, at the first `,` |
| `\|> xor(` left unclosed | `SyntaxError("Expected ')' but found end of input")`, or `SyntaxError("Unexpected end of input")` when no argument follows |
| A source that is not a reference, a selection or `model` | `TypeError("Cannot explain <source>")`, as before, before `OTHER` is evaluated |
| A source that reads an undefined name | `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`, as before, before `OTHER` is evaluated |
| `OTHER` selections nested more deeply than the parser allows | `SyntaxError("Expressions are nested too deeply")`, as for any nested expression |

- No error type or message is added. `<name> is not an operation` is the
  accepted pipeline diagnostic, and the errors of `OTHER` are those of a
  pipeline source. Only the messages that quote an expression holding the
  stage quote its own rendering.
- Source text cannot nest `OTHER` past the parser's limit. A tree built
  through the public API past the runtime's `MAX_DEPTH` raises the existing
  `TypeError("Maximum expression depth exceeded")` instead of overflowing
  the stack, through the guard that both evaluations of `OTHER` share.
- The positions, the `SyntaxError` messages of the argument list and the
  nesting limit are those of `union`, which shares the parsing and
  evaluation of `OTHER`. Measured after the eight lines of the Selection
  example: `why total |> xor()` raises `SyntaxError("Unexpected ')'")` at
  line 9, column 18; `why total |> xor(why freight, why price)` and
  `why total |> xor(why freight,)` raise
  `SyntaxError("Expected ')' but found ','")` at line 9, column 29;
  `why total |> xor(why freight` raises
  `SyntaxError("Expected ')' but found end of input")` and
  `why total |> xor(` raises `SyntaxError("Unexpected end of input")`, both
  at line 10, column 1; and 70 nested stages raise
  `SyntaxError("Expressions are nested too deeply")`.
