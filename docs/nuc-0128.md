## Abstract

- Adds two pipeline stages that keep the steps at the two ends of a
  reasoning selection. `SELECTION |> origins` keeps the steps of
  `SELECTION` that rest on no step of it, and
  `SELECTION |> outcomes` keeps the steps that no step of it rests
  on. Both keep the steps they keep in their order and with their `node`,
  `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields unchanged, and drop the
  others.
- So `price |> affects |> outcomes` selects where a change of the price
  ends, the steps it reaches that no step it reaches is computed
  from, and `price |> affects |> origins` where the change enters.
  `total |> why |> origins` selects what a total starts from, and
  `model |> origins` and `model |> outcomes` select the model's starting
  points and its final results.
- A step rests on another when it depends on it, directly or through nodes
  that need not be selected, along the edges that `why` and `path` follow,
  through no reasoning observer, and a step on a cycle of fixed anchors
  rests on itself. So `S |> origins` is the accepted
  `S |> except(S |> affects)`, and over a selection that `affects`, `why` or
  `model` produced, `S |> outcomes` keeps the steps whose node no step's
  `from` names.
- Both are filters, as `stated` and `derived` are: the result is a
  selection, so further stages apply, and the kept steps keep the direction
  of the most recent walk and their distances.
- At the accepted revision each program that holds either stage was a
  whole-program parse error, or an accepted program in which the parser
  skips the stage, such as a template interpolation whose trailing tokens
  are ignored. Most of the errors were the parse-time
  `TypeError("origins is not an operation")` or
  `TypeError("outcomes is not an operation")`; where the statement parser
  backtracks from an unpacking or object-destructuring pattern,
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` or `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")` came
  first. Only programs that were parse errors change: the accepted programs
  keep their results, and so does the parse error of a position that admits
  no pipeline, such as a parameter default.
  Both names stay ordinary everywhere else, as variables, functions,
  properties or object keys, and a binding of either name shadows its
  stage, as bindings shadow the other stages. No keyword, token or error
  type is added.

## Motivation

- World models ask where a change ends and what a result starts from. In
  an invoice, a change of the price reaches the subtotal, the tax and the
  total, but only the total is a result, a value that nothing else is
  computed from, and the subtotal is where the change enters. `affects`
  selects every step a change reaches and `why` every step a value follows
  from, in walk order, and no stage picks out their ends.
- The starts are spelled with the selection twice:
  `starts = price |> affects |> except(price |> affects |> affects)` is 14
  lexer tokens where `starts = price |> affects |> origins` is 7.
- The ends have no spelling in stages. `affects` selects a step of `S`
  exactly when the step rests on a step of `S`, so subtracting
  `S |> affects` finds the starts, but `why` keeps every step of `S`, so
  the same subtraction over `why` drops every step. Over an
  `affects` selection the ends are spelled with a filter and a quantifier
  over each step's `from`:
  `ends = (affects price).filter(s -> not any t in (affects price): s.node in t.from)`
  is 28 tokens where `ends = price |> affects |> outcomes` is 7.
- That spelling gives a list, not a selection, so a stage after it raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain price|>affects.filter(function())")`. And
  `from` names only direct inputs, so over
  `total |> why |> except(subtotal)` the filter keeps the price and the
  quantity, which the total rests on through the dropped subtotal.
- In logic these are the premises an argument starts from and the
  conclusions it ends at; in a dataflow graph, its sources and sinks. A
  stage for each lets a model report its inputs and its results in one
  pipeline that reads left to right.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `23b93c48` on 21-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform draw
  (`Get-Random -Minimum 0 -Maximum 5`) over the five categories that NUC 1's
  registry marks eligible for automatic selection, and drew index 3,
  Reasoning enhancement, with probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the
  caller chose this bounded enhancement because the pipeline has stages
  that walk a selection (`why`, `affects`), filter it (`stated`, `derived`,
  `depth`, `path`, `diff`), combine it (`intersect`, `except`, `union`) and
  project it (`nodes`, `holds`), but none that picks out the steps at its
  two ends, and no NUC proposes one. NUC 11's unimplemented `why not`,
  `rules`, `conflicts`, `describe`, `export`, `require` and `retract`, and
  the rejected exact depth of [NUC 101](nuc-0101.md), are other concepts.
  Token comparison is required; runtime applicability does not apply to
  this category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored (`missing_rubric`), not zero, because no rubric was frozen.
  Strategy learning is `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed,
  with no policy, selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("origins is not an operation")` or
  `TypeError("outcomes is not an operation")` for these names, whatever
  follows them, and only that parser consumes `|>`. Giving `|> origins` and
  `|> outcomes` a meaning therefore changes only inputs that were parse
  errors. Where the statement parser backtracks, some of those inputs
  reported another parse error first: after `a = 1`, `b = a + 1` and
  `c = b * 2`, `x, y = c |> why |> outcomes` raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")` and `{length} = c |> why |> origins`
  raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as for NUC 121's `holds`. Inside a
  template interpolation the accepted parser ignores trailing tokens, so
  `` `v ${c why c |> origins}` `` keeps its accepted result `"v 4"`.
- **Filters of the selection.** The two ends of a selection are steps of it,
  so both stages keep a selection, as `stated` and `derived` do, and further
  stages apply to what they keep. Rejected:
  - a member `SELECTION.origins`, which reads a missing property of a list
    today and gives undefined, which would change;
  - a call `origins(SELECTION)`, which is an ordinary call today, answered by
    a function named `origins` or raising
    `ReferenceError("origins is not defined")`;
  - a prefix form `origins c`, which is two juxtaposed statements today;
  - final stages that give the names or values of the ends, which `nodes`
    and `holds` already give after these filters.
- **Within the selection.** The ends are taken among the steps of the
  selection, not of the model. In the invoice `price = 10`, `quantity = 3`,
  `rate = 0.2`, `subtotal = price * quantity`, `tax = subtotal * rate` and
  `total = subtotal + tax`, `price |> affects |> origins` is the subtotal,
  which rests on the price and the quantity outside the selection, and
  `tax |> why |> outcomes` is the tax, which the total, outside the
  selection, rests on. Rejected: the model's sources and sinks within the
  selection, which select nothing in both. Over `model`, the two stages are
  the model's sources and sinks.
- **Transitive, through unselected nodes.** A step rests on another through
  any chain of dependencies, whether or not the selection holds the nodes
  along it, as `path` reaches its target through the steps an earlier filter
  dropped. So in the invoice, over the thinned
  `total |> why |> except(subtotal)`, whose steps are the total, the tax,
  the rate, the price and the quantity,
  `outcomes` keeps only the total, which rests on the price and the
  quantity through the dropped subtotal, while the filter on `from`, which
  names direct inputs only, also keeps the price and the quantity. Rejected:
  direct versions, whose ends would depend on what a selection happens to
  hold between them.
- **The edges the walks follow.** Reachability follows dependency edges as
  `why`, `affects` and `path` do: through pending nodes, which no selection
  lists, and through no reasoning observer. So a step whose only input was
  deleted rests on no step: in the model case below, after `delete fee`,
  the shipping that `fee * 2` declares is one of the model's origins,
  although it is derived and `model |> stated` leaves it out.
- **Cycles rest on themselves.** Resting is reachability along one or more
  dependency edges, so a step on a cycle rests on itself, and neither stage
  keeps it from any selection that holds it. The fixed cross-property
  anchors of [NUC 85](nuc-0085.md) make such cycles: after a class
  `Box(width, height)` that sets both properties,
  `$Box.width = $Box.height@first` and `$Box.height = $Box.width@first`,
  the width and the height of every `Box` instance depend on each other.
  For an instance `box`, `box.width |> origins`, `box.width |> outcomes`,
  `box.width |> why |> outcomes` and the accepted
  `box.width |> except(box.width |> affects)` are all empty, because
  `affects` already reaches the width from itself at the accepted revision.
  A property anchored only on itself, as in
  `$Box.width = $Box.width@first`, records no edge to itself, so it stays
  an ordinary step that rests on no step. Rejected: resting on other
  steps only, which would keep a step of a cycle as its own origin and
  outcome, break the identity with the accepted `S |> except(S |> affects)`
  and make the ends of a cycle depend on which of its steps a selection
  holds.
- **The accepted spellings by construction.** `S |> origins` keeps exactly
  what `S |> except(S |> affects)` keeps, in the same order, because
  `affects` selects every node that a step of `S` reaches by one edge or
  more, a step on a cycle included. Over a selection that `affects`, `why`
  or `model` produced, every
  node on a chain between two of its steps is itself a step, because
  pending nodes have no dependencies and the walks skip the observers that
  break a chain, so `S |> outcomes` keeps exactly the steps whose node no
  step's `from` names. Both stages walk once, from the selection's steps
  over the nodes they reach through their dependents, instead of selecting
  `S` twice.
- **Kept as the filters keep.** The kept steps keep their order, their
  `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields, their distances and
  the direction of the most recent walk, as `stated`, `derived`, `depth`,
  `diff`, `path`, `intersect` and `except` keep them, so every accepted
  stage applies to what they keep as to what those filters keep, and a
  later `why` or `affects` walks from the steps they keep. An empty
  selection stays empty, and a selection of one step keeps it under both
  stages unless the step lies on a cycle.
- **Undefined selections stay undefined.** A `depth` stage with a `null` or
  undefined limit makes a selection undefined ([NUC 82](nuc-0082.md)), and
  the stages after it are not applied, so both stages leave it undefined, as
  every accepted stage does.
- **Bindings shadow the stages.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `origins` or `outcomes` makes its stage raise
  `TypeError("origins is not an operation")` or
  `TypeError("outcomes is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md),
  NUC 82, [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md), [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md),
  [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md), [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md), [NUC 120](nuc-0120.md)
  and [NUC 121](nuc-0121.md) do for their stages. The check runs with the
  pipeline's other checks, before the source is selected. At the accepted
  revision these programs raised the same message while parsing; under this
  NUC the statements before the pipeline run first, and a `try` can catch
  it.
- **Naming.** `origins` reads as where a selection starts and `outcomes` as
  where it ends, the premises an argument starts from and the conclusions
  it reaches, and at the accepted revision no program of the four
  executable documents, the synthesized records or the ARC datasets binds
  either name or writes it in its code. Rejected:
  - `inputs` and `results`, of which `inputs` is a variable that programs
    of the specification and the examples declare, among them NUC 121's
    `Nucleoid rolls back failed updates through held values`, so each such
    declaration would shadow the stage;
  - `sources` and `sinks`, the words of dataflow graphs, of which `sources`
    also names data sources and pipeline sources in the documents;
  - `roots` and `leaves`, the words of trees, which a selection is not, and
    `leaves` also reads as a verb;
  - `starts` and `ends`, names that programs bind, as this NUC's own cases
    do;
  - `premises` and `conclusions`, since the documents already call the
    derived steps of an explanation its conclusions;
  - `first` and `last`, the element methods of [NUC 117](nuc-0117.md).
- **Requires.** NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 83, NUC 88, NUC 106, NUC 112, NUC 120 and
  NUC 121, whose stages these compose with and whose source, shadowing,
  undefined-selection and observation rules they share; NUC 88's `path`
  reaches its target through the same edges, and NUC 106's `except` gives
  the accepted spelling of `origins`. Those already require everything else
  they rely on, such as pipelines, `why`, `affects` and `model`.
- **Not added.** Prefix forms, direct versions, a stage that keeps both
  ends, the model's sources and sinks as stages of other selections, any
  change to the accepted stages, and the other operations of
  [NUC 11](nuc-0011.md).

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Origins and outcomes of reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#origins-and-outcomes-of-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> origins` and `SELECTION |> outcomes` are pipeline stages:
  `|>` and then the identifier `origins` or `outcomes`, with no argument.
  They bind at the precedence of `|>`, and they may follow a prefix stage,
  so `affects price |> outcomes` is `(affects price) |> outcomes`.
- Everywhere else both names are ordinary identifiers. There is no prefix
  form, so `origins total` stays the two statements `origins` and `total`,
  and `origins(selection)` stays an ordinary call. `|> Origins`,
  `|> origin`, `|> Outcomes`, `|> outcome` and every other name after `|>`
  that is not a stage keep the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("<name> is not an operation")`, and a line break right after
  `|>` outside parentheses and brackets keeps
  `SyntaxError("Expected an operation")`.
- What follows the name reads as it reads after `stated` or `nodes`:
  `|> origins(1)` and `|> origins[0]` are the stage followed by the
  juxtaposed statements `(1)` and `[0]`, and `|> origins.length` raises
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '.'")`, positioned as `|> stated(1)`,
  `|> stated[0]` and `|> stated.length` are at the accepted revision. The
  number of kept steps is written `(c |> why |> origins).length`.
- The stages parse to the public `Expr::Reason { stage: Stage::Origins, source }`
  and `Expr::Reason { stage: Stage::Outcomes, source }`. `Stage` gains
  `Stage::Origins` and `Stage::Outcomes`, which `Stage::as_str` names
  `"origins"` and `"outcomes"`. `Stage::from_name` is unchanged: it reads
  the prefix stages, and these two have none.

### Selections

- Step `t` of a selection rests on step `s` of it when `t` depends on `s`
  through one or more dependency edges, directly or through nodes that the
  selection does not hold, along the edges that `why`, `affects` and `path`
  follow: through pending nodes, and through no reasoning observer. So a
  step rests on itself exactly when it lies on a cycle of those edges, as
  every step of a cycle of [NUC 85](nuc-0085.md)'s fixed cross-property
  anchors does. A property anchored only on itself, as in
  `$Box.width = $Box.width@first`, records no edge to itself and lies on
  no cycle.
- `SELECTION |> origins` keeps the steps of `SELECTION` that rest on no
  step of it, and `SELECTION |> outcomes` keeps the steps that no step of
  it rests on, each in the selection's order, and drops the others. A step
  on a cycle rests on itself, so it is neither an origin nor an outcome of
  any selection that holds it.
- So `S |> origins` is exactly the accepted `S |> except(S |> affects)`:
  `price |> affects |> origins` selects where a change of the price enters
  the model, and `model |> origins` the model's sources.
- Over a selection that `affects`, `why` or `model` produced, `S |> outcomes`
  keeps exactly the steps whose node no step's `from` names:
  `price |> affects |> outcomes` selects where a change of the price ends,
  and `model |> outcomes` the model's sinks. Over an explanation,
  `x |> why |> outcomes` is the step of `x` alone, or nothing when `x`
  lies on a cycle.
- Over any other selection the ends follow the chains through the steps it
  does not hold: in the invoice of [Rationale](#rationale),
  `total |> why |> except(subtotal) |> outcomes` is the total alone.
- A step is an origin whatever its state: over the explanation of an
  instance property, the instance's own properties, which the class governs,
  are origins, though `stated` leaves them out, and so is a derived step
  whose every input was deleted or is a reasoning observer.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
rate = 0.2
subtotal = price * quantity
tax = subtotal * rate
total = subtotal + tax
unit = total / quantity
starts = quantity |> affects |> origins
ends = quantity |> affects |> outcomes
assert(starts.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal"])
assert(ends.map(s -> s.node), ["unit"])
shipping = quantity * 2
assert(starts.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "shipping"])
assert(ends.map(s -> s.node), ["unit", "shipping"])
```

- `unit` reads the quantity directly, but it rests on the total, which
  rests on the subtotal, so a change of the quantity enters at the subtotal
  and ends at the unit price. The shipping, which reads the quantity and
  nothing reads, is both an origin and an outcome.
- In the cycle of fixed anchors of [Rationale](#rationale), with
  `box = Box(3, 2)`, both stages drop the width and the height from every
  selection that holds them: `box.width |> origins`,
  `box.width |> why |> outcomes` and
  `model |> except(box.height) |> origins` keep neither, the last because
  the width rests on itself through the dropped height, and
  `model |> origins` and `model |> outcomes` keep the class `$Box` and the
  instance `box` alone. With `$Box.area = $Box.width * $Box.height`,
  `price = 10` and `total = price + box.area` beside the cycle,
  `model |> origins` keeps `$Box`, `box` and the price, and
  `model |> outcomes` keeps `$Box` and the total.

### Composition

- The kept steps keep their `node`, `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from`
  fields, their distances and the direction of the most recent walk, so
  `depth`, `path`, `stated`, `derived`, `diff`, `intersect`, `except`,
  `union`, `nodes`, `holds` and later walks compose with them as with the
  accepted filters.
- The accepted filters keep a step by a property of its own, and these two
  by its relation to the steps of the selection, so they do not commute
  with the filters: in the invoice of [Rationale](#rationale),
  `total |> why |> derived |> origins` keeps the subtotal, the first
  conclusion drawn from the facts, while `total |> why |> origins |> derived`
  keeps nothing.
- A reference alone denotes its own one-step selection, which both stages
  keep unless its step lies on a cycle. `model` selects every node of the
  model, in model order, without pending nodes and reasoning observers.
- Both may appear in `intersect`, `except` and `union` arguments as
  selections, and a `path` target that ends with either is not a reference,
  so `path(a |> why |> origins)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain a|>why|>origins")`, as
  `path(a |> why |> stated)` raises `TypeError("Cannot explain a|>why|>stated")`
  at the accepted revision.

### Sources and undefined selections

- The source is written as a pipeline's source is, and it keeps that
  source's diagnostics: `1 |> origins` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")`, `missing |> outcomes` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")` and, outside a `suppose`
  block, `c |> why |> diff |> origins` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- A `depth` stage with a `null` or undefined limit makes the selection
  undefined, and then the reasoning expression is undefined: the stages
  after it are not applied, and a declaration stores the undefined value as
  `null`.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Bindings

- Before its source is selected, each stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class of its name and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("origins is not an operation")` or
  `TypeError("outcomes is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Methods, properties and object keys are not bindings.
- The check runs with the checks of the other stages, from the outermost
  inward, then the source is selected, as for the accepted stages.
- A binding declared after a standing declaration makes that declaration's
  next evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails
  and rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- A binding named `origins` does not shadow `outcomes`, nor one named
  `outcomes` `origins`, and the accepted shadowing of every other stage is
  unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- Both stages keep every value and structural observation that their source
  made, including those of the steps they drop, and observe the structure
  of every node their walk visits, so a standing selection refreshes when an
  edge that decides its ends appears or disappears: a new dependent of the
  current outcome, a rewiring, a new or deleted node or a class-level rule.
  An empty result still follows its source.
- A declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier that
  holds either stage is a reasoning observer, which walks neither select
  nor traverse and `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate
  it in place, and class-level rules give each instance its own selection.
  `.value` snapshots it, and propagation order, `suppose` blocks and
  rollback behave as for the accepted stages.

### Rendering

- The stages render as `SOURCE|>origins` and `SOURCE|>outcomes` in graph
  keys, block keys, conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an
  expression, with the source rendered once, so a stage and its accepted
  spelling are different graph keys.

### Cost

- Pipelines without the stages take their accepted paths and do no work for
  them.
- Each stage walks once from the selection's steps over the nodes they reach
  through their dependents, in time linear in the nodes and edges walked,
  and keeps steps through a set of nodes. The accepted spelling of `origins`
  selects its source twice and materializes the second selection's steps,
  and the filter on `from` visits every pair of steps.

### Findings

- **Medium, public API:** `Stage` is an exhaustive public enum, so
  `Stage::Origins` and `Stage::Outcomes` break downstream code that matches
  it exhaustively, a SemVer-major change under the Rust API guidelines on
  the 1.0.0 crate, as for [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s `Stage::Nodes` and
  [NUC 121](nuc-0121.md)'s `Stage::Holds`. No version number changed;
  versions stay human-owned.
- **Low, intended:** only inputs that were errors at the accepted revision
  change. Programs that were parse errors now run, including those whose
  first reported error came from another part of the parser, such as
  `Unexpected '.'` or `Expected ')' but found identifier 'is'`. A REPL line
  that the accepted REPL rejected now defines its names, so later lines can
  behave differently. The error of a binding named `origins` or `outcomes`
  comes at run time instead of at parse time, so a `try` can catch it.
- **Informational:** `ObjectId::anonymous()` numbers anonymous objects with
  a counter shared across the process, at both revisions; the evaluator's
  differential normalizes those identifiers.
- **Informational, environment:** the disk filled during the evaluation.
  The evaluator discarded its interrupted run at the accepted revision,
  reran it cleanly and removed only build outputs; every result above comes
  from the clean runs.
