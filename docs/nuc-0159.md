## Abstract

- Add `Math.hypot(value, ...)`, the square root of the sum of the squares
  of its arguments: the Euclidean length of a vector, as JavaScript's
  `Math.hypot` and Python's `math.hypot` compute it. `Math.hypot(3, 4)` is
  `5`, `Math.hypot(1, 2, 2)` is `3`, `Math.hypot(-7)` is `7` and
  `Math.hypot()` is `0`.
- Every argument converts as the arguments of `Math.max` do. An infinite
  argument makes the result `Infinity`, even beside `NaN`; otherwise any
  `NaN` makes it `NaN`. While the largest magnitude lies between 2^-450 and
  2^450, the result is exactly `Math.sqrt(a * a + b * b + ...)` over the
  converted arguments, so it is exact for every integer Pythagorean tuple;
  outside that range every argument is first scaled by the same power of
  two, so the result is computed without intermediate overflow or
  underflow: `Math.hypot(1e200, 1e200)` is finite where
  `(1e200 ** 2 + 1e200 ** 2) ** 0.5` is `Infinity`.
- A declaration or class-level rule that calls it stays live with the
  dependencies of its arguments, as every Math call does, and an undefined
  argument makes the call undefined, as Math's gate does for every
  function.
- At the accepted revision every call of `Math.hypot` whose arguments
  evaluate without error and are not undefined raises
  `TypeError("Math.hypot is not a function")`, and only those calls change.
  A bare `Math.hypot` read stays undefined, as the read of every Math
  function does.
- This continuous Utility enhancement runs the token comparison: each new
  case is paired with its most compact accepted spelling, such as
  `(dx ** 2 + dy ** 2) ** 0.5`, with the implicit receivers `.x` of
  [NUC 102](nuc-0102.md) in class-level rules.
- The authoritative contract is
  [Reactive hypotenuse](../nucleoid.spec.md#reactive-hypotenuse).

## Motivation

- World models measure distances and magnitudes from live coordinates: how
  far one object is from another, the length of a velocity or an offset,
  the diagonal of a box. Today that is
  `((p.x - q.x) ** 2 + (p.y - q.y) ** 2) ** 0.5` or
  `Math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy)`, which square each difference by hand,
  overflow for large coordinates and vanish for tiny ones.
- JavaScript (`Math.hypot`), Python (`math.hypot`), C and C++ (`hypot`),
  Java (`Math.hypot`), Kotlin (`hypot`), Go (`math.Hypot`) and Rust
  (`f64::hypot`) name this operation, so programmers and language models
  reach for it.
- At the accepted revision `Math.hypot(3, 4)` raises
  `TypeError("Math.hypot is not a function")` at column 1 of its statement,
  and `Math.hypot` read without a call is undefined, as `Math.sqrt` is. No
  document case, dataset record or test calls it, and no NUC proposes it.
- One arm in the existing Math dispatcher, summing the squares of the
  converted arguments and scaling them by a power of two only when they
  would overflow or underflow, keeps the change bounded: no syntax,
  keyword, token, error message or public item.

- **One arm.** The name joins the variadic arms of `math` beside `max` and

  private helper of `src/builtins/numeric.rs`. The undefined gate, argument
  evaluation, call resolution, dependencies and transactions stay with
  their accepted owners, so the call behaves as every Math call does in

- **The method.** The helper returns `Infinity` as soon as it meets an
  infinite argument. Otherwise it takes the largest magnitude: between
  2^-450 and 2^450 it sums the squares of the arguments in argument order
  and returns the square root; above that range it multiplies every
  argument by 2^-600 and the root by 2^600, and below it by 2^600 and
  2^-600. Multiplying by a power of two is exact, so scaling keeps exact
  results exact, and within the range no square can overflow or turn
  subnormal for the largest argument. NaN and zero need no branch of their
  own: with no infinite argument, a NaN propagates through the scaling,
  the squares, the sum and the square root, and an empty or all-zero call
  sums to `+0`, whose square root is `+0`. The helper uses only IEEE
  multiplication, addition and square root, which give the same bits on
  every platform. Rejected, by an offline model of each method in IEEE
  doubles, which the Rust implementation matches bit for bit:
  - `Math.sqrt(a * a + b * b)` alone, which overflows to `Infinity` for
    `(1e200, 1e200)` and gives `0` for `(3e-200, 4e-200)`;
  - folding a two-argument `hypot`, as `f64::hypot` would be folded, which
    rounds each intermediate result: the fold gives `5.999999999999999` for
    `(3, 3, 3, 3)`, whose result is `6`, and the host C library's `hypot`
    behind `f64::hypot` is not specified to the bit, so it may differ
    between platforms;
  - dividing every argument by the largest magnitude, which is not exact:
    it gives `100.99999999999999` for `(99, 20)`, whose result is `101`;
  - a compensated or correctly rounded algorithm, such as Python's, which
    splits every square into exact parts and corrects the root, for less
    than one unit in the last place of gain.
- **Accuracy, a phase-2 result** on the production code that the final
  tree keeps. Over 100,000 random calls, with two to five arguments and
  magnitudes from 1e-323 to 1e308, the Rust implementation stays within
  one unit in the last place of the exactly rounded result, computed with
  exact rational arithmetic, and gives it in 79.5 to 99.1 percent of the
  calls, depending on the magnitudes and the number of arguments. It is
  exact on all 5,133 integer Pythagorean pairs tried, the 136 triples of
  three, the 61 sums of four and the 23 tuples of this proposal's cases,
  with either signs, and on the pairs scaled by 2^700 and 2^-700 and the
  triples scaled by 2^-1000, where `(a ** 2 + b ** 2) ** 0.5` overflows or
  vanishes for every one of them. Its result equals the phase-1 model's
  bit for bit on every one of these 131,510 calls. The one-unit figure is
  a measurement, not a bound: each square and each addition rounds once,
  so the error of the sum can grow with the number of arguments. Where it
  misses the exactly rounded result, it misses by one unit:
  `Math.hypot(3e200, 4e200)` is `4.9999999999999995e+200`.
- **Infinity before NaN, signs and the empty call.** These follow IEEE 754
  and C's `hypot`, which JavaScript's and Python's follow: an infinite
  argument gives `Infinity` because the length is infinite whatever the
  other components are, the result is never negative, and the empty sum is
  zero.
- **Conversion and the gate.** The arguments convert as those of
  `Math.max` do, so `Math.hypot("3", 4, true, null)` is
  `Math.sqrt(Math.square("3") + Math.square(4) + Math.square(true) + Math.square(null))`,
  `5.0990195135927845`. Math's undefined gate runs before any Math name is
  looked up, so an undefined argument makes the call undefined, where
  JavaScript gives NaN and Python raises `TypeError`; Python also raises
  for strings and `None`, where Nucleoid converts them as every Math
  function does. A string such as `"inf"` converts to `Infinity`, as for
  `Math.max`, where JavaScript gives NaN.
- **Calls only, static only.** Reading `Math.hypot` without a call stays
  undefined, as the read of every Math function does; `typeof Math.hypot`
  stays `"undefined"`. Rejected:
  - a global `hypot` or `dist`, because any new global changes what an
    accepted bare word reads: in an expression, `x is hypot` compares `x`
    with the text `"hypot"`;
  - methods on numbers and lists, such as `x.hypot(y)` and `xs.norm()`,
    and `Math.dist`, which are other proposals;
  - a list argument as a vector, `Math.hypot([3, 4])`: a list converts as
    for `Math.max`, so a list of two or more items is NaN, as in
    JavaScript;
  - `Math.toDegrees` and other new Math names.
- **Completion.** The terminal workspace offers twelve Math names, and
  [NUC 142](nuc-0142.md), [NUC 143](nuc-0143.md) and
  [NUC 146](nuc-0146.md) added none, so this proposal adds no completion
  entry either.
- **No `Requires` header.** The function uses only the accepted Math
  dispatcher, its undefined gate and `Value::to_number`, which no NUC

- **Public API.** No public item is added or removed, and no signature

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Reactive hypotenuse](../nucleoid.spec.md#reactive-hypotenuse).

### Calls

- `Math.hypot(value, ...)` is a static call on the native `Math`, with any
  number of arguments, none included.
- Calls are reached however a Math call is reached: declarations,
  class-level rules and conditionals, mappings and other callbacks,
  functions and methods, `try` and `catch`, `suppose`, templates and
  conditions.

### Evaluation

- The arguments are evaluated once, left to right, as for every Math call,
  and an argument's error propagates first: `Math.hypot(3, missing)` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`.
- Then Math's undefined gate applies: any undefined argument makes the call
  undefined, so after `o = {}`, `Math.hypot(o.missing, 4)` is undefined,
  `null` in JSON, as `Math.sqrt(o.missing)` is.
- A declaration whose argument reads a `null` binding holds `null` until
  the binding is defined, as for every Math call: after `x = null`,
  `d = Math.hypot(x, 4)` holds `null`, and `x = 3` makes it `5`.
- Then every argument converts with `Value::to_number`, as the arguments of
  `Math.max` do, and none is ignored:

| Argument | Converts to |
| --- | --- |
| A number | Itself, `-0`, NaN and both infinities included |
| `null`, and the keyword `undefined`, which reads as `null` | `0` |
| `true`, `false` | `1`, `0` |
| A string | `0` when it is empty or blank, otherwise its trimmed text parsed as a number, such as `" 3 "` as `3`, `"1e3"` as `1000` and `"inf"` as `Infinity`, or NaN, such as `"x"` |
| A list | `0` when it is empty, its item converted when it holds one, NaN otherwise |
| A date | Its milliseconds |
| An object, an instance, a class, a function or a regular expression | NaN |

### Result

- With the converted arguments:
  - `Infinity` when any is infinite, of either sign, even beside NaN:
    `Math.hypot(0 / 0, -1 / 0)` is `Infinity`;
  - otherwise NaN when any is NaN: `Math.hypot(0 / 0, 3)` is NaN;
  - otherwise the square root of the sum of their squares, which is never
    negative: `Math.hypot()`, `Math.hypot(-0)` and `Math.hypot(0, -0)` are
    `0`, and `Math.hypot(-7)` is `7`.
- JSON prints NaN and the infinities as `null`; plain output prints `NaN`
  and `Infinity`.

### Method and accuracy

- With `M` the largest magnitude:
  - when `M` lies between 2^-450 and 2^450, the result is exactly
    `Math.sqrt(a * a + b * b + ...)` over the converted arguments, the
    squares summed in argument order;
  - when `M` is larger, every argument is multiplied by 2^-600 first and
    the square root by 2^600, and when `M` is smaller, by 2^600 and 2^-600.
- Multiplying by a power of two is exact, so the result is exact whenever
  the scaled squares and their sum are exact and the sum is a perfect
  square, as for every integer Pythagorean tuple and its multiples by
  powers of two. Otherwise it rounds as those squares, sums and square
  root round, which [Rationale](#rationale) measures: within one unit in
  the last place of the exactly rounded result in every measured call. A
  result beyond the largest number is `Infinity`, and a subnormal result
  rounds twice, once at the square root and once at the final scaling.

| Call | Result | `(a ** 2 + b ** 2 + ...) ** 0.5` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Math.hypot(3, 4)`, `Math.hypot(5, 12)`, `Math.hypot(8, 15)`, `Math.hypot(20, 21)` | `5`, `13`, `17`, `29` | The same |
| `Math.hypot(1, 2, 2)`, `Math.hypot(2, 3, 6)`, `Math.hypot(8, 15, 144)` | `3`, `7`, `145` | The same |
| `Math.hypot(3, 4, 12, 84)` | `85` | The same |
| `Math.hypot(1.5, 2)`, `Math.hypot(0.1, 0.2)` | `2.5`, `0.223606797749979` | The same |
| `Math.hypot(1e200, 1e200)` | `1.414213562373095e+200` in JSON | `Infinity` |
| `Math.hypot(3e-200, 4e-200)` | `5e-200` | `0` |
| `Math.hypot(1e-300, 1e300)` | `1e+300` in JSON | `Infinity` |
| `Math.hypot(1.7e308, 1.7e308)` | `Infinity` | `Infinity` |

- The results were measured on the final tree's release build and the last
  column on the accepted revision's; [Rationale](#rationale) gives the
  accuracy and exactness measured in phase 2 on the production code that
  the final tree keeps.

### Liveness and class-level rules

- A declaration that calls `Math.hypot` depends on what its arguments read,
  as every Math call does: `gap = Math.hypot(charger.x - robot.x, charger.y - robot.y)`
  follows writes of either point.
- In a class-level rule each instance evaluates its own call, so
  `$Drone.reach = Math.hypot($Drone.x - home.x, $Drone.y - home.y)`
  follows each drone's coordinates and the home's, and a population
  mapping or filter over the rule follows new instances.
- A mapping that calls it follows its list: after
  `lengths = moves.map(move -> Math.hypot(move.x, move.y, move.z))`, a push
  to `moves` adds a length.

### Transactions

- An argument's error propagates through the existing transaction, so a
  failed update rolls back with every declaration that calls `Math.hypot`,
  its value and its dependencies: a push whose mapping reads an undefined
  name, `Math.hypot(leg.dx, leg.dy, rise)` before `rise` is defined, raises
  `ReferenceError("rise is not defined")` and leaves the list and the
  mapping as they were, and a thrown block undoes the writes before it.

### Rendering and graph keys

- Statements, `why`, `affects`, `--graph` and graph and block keys show the
  call in the canonical rendering of every call: after
  `d = Math.hypot(a, b)`, `(why d)[0].rule` is `"d = Math.hypot(a,b)"` and
  its `from` is `["a","b"]`.

### Cost

- The helper passes over the arguments twice, once for an infinite
  argument and the largest magnitude and once for the sum of squares,
  converting each argument with `Value::to_number` on each pass, which has
  no effect to repeat; it allocates nothing beyond the argument list that
  every Math call builds.
- **Spot check, a phase-2 result.** Measured, not projected, on the
  phase-2 release build `nucleoid-phase2.exe`, whose source the final tree
  keeps, and the accepted revision's release build, through the command
  line with `--json`, rustc
  1.97.1: the median of 21 interleaved runs of each program, including
  process start, one process at a time and each capped at 1.5 GB and 120
  seconds. Each program maps the 100,000 items of `0..<100000`; building
  them alone takes 17.1 ms on the candidate's build and 16.5 ms on the
  accepted one. Times are in milliseconds.

| Mapping | `Math.hypot` | `**` spelling | `Math.sqrt` spelling | `**` spelling, accepted revision | `Math.sqrt` spelling, accepted revision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `i -> Math.hypot(i, i + 1)` | 58.3 | 62.4 | 65.8 | 62.6 | 65.8 |
| `i -> Math.hypot(i, i + 1, i + 2, i + 3, i + 4)` | 69.7 | 86.2 | 93.9 | 85.3 | 93.0 |

- The spellings are `(i ** 2 + (i + 1) ** 2) ** 0.5` and
  `Math.sqrt(i * i + (i + 1) * (i + 1))`, and their five-argument forms.
  `Math.hypot` takes 0.93 and 0.81 times the `**` spelling's time with two
  and five arguments, because one call replaces the squares and sums that

### Findings

- **Low, accuracy example:** [Rationale](#rationale) calls
  `Math.hypot(3e200, 4e200)`, which gives `4.9999999999999995e+200`, a miss
  of one unit in the last place. It is not one: the exact result lies
  halfway between that value and `5e200`, and rounding half to even picks
  `4.9999999999999995e+200`, so the result is correctly rounded.
- **Low, accuracy figure:** the document's "within one unit in the last
  place" for two to five arguments is measured, not a bound, as it says,
  and it does not hold for every input:
  `Math.hypot(7.247968854536161e128, 4.584011508266885e129, 7.131411441737109e128, -5.628613831965113e128, -4.7528309557298655e128)`
  gives `4.7528695839704286e+129`, two units from the correctly rounded
  `4.7528695839704274e+129`. The specification makes no accuracy claim
  beyond exactness on exact sums.
- **Informational, other callees:** a parenthesized callee,
  `(Math.hypot)(3, 4)`, and an alias of `Math`, `m.hypot(3, 4)` after
  `m = Math`, change as well. Both raised exactly the former error at the
  accepted revision, so they fall within the documented change, but the
  document does not mention them and the suite's audit cannot see them; no
  accepted program uses either.
