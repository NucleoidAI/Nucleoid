## Abstract

- `selection |> union(other)` selects what either of two explanations
  selects: the selection's steps in their order, followed by the steps of
  `other` whose node the selection does not select, in `other`'s order,
  each with its fields unchanged. So
  `tax |> why |> union(shipping |> why)` is everything the tax or the
  shipping rests on, each node once.
- It completes the set stages of [NUC 106](nuc-0106.md): `intersect` keeps
  what both explanations select and `except` what only the first selects,
  and `union` gives what either selects. `other` is written as a
  pipeline's source is, evaluated in place each time the stage applies,
  and steps are compared by their node.
- For every source and stage the pipeline accepts, the stage selects
  exactly what the accepted `(selection).concat(other |> except(selection))`
  gives, except that an `other` that a `depth(null)` makes undefined makes
  the whole selection undefined, as for `intersect` and `except`, where the
  accepted spelling appends undefined as an element.
- The result is a selection, so further stages follow it: `stated`,
  `derived`, `diff`, `intersect` and `except` keep what they keep of each
  side, a later `why` or `affects` walks from every step, each step keeps the
  [NUC 82](nuc-0082.md) distance of the walk that selected it, and the union
  keeps the direction of the selection's most recent walk for a later
  [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md) `path`. It stays live through every observation of
  both selections and renders as `selection|>union(other)`.
- At the accepted revision each such pipeline raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("union is not an operation")`, or, where the statement parser
  backtracks, another parse error first. Only those programs change.
  `union` stays an ordinary name everywhere else, as a variable, function,
  property or object key, and a binding named `union` shadows the stage, as
  bindings shadow the other stages.
- No keyword, lexer token, error type or message is added. The public `Expr`
  enum gains one variant, `Expr::Union`, which needs human SemVer review. No
  project version is changed. The authoritative contract is
  [Union reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#union-reasoning-selections).

## Motivation

- World models answer questions about several conclusions at once: what
  an order's tax or its shipping rests on, which facts a decision or its
  audit depends on, and what a change of either of two inputs reaches. NUC
  106 answers the shared and the distinct parts of two explanations with
  `intersect` and `except`; their combined part has no stage.
- Today the combined part is spelled
  `(tax |> why).concat(shipping |> why |> except(tax |> why))`, which names
  the first explanation twice. The plain
  `(tax |> why).concat(shipping |> why)` lists a shared cause such as the
  quantity twice: with the order of [Selection](#selection) it gives
  `["tax", "subtotal", "rate", "price", "quantity", "shipping", "quantity"]`.
  Removing the repeats with [NUC 71](nuc-0071.md)'s `distinct`, as in
  `(tax |> why).concat(shipping |> why).distinct()`, compares the step
  records by value instead of by node, so a step whose value is NaN, which
  equals nothing, stays twice: with `x = 0 / 0`, `y = x + 1` and
  `z = x * 2`, `(why y).concat(why z).distinct()` lists `x` twice.
- Every such result is a list rather than a selection, so no stage can
  follow it: `((tax |> why).concat(shipping |> why |> except(tax |> why))) |> stated`
  raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain tax|>why.concat(shipping|>why|>except(tax|>why))")`.
  Narrowing the combined part to its facts takes another spelling with the
  filter written into both parts, such as
  `(tax |> why |> stated).concat(shipping |> why |> stated).distinct()`.
- A union stage states the question once, keeps each node once, and keeps
  the answer a selection that `stated`, `derived` and the other stages can
  narrow further. `why tax |> union(why shipping)` is 8 lexer tokens against
  14 for the most compact accepted spelling,
  `(why tax).concat(why shipping).distinct()`, and 16 for the spelling that
  compares by node, `(why tax).concat(why shipping |> except(why tax))`;
  `why tax |> union(why shipping) |> stated` is 10 against 18 and 20.
- Prior art: SQL's `UNION`, Bazel query's `union` and `+` operators over
  target sets, as in `deps(//a) union deps(//b)`, C# LINQ's `Union`,
  Kotlin's `union`, Python's set `|` and `set.union`, and JavaScript's
  `Set.prototype.union`. LINQ, Kotlin and JavaScript keep the first
  collection's elements in their order and then add the second's new ones in
  theirs, as this stage does.
- Draft [NUC 11](nuc-0011.md) plans the stages `why`, `why not`, `affects`,
  `rules`, `conflicts`, `describe`, `export` and `depth(n)` and filters over
  a single selection; none combines two selections. NUC 106 left a union
  out, because its request excluded one and its order would need a rule of
  its own; this NUC gives that rule.

## Rationale

- **Category and selection.** The cycle that allocated this Draft, in the
  pushed commit `22d7c31d` on 18-Nov-2026, made one tool-backed uniform draw
  over the five categories that NUC 1's registry marks eligible for
  automatic selection and selected Reasoning enhancement, index 3, with
  probability 1/5 and no redraw. Within it, the caller chose this bounded
  enhancement because it completes NUC 106's set stages, which left a union
  out on purpose, and no NUC proposes it. Token comparison is required;
  runtime applicability does not apply to this category.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Only former errors.** At the accepted revision the pipeline parser reads
  the name after `|>` as a stage and raises the parse-time
  `TypeError("union is not an operation")` for `union`, whatever follows it,
  and only that parser consumes `|>`. Giving `|> union(` a meaning therefore
  changes only inputs that were parse errors. Where the statement parser
  backtracks, some of those inputs reported another parse error first:
  `x, y = c |> why |> union(f |> why)` raised `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")`
  and `{length} = c |> why |> union(f |> why)` raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '='")`, as for NUC 88's `path`, NUC 106's set
  stages and [NUC 112](nuc-0112.md)'s `nodes`. Inside a template
  interpolation the accepted parser ignores trailing tokens, so
  `` `v ${quantity why tax |> union(why shipping)}` `` keeps its accepted
  result.
- **A stage, not a function, prefix or operator.** A union combines two
  selections into one, as `intersect` and `except` do, so it is a pipeline
  stage with its other selection written as theirs is. Rejected:
  - an infix operator such as Bazel's `a union b` or Python's `a | b`, which
    would be a new keyword or a new meaning of an accepted operator;
  - a call form `union(a, b)`, which is an ordinary call today and stays
    one;
  - a prefix form `union a b`, which is juxtaposed statements today;
  - a bare `|> union` with the other selection elsewhere, which keeps its
    accepted error.
- **The order.** The steps of `SELECTION` come first, in their order, and
  then the steps of `OTHER` whose node `SELECTION` does not select, in
  `OTHER`'s order. This is the order of the accepted spelling, of LINQ's
  `Union`, Kotlin's `union` and JavaScript's `Set.prototype.union`, and it
  keeps `SELECTION` a prefix of the result, so a union only adds to what it
  starts from. Rejected:
  - model order, which would reorder `SELECTION` and lose the walk order
    that `why` and `affects` give;
  - interleaving the two walks, which no accepted spelling gives and no
    reader can predict;
  - keeping a shared node at its place in `OTHER`, which would move a step
    of `SELECTION`.
- **Membership by node, fields unchanged.** A step is added when
  `SELECTION` has no step for the same graph node, whatever the steps hold,
  as NUC 106 compares steps. Every field of a step is a property of its node
  at that moment, so two selections never disagree about a node they share,
  and the union keeps the step that `SELECTION` holds, with its `node`,
  `holds`, `rule`, `state` and `from` fields. The comparison goes through a
  set of `SELECTION`'s nodes, linear in the sizes of both selections.
  Rejected: comparing by value, which would add a step whose value is NaN
  twice, and by node name through the step records, which the stage has no
  need to build.
- **Distances from the walk that selected each step.** The steps of
  `SELECTION` keep their NUC 82 distances, and the steps added from `OTHER`
  keep the distances they have in `OTHER`'s own selection, so
  `S |> union(O) |> depth(n)` is `(S |> depth(n)).concat(O |> except(S) |> depth(n))`
  by construction: each side is measured from where its own walk began.
  Rejected:
  - distances from the union's steps, which would put every step at
    distance 0 and make a later `depth(n)` keep everything;
  - the smaller of the two distances for a shared node, which would make a
    later `depth(n)` keep a step of `SELECTION` that `S |> depth(n)` drops;
  - distance 0 for the added steps, which would make `depth(0)` keep all of
    `OTHER`'s new steps.
- **The direction of `SELECTION`'s most recent walk.** A selection has one
  walk direction, which [NUC 88](nuc-0088.md)'s `path` uses for every step,
  and the union keeps `SELECTION`'s, as `intersect` and `except` keep it. A
  later `path(t)` after `why x |> union(affects y)` therefore keeps the steps
  of either side that are `t` or rest on it, as after `why`. Rejected: a
  direction per step, which no accepted stage has, and `OTHER`'s direction,
  which would make the order of the two sides change what `path` keeps of
  `SELECTION` itself.
- **Evaluated in place, once per application.** `OTHER` is evaluated when
  its stage applies, after the source selection and the stages before it,
  as NUC 106 evaluates it, once per application, and also when `SELECTION`
  is empty, so an empty `SELECTION` still follows `OTHER`. When the pipeline
  holds a `depth` stage, `OTHER` is evaluated with its distances.
- **An undefined `OTHER` makes the selection undefined.** NUC 82 makes a
  selection undefined when a `depth` limit is `null` or undefined, and NUC
  106 makes a set stage undefined when its `OTHER` is. The union follows
  NUC 106: it applies no later stage and evaluates no later argument.
  Rejected:
  - the accepted spelling's result, the steps of `SELECTION` followed by an
    undefined element, which is not a selection and which no stage could
    follow;
  - reading an undefined `OTHER` as empty, which would give a known answer
    from a value that is not known.
- **Bindings shadow the stage.** A local or parameter, state variable,
  function or class named `union` makes the stage raise
  `TypeError("union is not an operation")`, as [NUC 48](nuc-0048.md), NUC
  82, [NUC 83](nuc-0083.md), NUC 88, NUC 106 and NUC 112 do for their
  stages. A model that defines its own `union` never has it silently
  replaced by a stage. The check runs with the pipeline's other checks,
  before the source is selected. At the accepted revision these programs
  raised the same message while parsing; under this NUC the statements
  before the pipeline run first, and a `try` can catch it.
- **A syntax-tree variant of its own.** The stage parses to a new public
  variant `Expr::Union { source, other }`, with a matching crate-private
  `Link::Union`, rather than to a third `SetOperation` in `Expr::Combine`:
  - `intersect` and `except` only filter the steps of `SELECTION`, where a
    union merges the steps of two walks and their distances, so it needs a
    combination of its own in both the plain and the ranked evaluation;
  - the accepted whole-corpus check
    `no_earlier_document_case_writes_a_set_stage` in
    `tests/selection_intersections.rs` counts `Combine {` in the parse of
    every document case and pins nine such cases, so a union inside
    `Expr::Combine` would fail that accepted test for every new case.
  `SetOperation` stays unchanged.
- **Observations.** A standing union must change when either side does, so
  the stage keeps every observation that `SELECTION` made, including those
  of the steps its filters drop, and adds every observation that evaluating
  `OTHER` makes, including those of the steps `OTHER` holds that the union
  does not add again.
- **Naming.** `union` is SQL's and Bazel query's name for this operator and
  the method name of LINQ, Kotlin, Python's sets and JavaScript's sets, and
  it reads as a verb in a pipeline, as `intersect` and `except` do.
  Rejected:
  - `concat` and `plus`, which keep duplicates in JavaScript and Kotlin;
  - `or` and `and`, which are keywords;
  - `either`, `both` and `merge`, which read as quantities or objects and
    are common variable names, as `either` is in this NUC's cases;
  - `join`, which lists already have for joining their items into a
    string.
- **Requires.** NUC 48, NUC 82, NUC 83, NUC 88, NUC 106 and NUC 112, whose
  stages the union composes with and whose `OTHER`, distance, direction,
  shadowing and undefined-selection rules it shares; NUC 112's `nodes`
  names the union's steps, and its contract lists `union` among the stages
  it ends. Those already require everything else the stage relies on, such
  as pipelines, `why`, `affects` and `model`.
- **Not added.** A symmetric difference; several selections in one stage;
  an infix, call or prefix form; matching by value or by node name; a union
  of lists of values; a per-step direction; and any change to the accepted
  stages.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Union reasoning selections](../nucleoid.spec.md#union-reasoning-selections).

### Syntax

- `SELECTION |> union(OTHER)` is a pipeline stage: `|>`, the identifier
  `union`, then a `(` token, one expression and `)`. Whitespace may separate
  the name from `(`; a line break may only where the lexer suppresses it,
  inside parentheses and brackets.
- `OTHER` is parsed as a parenthesized expression is, and an `OTHER` that is
  exactly the name `model` is the model, as for `intersect` and `except`.
  `|> union()` and `|> union(a, b)` report the errors that `()` and `(a, b)`
  report.
- The stage takes part in a pipeline as the other stages do: it binds at the
  precedence of `|>`, may be followed by further stages, and may follow a
  prefix stage, so `why tax |> union(why shipping)` is
  `(why tax) |> union(why shipping)`.
- Everywhere else `union` is an ordinary identifier, and `|> union` not
  followed by `(` keeps the accepted parse-time
  `TypeError("union is not an operation")`. There is no prefix form, so
  `union c` stays the two statements `union` and `c`, and `union(a, b)`
  stays an ordinary call.
- The stage parses to the new public variant
  `Expr::Union { source: Box<Expr>, other: Box<Expr> }`. The accepted
  `Expr::Reason`, `Expr::Depth`, `Expr::Path`, `Expr::Combine`, `Stage` and
  `SetOperation` are unchanged.

### Selection

- The stage evaluates its source selection and the stages before it, then
  evaluates `OTHER` as a selection, and selects the steps of the source in
  their order, followed by the steps of `OTHER` whose node the source does
  not select, in `OTHER`'s order, every step with its `node`, `holds`,
  `rule`, `state` and `from` fields unchanged.
- For every source and stage the pipeline accepts, that is exactly what the
  accepted `(SELECTION).concat(OTHER |> except(SELECTION))` gives, where
  `SELECTION` is the selection that the pipeline before `union` denotes,
  read as the accepted stages read their source: a reference alone denotes
  its own one-step selection, as in
  `(price |> why |> depth(0)).concat(rate |> except(price))` for
  `price |> union(rate)`, and `model` the model's steps in model order. The
  only exception is an undefined `OTHER`, under
  [Undefined selections](#undefined-selections).
- Steps are compared by graph node, whatever they hold, so a node that both
  selections select appears once, at its place in `SELECTION`, and a step
  whose value is NaN is not added twice.
- `A |> union(B)` and `B |> union(A)` select the same nodes, each beginning
  with its own source's steps.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
rate = 0.2
subtotal = price * quantity
tax = subtotal * rate
shipping = quantity * 2
either = tax |> why |> union(shipping |> why)
causes = why shipping |> union(why tax)
assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["tax", "subtotal", "rate", "price", "quantity", "shipping"])
assert(causes.map(s -> s.node), ["shipping", "quantity", "tax", "subtotal", "rate", "price"])
fee = 2
shipping = quantity * 2 + fee
assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["tax", "subtotal", "rate", "price", "quantity", "shipping", "fee"])
tax = subtotal * rate + fee
assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["tax", "subtotal", "rate", "fee", "price", "quantity", "shipping"])
```

### Other selections

- `OTHER` denotes a selection, as the source of a pipeline does, and as it
  does for `intersect` and `except`:
  - a reference selects its own node: a name, a property path such as
    `order.price`, `this.price` in a method, or `$Class.property` inside a
    class-level rule for the instance's property;
  - `model` selects every node of the model, in model order;
  - a prefix stage such as `why shipping`, or a pipeline such as
    `shipping |> why |> stated`, selects what it selects, and may itself
    hold `union`, `intersect` and `except`.
- A reference to a node that is not in the model, such as an absent
  property, adds nothing, and the result fills in once the property is
  defined.
- A name that holds a selection is a reasoning observer, which no selection
  holds, so as `OTHER` it adds nothing: after `causes = why shipping`,
  `why tax |> union(causes)` is `why tax`. That is how the accepted revision
  reads such a name as a source. The pipeline is written in place instead,
  as in `why tax |> union(why shipping)`.
- `OTHER` is evaluated when its stage applies, after the source selection
  and the stages before it, and before the stages after it, once per
  application, even when `SELECTION` is empty. When the pipeline holds a
  `depth` stage, `OTHER` is evaluated with its distances.
- An invalid `OTHER` keeps the diagnostics of a pipeline source:
  - a literal, a property path whose base holds no object, and any other
    expression that is neither a reference nor a selection raise
    `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`, where `<other>` is the canonical
    source of `OTHER`: `union(1)`, `union("price")`, `union(price + 1)`,
    `union(this)`, and `$Class.property` outside a class-level rule;
  - a name that nothing defines raises `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`,
    including the first name of a property path;
  - a pipeline in `OTHER` raises what it raises as a pipeline, including the
    shadowing error of a stage in it and, outside a `suppose` block,
    `TypeError("Cannot use 'diff' outside suppose")`.
- These errors are positioned at the running top-level statement and roll
  back its transaction.

### Composition

- The result is a selection, and every stage applies to it as to any other
  selection.
- `stated`, `derived`, `diff`, `intersect(X)` and `except(X)` keep a step by
  a property of its node, given a fixed `X`, so after a union they keep what
  they keep of each side:
  `S |> union(O) |> stated` is `(S |> stated).concat(O |> except(S) |> stated)`,
  and `S |> union(O) |> intersect(X)` is
  `S |> intersect(X) |> union(O |> intersect(X))`.
- A later `why` or `affects` walks from every step of the union, as from any
  selection, and `nodes` names its steps.
- **Distances.** Each step keeps the distance of the walk that selected it:
  the steps of `SELECTION` their own, and the steps added from `OTHER` the
  distances they have in `OTHER`'s own selection. So
  `S |> union(O) |> depth(n)` is `(S |> depth(n)).concat(O |> except(S) |> depth(n))`.
  A node that both sides select keeps its distance in `SELECTION`, so
  `S |> union(O) |> depth(n)` is not `S |> depth(n) |> union(O |> depth(n))`
  when `OTHER` reaches a node of `SELECTION` sooner than `SELECTION` does.
  A selection that no walk produced, a reference's own step and `model`,
  puts its steps at distance 0, as NUC 82 does.
- **Direction.** The union keeps the direction of `SELECTION`'s most recent
  walk, and a later `path(t)` uses it for every step. After
  `why x |> union(affects y)`, `path(t)` keeps the steps of either side that
  are `t` or rest on it, as after `why`; after `affects y |> union(why x)` it
  keeps the steps that are `t` or that `t` rests on, as after `affects`.
  When both sides walk in the same direction,
  `S |> union(O) |> path(t)` is `(S |> path(t)).concat(O |> except(S) |> path(t))`.
- **Identities.**
  - `S |> union(S)` selects what `S` selects, for the same pipeline `S`
    written twice, and repeating a stage with the same `OTHER` changes
    nothing.
  - `S |> union(model)` selects the steps of `S` followed by every other node
    of the model in model order, and `model |> union(S)` selects the model,
    because every step of a selection is a node of the model.
  - `A |> union(B) |> union(C)` and `A |> union(B |> union(C))` select the
    same steps in the same order, with the same distances.
  - `S |> union(O) |> except(O)` is `S |> except(O)`, and
    `S |> union(O) |> intersect(S)` is `S`.
- **Order still shows.** A filter before the union applies to `SELECTION`
  alone: `S |> stated |> union(O)` adds every new step of `O`, derived ones
  included, where `S |> union(O) |> stated` keeps only stated steps. When two
  stages would fail, the one applied first reports its error.

### Undefined selections

- A `depth` stage whose limit is `null` or undefined makes a selection
  undefined, as NUC 82 specifies.
- When `OTHER` is undefined for that reason, the stage makes the reasoning
  expression undefined: the stages after it are not applied and their
  arguments are not evaluated, and a declaration stores the undefined value
  as `null`, as for NUC 106's stages. The accepted spelling appends the
  undefined value to the steps of `SELECTION` as an element instead: with
  `a = 1`, `b = a + 1`, `c = b * 2`, `d = a + 3`, `e = 7` and `f = e + d`,
  `(c |> why).concat(f |> why |> depth(null) |> except(c |> why))` gives the
  steps of `c`, `b` and `a` and then `undefined`, where
  `c |> why |> union(f |> why |> depth(null))` is undefined.
- When `SELECTION` is undefined before the stage, `OTHER` is not evaluated,
  and the selection is undefined, as the accepted spelling's
  `.concat` on an undefined receiver is.

### Bindings and sources

- Before its source is selected, the stage checks for a local or parameter,
  state variable, function or class named `union` and, when one exists,
  raises `TypeError("union is not an operation")` at the running statement.
  Function and method parameters, lambda and arrow parameters, loop
  variables, quantifier binders and block locals are bindings; methods,
  properties and object keys are not.
- The checks of a pipeline's stages run from the outermost stage inward,
  then the source is selected, then the stages apply from the innermost
  outward, each `union` evaluating its `OTHER`, whose own checks run when it
  is evaluated. Accepted pipelines keep this order.
- A binding declared after a standing selection makes that selection's next
  evaluation raise, so the statement that triggered the refresh fails and
  rolls back, as for the accepted stages.
- A binding named `except` shadows only `except`: the union does not read
  it, where the accepted spelling then raises
  `TypeError("except is not an operation")`.
- An invalid source keeps its accepted diagnostic and is reported before
  `OTHER` is evaluated: `1 |> union(missing)` raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain 1")` and `missing |> union(1)` raises
  `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`.
- The accepted shadowing of `why` and `affects`, which ignore bindings, and
  of `stated`, `derived`, `depth`, `diff`, `path`, `intersect`, `except` and
  `nodes`, which a binding shadows, is unchanged.

### Observation, liveness and transactions

- The stage keeps every value and structural observation its source made,
  including those of the steps its filters drop.
- It adds every observation that evaluating `OTHER` makes, including those
  of the steps `OTHER`'s own stages drop, of the steps that the union does
  not add because `SELECTION` holds their node, and of model membership when
  `OTHER` is `model`, so a standing selection refreshes when either side
  changes through a value, a rule, a new or deleted node or a class-level
  rule. So does a declaration that calls a function evaluating a stage,
  because subscriptions are replaced on every evaluation.
- An expression that contains a stage is a reasoning expression: a
  declaration, property, class-level rule, condition or quantifier holding
  it is a reasoning observer that walks neither select nor traverse and
  that `model` excludes. Functions, methods and lambdas evaluate it in
  place, and class-level rules give each instance its own selection.
- `.value` snapshots a selection as for the accepted stages. When a
  declaration is stored, a `.value` read in the limit of a `depth` stage
  inside `OTHER` is frozen, as it is in a limit elsewhere, while the sources
  in `OTHER` stay unfrozen, as a pipeline's source does.
- Propagation order, `suppose` blocks, caught failures and failed
  statements behave as for the accepted stages: rollback restores
  selections, observations and queued refreshes.

```
price = 10
quantity = 3
subtotal = price * quantity
shipping = quantity * 2
either = why subtotal |> union(why shipping)
failure = ""
try:
    either = why subtotal |> union(1)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert(failure, TypeError("Cannot explain 1"))
assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "price", "quantity", "shipping"])
try:
    fee = 2
    shipping = quantity * 2 + fee
    assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "price", "quantity", "shipping", "fee"])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert(either.map(s -> s.node), ["subtotal", "price", "quantity", "shipping"])
```

### Rendering

- The stage renders as `SOURCE|>union(OTHER)` in graph keys, block keys,
  conditions, `rule` text and the errors that quote an expression, with the
  source and `OTHER` rendered once each, so rendered size stays linear.
  `why tax |> union(why shipping)` renders as
  `tax|>why|>union(shipping|>why)`.
- The rendering parses back to the same expression, except where the
  accepted renderer already abbreviates part of it, as NUC 88 records.
- A union and its accepted spelling render differently, so they are
  different graph keys, conditions and `rule` texts, and the errors that
  quote them quote their own spelling, such as
  `TypeError("Cannot delete tax|>why|>union(shipping|>why)")`.

### Cost

- Pipelines without the stage take their accepted paths and do no work for
  it.
- One application evaluates `SELECTION` and `OTHER` once each, builds a set
  of the nodes of `SELECTION` and appends the steps of `OTHER` that the set
  does not hold, in time and memory linear in the sizes of both selections,
  besides the walks themselves. The accepted spelling evaluates `SELECTION`
  twice, once for `concat` and once inside `except`, and builds a step record
  for every step of both parts.
- Phase-2 measurements, on release builds of the candidate and of the
  accepted revision, on one Windows 11 machine with 16 logical processors.
  They compare spellings on one machine and are not guarantees. Each figure
  is the best of five runs of one statement after its setup, every run in a
  process of its own, because a measurement taken late in a long-running
  process can take several times as long. The questions are:

| A source that reads an undefined name | `ReferenceError("<name> is not defined")`, as before, before `OTHER` is evaluated |
| `OTHER` selections nested more deeply than the parser allows | `SyntaxError("Expressions are nested too deeply")`, as for any nested expression |

- No error type or message is added. `<name> is not an operation` is the
  accepted pipeline diagnostic, and the errors of `OTHER` are those of a
  pipeline source. Only the messages that quote an expression holding a
  union quote its own rendering.
- Source text cannot nest `OTHER` past the parser's limit. A tree built
  through the public API past the runtime's `MAX_DEPTH` raises the existing
  `TypeError("Maximum expression depth exceeded")` instead of overflowing
  the stack.

### Findings

- **Medium, public API (F1).** `Expr` is public and not `#[non_exhaustive]`,
  so adding `Expr::Union` breaks downstream code that matches it
  exhaustively, as the arm that `tests/common/assertions.rs` needs shows,
  which Rust's semantic versioning treats as a major change. The version
  decision is human-owned, and no version number changes.
- **Low, documentation (F2).** [Compatibility](#compatibility) names the
  parse-time `TypeError` and the backtracking `SyntaxError`s as the errors
  that changed programs raised at the accepted revision. A program that also
  holds a form that only a later pass of the accepted parser reads, a
  receiver dot, an operator section of [NUC 109](nuc-0109.md) or a
  Python-style `self` parameter of [NUC 107](nuc-0107.md), reported that
  pass's first error instead: `SyntaxError("Unexpected '.'")`,
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected '>'")` or
  `SyntaxError("Expected a parameter but found keyword 'this'")`. Each was
  still a whole-program parse error at the accepted revision, so no accepted
  program changes.
- **Low, documentation (F3).** [Errors](#errors) says that an `OTHER` that
  is a property path whose base holds no object raises
  `TypeError("Cannot explain <other>")`. That holds for a base such as a
  number. For a `null` or undefined base the stage raises
  `ReferenceError("<base> is not defined")`, as an accepted pipeline source
  and `intersect` do, and as the focused suite pins. The caller reproduced
  both readings with the stage on its own final build, and with `intersect`
  and a pipeline source on the builds of both revisions.
- **Info, cost (F4).** [Cost](#cost) says that the stage is faster than both
  accepted spellings in every row. The evaluator's whole-process timings
  confirm that for the overlapping question, but on the disjoint one the
  stage is within noise of the `distinct` spelling, 182.8 ms against 178.4
  ms at 10,000 nodes, as Policy results records. Those timings include
  process start-up and building the graph, which the figures of Cost leave
  out.
- **Info, process (F5).** A read-only lock check of the evaluator's,
  `cargo tree --locked --offline`, created an ignored
  `target/.rustc_info.json` in the candidate's ignored `target` directory.
  The evaluator removed it, so that directory again held only `.keep`; no
  tracked or manifest path was affected, and every blob held.
