```

Requires: 10, 61
```

## Abstract

- A slice gains an optional step after a second colon:
  `SEQUENCE[start:stop:step]`. Every part may be omitted, so `xs[::2]` takes
  every other item, `xs[1::2]` the others, `s[::-1]` reverses a string and
  `Reading[::3]` takes every third instance of a class.
- The step follows Python's slices: a positive step walks forward from the
  start and keeps the bounds of [NUC 61](nuc-0061.md)'s slices and ranges
  exactly, so `xs[a:b:1]` is `xs[a:b]`; a negative step walks backward, from
  the last position unless a start is written, with Python's defaults and
  clamping. Negative bounds count from the end, and every bound is clamped
  to the sequence.
- The step is converted to a number and truncated toward zero, as bounds
  are. A step that converts to NaN or truncates to `0` raises
  `TypeError("Slice step must be a nonzero number")`, on every receiver,
  once the object, the bounds and the step are evaluated.
- It applies wherever a slice applies today: lists, strings, which it walks
  by Unicode scalar value as `slice` does, and the instance lists of
  declared classes, in population order. Every other value gives undefined,
  as a one-colon slice gives. A declaration or class-level rule that holds a
  stepped slice stays live, and follows a changed step as it follows a
  changed bound.
- At the accepted revision a second colon after a slice's start or stop is
  a whole-program `SyntaxError`, such as `Unexpected ':'` for `xs[::2]` and
  `Expected ']' but found ':'` for `xs[1:5:2]`. Only those inputs change.
  Every one-colon slice, every subscript whose parts hold their own colon,
  such as the conditional in `xs[c ? 1 : 2 : 4]`, and the `slice` method
  keep their parse and their result.
- One public syntax-tree variant, `Expr::SteppedSlice`, is added beside
  `Expr::Slice`, which a one-colon slice still builds. No keyword, token or
  error type is added; the step's message is new.
- The authoritative contract is
  [Stepped slices](../nucleoid.spec.md#stepped-slices).

## Motivation

- World models walk their sequences by stride. A grid model reads every
  other row or column, mirrors rows to flip a shape, and splits interleaved
  readings; a text model reverses words; a rota deals tasks round-robin.
  Slices already cut a sequence by position, but they cannot skip or walk
  backward.
- Without a step, accepted programs spell a stride with a filter that looks
  every item up again, `samples.filter(s -> samples.indexOf(s) % 2 == 0)`,
  which holds only while the items are distinct: for `xs = [4, 4, 5]` it
  keeps all three items, where every other item is `[4, 5]`, and for the
  word `"noon"` the same filter over its letters gives `"nn"` where every
  other letter is `"no"`. A reversed string is spelled
  `word.split("").reverse().join()`. A helper function with a loop is
  general, but at the accepted revision two standing declarations or a
  class-level rule that call such a helper fail with
  `TypeError("Propagation did not settle")`.
- The accepted spellings are long. `left = samples[::2]` is 8 lexer tokens
  where `left = samples.filter(s -> samples.indexOf(s) % 2 == 0)` is 19;
  `mirror = word[::-1]` is 9 where
  `mirror = word.split("").reverse().join()` is 16; and the round-robin rule
  `$Roommate.duties = chores[$Roommate.turn::Roommate.length]` is 15 where
  its filter is 25. `back = route[here::-1]` is 10 where
  `back = route[:here + 1].reverse()` is 13 and must remember the `+ 1`.
- Python, NumPy, Ruby's `step`, Rust's `step_by` and Kotlin's `step` name
  the operation, and Python and NumPy write a stride and a reversal as
  `xs[::2]` and `s[::-1]`. At the accepted revision those spellings are
  syntax errors, so a program written with them does not run at all.

## Rationale

- **Category and mode.** The caller drew Syntax enhancement with one
  tool-backed uniform draw over the five categories that NUC 1's registry
  marks eligible for automatic selection: index 0, with probability 1/5, at
  11:18:04 on 20-Nov-2026, and no redraw. This is a continuous cycle, so the
  token non-regression gate is required. Runtime applicability does not
  apply to this category.
- **Selection.** Within the category the caller chose one bounded
  enhancement: a step in the existing slice syntax. At the accepted revision
  a second colon after a slice's start or stop is a whole-program
  `SyntaxError`, no NUC proposes a step, and NUC 61 specified the bounds of
  slices and ranges without one. A step adds what slices cannot express
  today, strides and backward walks, so it extends the syntax rather than
  aliasing an accepted form.
- **Optional evidence.** No hint was supplied. Advisory usefulness is
  unscored, not zero, because no rubric was frozen. Strategy learning is
  `not_configured`, so this revision is unattributed, with no policy,
  selection receipt, trial or reward.
- **Python's slice, in Nucleoid's slice syntax.** `[start:stop:step]` with
  every part optional is Python's extended slice, also NumPy's, and the
  spelling that most programmers and models reach for. It needs no new token
  or keyword: the second colon is the accepted `Colon` token, and `::` lexes
  as two of them. Rejected:
  - MATLAB's and Julia's `start:step:stop`, with the step in the middle,
    which would give `xs[1:5:2]` a different meaning from Python's;
  - Go's three-index `s[low:high:max]`, whose third part caps the capacity
    of a slice, which Nucleoid lists do not have;
  - a method such as `xs.step(2)` or `xs.stride(2)`, which would add a name
    beside the slice and could not take bounds without becoming `slice`
    with three arguments, and `every`, which is already a quantifier and a
    list method;
  - a step argument for the `slice` method, which would change the accepted
    result of every `slice(a, b, c)` call: `xs.slice(0, 4, 2)` ignores its
    third argument and keeps doing so.
- **Where the second colon goes.** The second colon follows the first colon
  and the optional stop, and the optional step follows it, so the eight
  shapes `[::]`, `[::step]`, `[start::]`, `[start::step]`, `[:stop:]`,
  `[:stop:step]`, `[start:stop:]` and `[start:stop:step]` all parse. The
  start and the stop are parsed exactly as today, and the step is parsed as
  the stop is. A conditional, a quantifier and a `lambda` consume their own
  colon wherever they stand, so every accepted input keeps its parse:
  `xs[c ? 1 : 2 : 4]` is still the one-colon slice from `c ? 1 : 2` to `4`,
  `xs[1:c ? 3 : 4]` still ends at `c ? 3 : 4`, `items[all v in xs: v:]` still
  starts at the quantifier and `values[1:lambda: 2]` still ends at the lambda.
  The parser takes the new path only where the accepted parser raised: at a
  second colon after a slice's start or stop. A third colon stays a
  `SyntaxError`. Rejected: reading `xs[c ? 1 : 2 : 4]` as a stepped slice,
  which would change an accepted result.
- **A syntax-tree variant of its own.** A slice written with a second colon
  builds the new public variant
  `Expr::SteppedSlice { object, start, end, step }`, and a one-colon slice
  still builds `Expr::Slice { object, start, end }` exactly. Its `step` is
  optional, so `xs[1:5:]` and `xs[::]` keep their second colon when they are
  rendered. Rejected: a `step` field on `Expr::Slice`, which would change the
  tree of every accepted slice and break every exhaustive destructuring of
  the public variant, among them the accepted
  `Expr::Slice { object, start, end }` arm of `tests/common/assertions.rs`.
  As for [NUC 119](nuc-0119.md)'s `Stmt::TryFinally` and
  [NUC 120](nuc-0120.md)'s `Expr::Union`, that exhaustive match gains only
  the arm that visits the new variant's children, and no other existing test
  changes beyond count-only pins.
- **The step converts as a bound does.** The step is converted with the
  numeric conversion that bounds use, `null`, `false`, blank strings and
  empty lists being `0`, `true` `1`, numeric strings and one-item lists their
  number, dates their milliseconds and every other value NaN, and is
  truncated toward zero, as a bound's position is. So `xs[::2.7]` steps by
  `2`, `xs[::"2"]` and `xs[::[2]]` by `2`, and `xs[::true]` by `1`. An
  omitted step is `1`. [Steps](#steps) lists every kind of value measured. Rejected: Python's
  requirement of an integer step, which would make the step the only part of
  a Nucleoid subscript that rejects a fractional or numeric-string value.
- **A zero or NaN step raises.** A step of `0` has no direction and would
  never reach the stop, and a NaN step has no number at all, so both raise
  `TypeError("Slice step must be a nonzero number")`, as Python raises
  `ValueError: slice step cannot be zero`. That covers `0`, `-0`, a fraction
  such as `0.5` or `-0.9`, `null`, `false`, `""`, non-numeric strings, empty
  lists and lists of more than one item, objects, classes, functions,
  regular expressions, undefined and the epoch date `Date(0)`.
  The error type is `TypeError`, the type Nucleoid's queries raise for an
  argument of the wrong kind, such as
  `TypeError("quantile expects a number from 0 to 1")`; Nucleoid has no
  `ValueError`. Rejected:
  - reading `null` as an omitted step, as Python reads `None`, which would
    make the step the only part where `null` is not `0`, and would let a
    standing `xs[::k]` silently fall back to every item when `k` becomes
    `null`;
  - giving `[]` or undefined for a zero step, which would hide a wrong step
    as an empty result.
- **A positive step keeps the bounds of a one-colon slice.** With a positive
  step the start and the stop go through exactly the bound rules of a
  one-colon slice: an omitted start is `0`, an omitted stop is the length, a
  negative bound counts from the end, a NaN bound reads as `0`, and every
  bound is truncated and clamped to the sequence. So `xs[a:b:1]` and
  `xs[a:b:]` equal `xs[a:b]` for every `a` and `b`, on lists, strings and
  classes, and a stride only thins out what the one-colon slice selects.
- **A negative step uses Python's defaults and clamping.** With a negative
  step an omitted start is the last position and an omitted stop lies before
  the first, so `xs[::-1]` reverses the whole sequence. A negative bound
  counts from the end, as for a positive step, and a position is truncated
  toward zero and clamped to the range from `-1`, one before the first
  position, to the last position. The positions `start`, `start + step` and
  so on that lie above the stop are taken. For whole numbers these are
  exactly Python's `slice.indices` and `range` rules. A fractional or NaN
  bound goes through the same position computation as for a positive step,
  with the other clamp: `xs[-2.5::-1]` on six items starts at
  `trunc(6 - 2.5)`, position 3, and `xs[0 / 0::-1]` starts at position 0.
  Rejected: JavaScript's truncation of a bound before it is counted from the
  end, which a one-colon slice does not use either.
- **Large steps.** A step whose magnitude is at least the length takes only
  the start position, when it lies in the sequence, and `Infinity` and
  `-Infinity` are such steps. The walk visits at most `n` positions for a
  sequence of `n` items, without recursion, so a huge step costs what a step
  of `n` costs.
- **The receivers of a slice.** Lists give lists; strings give strings,
  walked by Unicode scalar value, as `slice`, `length` and indexing walk
  them, so `"añejo"[::-1]` is `"ojeña"` and a character outside the Basic
  Multilingual Plane stays whole; and a declared class's instance list gives
  its instances in population order, read before the bounds and the step, as
  ranges read it. Every other value gives what a one-colon slice gives it,
  undefined, once the step is valid. A combining mark is its own scalar
  value, so reversing an `e` followed by the combining acute accent U+0301
  puts the mark first, as `split("").reverse().join()` does. Rejected:
  grapheme clusters, which no other string operation uses, and raising for
  other receivers, which a one-colon slice does not do.
- **The step is checked after every part is evaluated.** The object is
  evaluated first, then the start, the stop and the step, left to right, as
  they are written; then the step is checked, for every receiver; then the
  positions are taken. So a part that reads an undefined name reports that
  name before the step is checked, and `null[::0]` raises the step error
  rather than giving undefined: a wrong step is wrong whatever the receiver
  holds at the moment. Rejected: checking the step only for sequences, which
  would let a standing slice over a value that is still `null` hide a zero
  step until the value arrives.
- **Live as slices are.** A declaration or class-level rule that holds a
  stepped slice depends on what a one-colon slice depends on, and on what its
  step reads, so list mutations, new and deleted instances, a changed bound
  and a changed step re-evaluate it, and `.value` freezes a bound or the step.
  A class-level rule checks the references in its bounds and its step when
  it is declared, as [NUC 10](nuc-0010.md) has it check the bounds. A change
  that makes the step invalid raises the step error during re-evaluation and
  rolls back, as an invalid rank of a standing `quantile` does.
- **Rendered as written.** Statements, `why`, `affects`, graph and block
  keys and error messages that quote an expression show the slice as it is
  written, `xs[1::2]`, `s[::-1]`, `xs[a:b:c]` and `xs[1:5:]`, so a stepped
  slice and its accepted spelling are different statements and keys.
- **Not an assignment target.** `xs[::2] = v` raises the accepted
  `SyntaxError("Cannot assign to xs[::2]")`, as `xs[1:3] = v` raises
  `SyntaxError("Cannot assign to xs[1:3]")`. Rejected: Python's extended
  slice assignment, which would need list writes through positions that
  Nucleoid does not offer for one-colon slices either.
- **Requires.** [NUC 10](nuc-0010.md), whose slicing defines the
  `[start:end]` syntax, the dependencies of a slice and its bounds, `.value`
  on a bound and the class-level rule check of both bounds that a stepped
  slice extends to its step; and [NUC 61](nuc-0061.md), whose bound rules
  for slices and class instance ranges a positive step keeps exactly and a
  negative step follows with Python's clamp. Those already require everything
  else these rules rest on.
- **Not added.** A step for the `slice` method, steps in ranges of other
  forms, `range(...)`, slice objects, assignment to or deletion of stepped
  slices, Python's `None` as an omitted part, grapheme-cluster strings, a
  `reverse()` method on strings, and any change to what one-colon slices,
  ranges, indexing or `slice` return.

## Specification

- The normative contract is
  [Stepped slices](../nucleoid.spec.md#stepped-slices).

### Syntax

- Inside a subscript, a second colon may follow the slice's first colon and
  its optional stop, and an optional step follows it:

```
subscript    : '[' ( expression | slice | steppedSlice ) ']'
slice        : expression? ':' expression?
steppedSlice : expression? ':' expression? ':' expression?
```

- The start is parsed as the start of a one-colon slice, and the stop and the
  step as its stop. A conditional, a quantifier or a `lambda` inside any part
  keeps its own colon. A third colon, as in `xs[1:2:3:4]`, stays a
  `SyntaxError`.
- Spaces around the colons do not matter, and a subscript may stay open
  across lines, as every bracket may: `xs[ : : 2 ]` and `xs[::` followed by
  `2]` on the next line are `xs[::2]`.

### Steps

- The step is converted with `Value::to_number`, the conversion of the
  bounds, and truncated toward zero. An omitted step is `1`.
- A step that converts to NaN, or truncates to `0` or `-0`, raises
  `TypeError("Slice step must be a nonzero number")`.
- Phase 2 measured every kind of value as the step of `xs[::step]` on the
  candidate's release build, after `xs = [10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60]`, and the
  final tree's release build gives the same result for all 65. These are
  steps:
  - numbers of magnitude `1` or more, fractions such as `2.7` and `-2.7`
    included, which step by `2` and `-2`, and `1e300`, `-1e300`, `Infinity`
    and `-Infinity`, which take only the first or the last item;
  - `true`, which steps by `1`;
  - strings whose trimmed text parses as such a number: `"2"`, `" 2 "`,
    `"-2"`, `"+2"`, `"2.7"`, `"1e1"`, `"inf"` and `"-Infinity"`;
  - one-item lists whose item is a step: `[2]`, `[-2]`, `["2"]`, `[[2]]` and
    `[true]`;
  - dates other than the epoch, which convert to their milliseconds:
    `Date(2)` steps by `2`, `Date(-2)` by `-2`, and `Date("2020-1-1")` takes
    only the first item.
- These raise the step error:
  - numbers of magnitude below `1`, among them `0`, `-0`, `0.5`, `-0.5` and
    `-0.999`, and NaN, such as `0 / 0`;
  - `false`, `null` and undefined;
  - strings whose trimmed text does not parse as a number of magnitude `1`
    or more: the blank `""` and `" "`, `"0"` and `"0.5"`, `"NaN"`, which
    parses as NaN, and `"a"`, `"2a"`, `"0x10"` and `"1_0"`, which parse as
    no number;
  - empty lists, one-item lists whose item raises, `[0]`, `[null]`,
    `[false]`, `["a"]` and `[[]]`, and lists of two or more items, such as
    `[1, 2]`;
  - objects, `{}` and `{a: 1}`, instances, classes, functions, those that
    `def` declares and lambdas alike, and regular expressions, `/a/` and
    `/2/`;
  - the epoch date `Date(0)`.

### Selection

- For a sequence of `n` items and a positive step, the start and the stop are
  those of the one-colon slice with the same bounds, `builtins::slice_index`
  of each written bound, `0` and `n` for omitted ones. The positions `start`,
  `start + step` and so on that lie below the stop are taken, in order.
- For a negative step, a written bound `b` gives the position `n + b` when
  `b` is negative and `b` otherwise; that position is truncated toward zero,
  NaN reads as `0`, and it is clamped to the range from `-1` to `n - 1`. An
  omitted start is `n - 1` and an omitted stop `-1`. The positions `start`,
  `start + step` and so on that lie above the stop are taken, in order. On
  six items, `xs[-2.5::-1]` starts at `trunc(6 - 2.5)`, position 3,
  `xs[:-2.5:-1]` stops above position 3, and `xs[0 / 0::-1]` starts at
  position 0.
- A step of magnitude `n` or more, `Infinity` and `-Infinity` included,
  takes only the start position, when it lies in the sequence. The walk takes
  at most `n` steps.

### Receivers

- A list gives a list of the items at the taken positions.
- A string gives a string of the Unicode scalar values at the taken
  positions: `"stressed"[::-1]` is `"desserts"`, `"héllo wörld"[::-1]` is
  `"dlröw olléh"`, `"abcdef"[1::2]` is `"bdf"` and `""[::-1]` is `""`.
- A declared class gives a list of its instances at the taken positions, in
  population order, whether it is named directly, through a variable that
  holds it or by `$Name`; a class without instances gives `[]`.
- Every other value, among them `null`, undefined, numbers, booleans,
  objects, functions and built-in namespaces such as `Math`, gives
  undefined, once the step is valid.

### Evaluation order

- The object is evaluated first; a class's population is read at once, before
  the bounds and the step. Then the start, the stop and the step are
  evaluated, left to right, skipping omitted parts. Then the step is checked.
  Then the positions are taken.
- So `xs[missing::0]` raises `ReferenceError("missing is not defined")`, and
  `null[::0]` raises the step error.

### Liveness and class-level rules

- A declaration that holds a stepped slice depends on the object, on what its
  bounds read and on what its step reads, unless `.value` freezes a part:
  after `k = 2` and `ys = xs[::k.value]`, `k = 3` leaves `ys` as it was, and
  a later `xs.push(70)` re-evaluates it with the frozen step `2`, as a frozen
  bound of a one-colon slice stays frozen.
- Pushing, popping, shifting or replacing a list, creating or deleting an
  instance, and changing a bound or the step re-evaluate it.
- A class-level rule checks the references in its bounds and its step when it
  is declared, so `$Row.dark = $Row.cells[::step]` raises
  `ReferenceError("step is not defined")` while `step` is undefined, and each
  instance evaluates its own slice.

### Transactions

- The step error and errors raised while evaluating a part propagate through
  the existing transaction: a change that makes a standing step invalid raises
  the step error and rolls back, and a rolled-back transaction restores the
  results of its declarations. `suppose` sees the hypothetical slice and
  restores the accepted one when it ends.

```
def checked(amount):
    if amount < 0:
        throw "NEGATIVE"
    return amount
prices = [4, 9, 7, 12]
picked = prices[::2].map(checked)
failure = ""
try:
    prices.push(-3)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert([failure, prices, picked], ["NEGATIVE", [4, 9, 7, 12], [4, 7]])
try:
    prices.push(5)
    assert(picked, [4, 7, 5])
    throw "UNDO"
catch error:
    assert(error, "UNDO")
assert([prices, picked], [[4, 9, 7, 12], [4, 7]])
class Crate(weight): this.weight = weight
Crate(6)
Crate(2)
Crate(9)
latest = Crate[::-2].map(crate -> checked(crate.weight))
failure = ""
try:
    Crate(-1)
catch error:
    failure = error
assert([failure, Crate.length, latest], ["NEGATIVE", 3, [9, 6]])
Crate(5)
latest
```

- This returns `[5, 2]`: the failed push, the undone push and the failed new
  crate leave the prices, the crates and both results as they were.

### Rendering and graph keys

- The slice renders as written, with one or two colons: `xs[1::2]`,
  `s[::-1]`, `xs[a:b:c]`, `xs[1:5:]` and `xs[::]`. `why`, `affects`, graph
  and block keys, and error messages that quote an expression show that
  rendering.

### Assignment targets

- A stepped slice is not an assignment target: `xs[::2] = [1]` raises
  `SyntaxError("Cannot assign to xs[::2]")`, as `xs[1:3] = [1]` raises
  `SyntaxError("Cannot assign to xs[1:3]")`.

### Findings

- **Medium, public API (F1).** The public `Expr` enum is not
  `#[non_exhaustive]` and gains `Expr::SteppedSlice`, so downstream code
  that matches `Expr` exhaustively stops compiling. That is a
  semantic-versioning major change, which needs the review of the version's
  human owners. No version number changed.
- **Low, documentation (F2).** [Compatibility](#compatibility) and
  [Errors](#errors) say that every changed program raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ':'")` or
  `SyntaxError("Expected ']' but found ':'")` at the accepted revision. Some
  raised other whole-program errors there: an object destructuring that
  holds a stepped slice, such as `{a, b} = {a: xs[::2], b: 1}`, raised
  `SyntaxError("Unexpected ','")`, and a stepped slice in a method with a
  Python-style `self` parameter raised
  `SyntaxError("Expected a parameter but found keyword 'this'")`; the caller
  reproduced both. They were whole-program errors at the accepted revision,
  so compatibility holds.
- **Low, rendering code (F3).** `Shown::slice` keeps a silent
  `_ => return Ok(())` fallback over the closed `Expr` enum. Only the
  `Display` arms of `Expr::Slice` and `Expr::SteppedSlice` call it, so it
  cannot be reached today; passing the destructured parts as parameters
  would make it unrepresentable.
- **Info, counterpart compactness (F4).** Rewriting a whole expression as a
  comprehension that also replaces a case's own `.map(...)` would shorten
  three counterparts, to 105 tokens against the recorded 111, 302 against
  309 and 131 against 132. The site rule excludes such rewrites, and every
  case would still use fewer tokens than them.

Correcting the wording of F2 or the fallback of F3 needs a fresh proposal
and evaluation.
